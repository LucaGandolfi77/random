# Chapter 4 — Stellar Evolution: From Main Sequence to Supernova

**Level:** Advanced  
**Key Concepts:** Hertzsprung-Russell diagram, nuclear burning stages, supernova mechanism, neutron star formation.

---

## 4.1 The Main Sequence: Hydrogen Burning

On the main sequence, the luminosity \( L \) and effective temperature \( T_{\mathrm{eff}} \) are related by the mass-luminosity relation (approximately \( L \propto M^{3.5} \) for \( M \gtrsim M_\odot \)) and the Stefan-Boltzmann law \( L = 4\pi R^2 \sigma T_{\mathrm{eff}}^4 \). Combining these gives the main-sequence radius-luminosity relation.

The nuclear energy generation rate per unit mass for the pp-chain (dominant for \( M \lesssim 1.3 M_\odot \)) is approximately:

\[
\epsilon_{\mathrm{pp}} \approx \epsilon_0 \rho X^2 T^4
\]

where \( X \approx 0.7 \) is the hydrogen mass fraction. For the CNO cycle (dominant for higher masses):

\[
\epsilon_{\mathrm{CNO}} \approx \epsilon_0 \rho X Z T^{20}
\]

The extreme temperature sensitivity of the CNO cycle (\( T^{20} \)) explains why massive stars have convective cores.

## 4.2 Post-Main-Sequence Evolution and the Chandrasekhar Limit

When hydrogen is exhausted in the core, the core contracts and heats until helium burning begins (the triple-alpha process). For stars with initial mass \( M \lesssim 8 M_\odot \), carbon-oxygen cores form and eventually collapse until supported by electron degeneracy pressure. The maximum mass supported by electron degeneracy is the Chandrasekhar limit:

\[
M_{\mathrm{Ch}} = \frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{2} \left( \frac{\hbar c}{G} \right)^{3/2} \frac{1}{(\mu_e m_u)^2} \approx 1.4 M_\odot
\]

The collapse releases \( \sim 10^{46} \, \mathrm{J} \) of gravitational binding energy, with only \( \sim 1\% \) appearing as electromagnetic radiation; the remainder is carried away by neutrinos.

## 4.3 The Main-Sequence Lifetime: Derivation

The total nuclear energy available from hydrogen burning is approximately:

\[
E_{\mathrm{nuc}} = f M X \epsilon_{\mathrm{H}}
\]

where \( \epsilon_{\mathrm{H}} \approx 6.3 \times 10^{14} \, \mathrm{J/kg} \) is the energy released per unit mass of hydrogen fused to helium, and \( f \approx 0.1 \) is the fraction of the stellar mass available for fusion (only the core, where temperature is sufficient, participates). Thus:

\[
E_{\mathrm{nuc}} \approx 0.1 M X \epsilon_{\mathrm{H}} \approx 0.07 M \epsilon_{\mathrm{H}}
\]

The luminosity is \( L \propto M^{3.5} \). The main-sequence lifetime is:

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{MS}} = \frac{E_{\mathrm{nuc}}}{L} \propto \frac{M}{M^{3.5}} \propto M^{-2.5}
\]

For the Sun (\( M = M_\odot \), \( L = L_\odot \)), \( \tau_{\mathrm{MS}} \approx 10^{10} \) years, consistent with stellar evolution models. This scaling explains why massive stars live brief lives (\( \sim 10^6 \) years for \( M \approx 20 M_\odot \)) and low-mass stars are essentially eternal on cosmological timescales.

## 4.4 The Supernova Mechanism: Energy and Neutrinos

In a core-collapse supernova, the gravitational binding energy released is approximately:

\[
E_{\mathrm{bind}} \approx \frac{3}{5} \frac{G M_{\mathrm{core}}^2}{R_{\mathrm{NS}}}
\]

with \( M_{\mathrm{core}} \approx 1.4 M_\odot \) and \( R_{\mathrm{NS}} \approx 10 \) km, yielding \( E_{\mathrm{bind}} \approx 3 \times 10^{46} \, \mathrm{J} \approx 10^{53} \) erg. Approximately 99% of this energy is emitted in neutrinos over a timescale of \( \sim 10 \) seconds. The remaining 1% powers the kinetic energy of the ejecta (\( \sim 10^{44} \, \mathrm{J} \)) and the electromagnetic display.

The claim of the supernova is not the light we see. It is the neutrino flux — a claim carried by particles that barely interact with matter, yet carry the entire energy budget of the collapse. The observer measures the light; the universe measures the neutrino.
