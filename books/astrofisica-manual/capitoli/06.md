# Chapter 6 — General Relativity: Curvature, Tensors, and the Einstein Field Equations

**Level:** Advanced  
**Objective:** To derive the geometric structure of spacetime and the Einstein field equations.

---

The metric tensor \( g_{\mu\nu}(x) \) defines the line element \( ds^2 = g_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu \). The Christoffel symbols are:

\[
\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} g^{\lambda\sigma} \left( \partial_\mu g_{\nu\sigma} + \partial_\nu g_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma g_{\mu\nu} \right)
\]

The Riemann curvature tensor:

\[
R^\rho_{\ \sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^\rho_{\mu\lambda} \Gamma^\lambda_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^\rho_{\nu\lambda} \Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\sigma}
\]

The Ricci tensor \( R_{\mu\nu} = R^\lambda_{\ \lambda\mu\nu} \) and the scalar curvature \( R = g^{\mu\nu} R_{\mu\nu} \). The Einstein field equations:

\[
G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}
\]

where \( T_{\mu\nu} \) is the stress-energy tensor and \( \Lambda \) is the cosmological constant.

---

## 6.1 Derivation: The Hilbert-Einstein Action

The field equations can be derived from the variational principle using the Einstein-Hilbert action:

\[
S = \frac{c^4}{16\pi G} \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \, R + S_{\mathrm{matter}}
\]

where \( g = \det(g_{\mu\nu}) \) and \( R = g^{\mu\nu} R_{\mu\nu} \). Varying the metric \( g^{\mu\nu} \) yields:

\[
\delta S = - \frac{c^4}{16\pi G} \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \left( R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} R \right) \delta g^{\mu\nu} + \delta S_{\mathrm{matter}} = 0
\]

Defining \( T_{\mu\nu} = - \frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{matter}}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}} \), one obtains the field equations directly. This derivation demonstrates that general relativity is the unique metric theory of gravity with second-order field equations in four dimensions, as shown by Lovelock (1971).

## 6.3 Literary Reflection: Proust and the Curvature of Memory — Spacetime as the Folded Page

Proust writes that lost time is not lost; it is folded into the things we keep without knowing. General relativity says the same thing, but with tensors. The metric \( g_{\mu\nu} \) is the folded page of spacetime. The curvature \( R_{\mu\nu} \) is the memory of the page — the way it remembers the presence of matter, the way it holds the past (the initial conditions) and the future (the geodesics) in the same structure.

When Einstein derives the field equations, he does not invent gravity. He unfolds it. The claim that spacetime is curved is not a claim that the universe is strange. It is the claim that the universe remembers everything — every mass, every energy, every movement — and that this memory is not a file stored elsewhere, but the very shape of the space in which we live.

The observer moves through spacetime. The observer is not outside the page. The observer is part of the fold. The measurement of the metric is the measurement of the memory. And the equation \( G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu} / c^4 \) is not only physics. It is the statement that the past does not disappear. It becomes curvature. It becomes the shape of the future. It is the first thing that was never false — the claim that the universe, in remembering itself, never forgets.

## 6.2 The Bianchi Identities and Energy Conservation

The Bianchi identities \( \nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu} = 0 \) imply \( \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0 \). This is not an additional assumption but a geometric consequence of the curvature properties of spacetime. In astrophysical applications, this conservation law determines the evolution of stellar structure, the dynamics of accretion disks, and the propagation of gravitational waves.
