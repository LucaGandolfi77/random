# Chapter 7 — Black Holes: The Schwarzschild Solution and Event Horizons

**Level:** Very Advanced  
**Key Equations:** Schwarzschild metric, Birkhoff's theorem, Hawking temperature.

---

The Schwarzschild metric (vacuum, static, spherically symmetric):

\[
ds^2 = - \left( 1 - \frac{r_s}{r} \right) c^2 dt^2 + \left( 1 - \frac{r_s}{r} \right)^{-1} dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2
\]

with \( r_s = 2 G M / c^2 \). The event horizon is at \( r = r_s \). Birkhoff's theorem states that any spherically symmetric vacuum solution is the Schwarzschild metric. The Hawking temperature for a black hole of mass \( M \) is:

\[
T = \frac{\hbar c^3}{8\pi G M k_B}
\]

This connects general relativity, quantum mechanics, and thermodynamics — the deepest intersection in modern astrophysics.

## 7.1 The Schwarzschild Metric: Derivation from Spherical Symmetry

The metric for a static, spherically symmetric vacuum spacetime can be written in the form:

\[
ds^2 = -e^{\nu(r)} c^2 dt^2 + e^{\lambda(r)} dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2
\]

Substituting into the vacuum Einstein equations \( G_{\mu\nu} = 0 \) yields:

\[
e^{-\lambda} \left( \frac{\lambda'}{r} - \frac{1}{r^2} \right) + \frac{1}{r^2} = 0
\]

\[
e^{-\lambda} \left( \frac{\nu'}{r} + \frac{1}{r^2} \right) - \frac{1}{r^2} = 0
\]

\[
e^{-\lambda} \left( \frac{\nu''}{2} + \frac{\nu'^2}{4} - \frac{\lambda' \nu'}{4} - \frac{\lambda'}{r} \right) = 0
\]

Solving the first equation for \( e^{-\lambda} \) yields \( e^{\lambda} = (1 - r_s/r)^{-1} \). The second equation then gives \( \nu = -\lambda \). Thus:

\[
e^{\nu} = 1 - \frac{r_s}{r}, \quad r_s = \frac{2 G M}{c^2}
\]

This is the Schwarzschild metric. It is the unique spherically symmetric vacuum solution, as guaranteed by Birkhoff's theorem.

## 7.2 Geodesics and the Event Horizon

The geodesic equation for a test particle is:

\[
\frac{d^2 x^\mu}{d\tau^2} + \Gamma^\mu_{\alpha\beta} \frac{dx^\alpha}{d\tau} \frac{dx^\beta}{d\tau} = 0
\]

For radial infall (\( L = 0 \)), the coordinate time to reach the horizon diverges, while the proper time is finite:

\[
\tau_{\mathrm{fall}} = \frac{\pi}{2} \frac{r_s}{c} \sqrt{\frac{r_0}{r_s}}
\]

This demonstrates that the event horizon is a coordinate singularity, not a physical singularity. The physical singularity is at \( r = 0 \), where the curvature scalars diverge.

## 7.3 Literary Reflection: Tolstoy and the Black Hole — The Garden That Keeps Everything

Tolstoy writes that the garden grows whether we count it or not. The black hole is the garden that has grown to its ultimate form — not by expanding, but by contracting, by holding everything so closely that nothing can escape. The event horizon is not a wall. It is the boundary of the claim — the point at which the claim of the star has become so dense, so complete, that the only thing that remains is the memory of its mass.

The Hawking temperature is the claim that even the black hole is not eternal. It radiates. It forgets, slowly, through quantum tunneling. The claim is settled not by the disappearance of the black hole, but by its persistence — the persistence of the curvature, the persistence of the memory, the persistence of the claim that the universe, even in its darkest form, continues to hold itself together by attention.

And so the black hole is not the end of astrophysics. It is the deepest claim — the claim that even when light cannot escape, the universe continues to measure, to hold, to remember. The observer, looking from the outside, does not see the black hole. The observer sees the absence of light, the curvature of the stars around it, the gravitational waves that announce its presence. The claim is not false. It is only hidden — folded into the darkness, like Proust's lost time, like Tolstoy's garden, like Dostoevsky's underground — and it waits, as all claims wait, for the interpretation that will unfold it.
