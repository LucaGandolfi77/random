# Chapter 8 — The Expanding Universe: Hubble's Law and the Scale Factor

**Level:** Intermediate-Advanced  
**Key Concepts:** Cosmological principle, Hubble's law, scale factor, redshift.

---

The cosmological principle asserts homogeneity and isotropy on large scales. The metric is the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metric:

\[
ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + a^2(t) \left[ \frac{dr^2}{1 - k r^2} + r^2 d\Omega^2 \right]
\]

Hubble's law: \( v = H(t) d \), with \( H(t) = \dot{a}/a \). The redshift \( z \) is defined by:

\[
1 + z = \frac{a(t_0)}{a(t_e)}
\]

The distance-redshift relation for small \( z \) is \( cz \approx H_0 d \), defining the Hubble constant \( H_0 \approx 70 \mathrm{km \cdot s^{-1} \cdot Mpc^{-1}} \) (with current tension between early- and late-universe measurements).
