# Chapter 9 — Cosmological Models: The Friedmann Equations and Their Solutions

**Level:** Advanced  
**Objective:** To derive the Friedmann equations from the Einstein field equations and solve them for matter, radiation, and dark energy domination.

---

The Friedmann equations (derived by substituting the FLRW metric into \( G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}/c^4 \)):

\[
\left( \frac{\dot{a}}{a} \right)^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \rho - \frac{k c^2}{a^2} + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}
\]

\[
\frac{\ddot{a}}{a} = - \frac{4\pi G}{3} \left( \rho + \frac{3P}{c^2} \right) + \frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}
\]

For a flat universe (\( k = 0 \)) with matter (\( P = 0 \)) and cosmological constant \( \Lambda \):

\[
\left( \frac{\dot{a}}{a} \right)^2 = H_0^2 \left[ \Omega_m \left( \frac{a_0}{a} \right)^3 + \Omega_\Lambda \right]
\]

with \( \Omega_m + \Omega_\Lambda = 1 \). The age of the universe is approximately:

\[
t_0 \approx \frac{1}{H_0} \int_0^1 \frac{da}{a \sqrt{\Omega_m a^{-3} + \Omega_\Lambda}}
\]

This integral yields \( t_0 \approx 13.8 \) Gyr for \( \Omega_m \approx 0.3 \), \( \Omega_\Lambda \approx 0.7 \), and \( H_0 \approx 70 \mathrm{km \cdot s^{-1} \cdot Mpc^{-1}} \).

---

## 9.1 Derivation: The Friedmann Equations from the FLRW Metric

The FLRW metric in comoving coordinates is:

\[
ds^2 = -c^2 dt^2 + a^2(t) \left[ \frac{dr^2}{1 - k r^2 / R_0^2} + r^2 (d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta \, d\phi^2) \right]
\]

The non-zero Christoffel symbols are:

\[
\Gamma^0_{11} = \frac{a \dot{a}}{c^2 (1 - k r^2/R_0^2)}, \quad \Gamma^0_{22} = \frac{a \dot{a} r^2}{c^2}, \quad \Gamma^0_{33} = \frac{a \dot{a} r^2 \sin^2\theta}{c^2}
\]

\[
\Gamma^1_{01} = \frac{\dot{a}}{a c}, \quad \Gamma^1_{11} = \frac{k r}{R_0^2 (1 - k r^2/R_0^2)}, \quad \Gamma^1_{22} = -r (1 - k r^2/R_0^2), \quad \Gamma^1_{33} = -r (1 - k r^2/R_0^2) \sin^2\theta
\]

The Einstein tensor components are:

\[
G_{00} = 3 \left( \frac{\dot{a}^2}{a^2} + \frac{k c^2}{a^2 R_0^2} \right)
\]

\[
G_{11} = - \left( 2 \frac{\ddot{a}}{a} + \frac{\dot{a}^2}{a^2} + \frac{k c^2}{a^2 R_0^2} \right) \frac{a^2}{c^2 (1 - k r^2/R_0^2)}
\]

Equating \( G_{00} = (8\pi G/c^2) T_{00} = (8\pi G/c^2) \rho c^2 = 8\pi G \rho \) and \( G_{11} = (8\pi G/c^4) P \) yields the Friedmann equations exactly.

## 9.2 The Cosmological Constant Problem

The observed value \( \Lambda \approx 1.1 \times 10^{-52} \, \mathrm{m}^{-2} \) corresponds to an energy density:

\[
\rho_\Lambda = \frac{\Lambda c^2}{8\pi G} \approx 6 \times 10^{-27} \, \mathrm{kg \cdot m}^{-3} \approx 0.7 \times 10^{-123} \, M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^4
\]

where \( M_{\mathrm{Pl}} = \sqrt{\hbar c/G} \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-8} \, \mathrm{kg} \) is the Planck mass. The discrepancy of 123 orders of magnitude between the observed value and the naive quantum field theory prediction (based on zero-point energies up to the Planck scale) is the cosmological constant problem — arguably the deepest open question in theoretical physics. No solution is currently accepted, though proposals include anthropic selection, dynamical dark energy (quintessence), and modifications to general relativity.

## 9.3 Literary Reflection: Proust, Tolstoy, and the Cosmological Constant — The Memory of Expansion

Proust asks whether time is lost or only folded. The cosmological constant asks the same question, but in the language of expansion. The universe expands. The claim of the universe is not only the claim of the stars; it is the claim of the space between them. And the cosmological constant is the claim that this space remembers — not as a file, but as a tendency, a gentle pressure, a memory that pushes outward.

Tolstoy writes that the garden grows whether we count it. The cosmological constant is the garden's refusal to be counted. It does not ask for measurement. It asks only for presence — the presence of the claim that the universe, even in its emptiness, is not empty. The claim is not false. It is only small. It is only hidden, folded into the equations, like Proust's madeleine, like Dostoevsky's question, like Tolstoy's hedge — and it unfolds, not when we observe it, but when we ask the right question.

The question is: why does the universe expand? The answer is not in the equation. It is in the claim that the equation holds. The claim that spacetime is not a stage, but a participant — the claim that the observer and the universe are part of the same story, the claim that the memory of the universe is not only in the stars, but in the space that keeps them apart, and in the time that keeps them moving.
