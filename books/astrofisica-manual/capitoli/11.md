# Chapter 11 — Mathematical Appendices: Tensor Calculus, Differential Geometry, and Differential Equations

**Level:** Advanced  
**Objective:** To provide the mathematical tools required for the derivation of the Einstein field equations and their solutions.

---

**Tensor calculus:** The covariant derivative of a vector field \( V^\mu \) is:

\[
\nabla_\mu V^\nu = \partial_\mu V^\nu + \Gamma^\nu_{\mu\lambda} V^\lambda
\]

The Bianchi identity \( \nabla_\mu G^{\mu\nu} = 0 \) ensures energy-momentum conservation \( \nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0 \).

**Differential geometry:** The metric compatibility condition \( \nabla_\lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 0 \) determines the Christoffel symbols uniquely.

**Exact and inexact differentials:** In thermodynamics of stellar interiors, the exact differential of entropy \( dS = (1/T) dU + (P/T) dV \) relates to the first and second laws. The inexact differential of heat \( \delta Q \) requires an integrating factor (the inverse temperature) to become exact.

These tools are not optional. They are the language in which the universe writes its laws.

---

## 11.1 The Variational Principle: From Mechanics to Fields

The Euler-Lagrange equation for a Lagrangian \( \mathcal{L}(q, \dot{q}, t) \) is:

\[
\frac{d}{dt} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{q}} - \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial q} = 0
\]

For fields \( \phi(x) \), the action is a functional:

\[
S[\phi] = \int d^4x \, \mathcal{L}(\phi, \partial_\mu \phi)
\]

The functional derivative \( \delta S / \delta \phi = 0 \) yields the field equations. For gravity, \( \phi \) is replaced by the metric \( g_{\mu\nu}(x) \), and the Lagrangian density is \( \mathcal{L} = \sqrt{-g} R / (16\pi G) \). The variational principle ensures that the field equations are second-order and gauge-invariant (under coordinate transformations).

## 11.2 Exact and Inexact Differentials: The Second Law of Thermodynamics

In stellar interiors, the heat flow \( dQ \) is an inexact differential: \( dQ \) depends on the path, not only on the state. The integrating factor \( 1/T \) transforms \( dQ \) into the exact differential \( dS \):

\[
dS = \frac{dQ}{T}
\]

The second law requires \( dS \geq 0 \) for any irreversible process. In a convective region, the entropy gradient is nearly zero (adiabatic), while in a radiative region, the entropy increases outward. This distinction determines the stability of stellar layers and is fundamental to the derivation of the Schwarzschild criterion.
