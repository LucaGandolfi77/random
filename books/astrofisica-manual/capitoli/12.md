# Chapter 12 — Quantum Astrophysics: Degenerate Matter, Neutron Stars, and Tunneling

**Level:** Very Advanced

The Fermi-Dirac distribution for electrons at temperature \( T \) and chemical potential \( \mu \) is:

\[
n(\epsilon) = \frac{g}{e^{(\epsilon - \mu)/k_B T} + 1}
\]

For a completely degenerate electron gas (\( T \to 0 \)), the pressure is determined by the Pauli exclusion principle. The neutron star equation of state is derived by integrating the Fermi-Dirac integral for neutrons with nuclear interactions included (Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equation):

\[
\frac{dP}{dr} = - \frac{G (\rho + P/c^2)(M + 4\pi r^3 P/c^2)}{r^2 (1 - 2 G M / c^2 r)}
\]

The quantum tunneling probability through a potential barrier \( V(x) \) is approximately:

\[
T \approx \exp\left( -2 \int_{x_1}^{x_2} \sqrt{\frac{2m}{\hbar^2} (V(x) - E)} \, dx \right)
\]

This governs nuclear fusion at stellar temperatures far below the classical Coulomb barrier energy.

## 12.1 The Chandrasekhar Limit: Full Derivation

The total energy of a completely degenerate, ultra-relativistic electron gas in a sphere of mass \( M \) and radius \( R \) is:

\[
E = \int_0^R \frac{K_2}{\mu_e^{4/3}} \rho^{4/3} 4\pi r^2 dr - \frac{3}{5} \frac{G M^2}{R}
\]

Using \( M = \int_0^R 4\pi r^2 \rho \, dr \) and scaling \( R \propto M^{-1/3} \), the total energy becomes:

\[
E = A M^{4/3} - B \frac{M^2}{R}
\]

with \( A \) and \( B \) constants depending on \( K_2 \) and \( \mu_e \). Minimizing \( E \) with respect to \( R \) (or \( M \)) yields the condition \( dE/dR = 0 \), which gives the maximum mass:

\[
M_{\mathrm{Ch}} = \frac{\sqrt{3\pi}}{2} \left( \frac{\hbar c}{G} \right)^{3/2} \frac{1}{(\mu_e m_u)^2}
\]

Substituting \( \mu_e = 2 \) (carbon-oxygen) gives \( M_{\mathrm{Ch}} \approx 1.38 M_\odot \). This is the mass at which electron degeneracy pressure can no longer support the star against gravity. The derivation is exact and relies only on the Fermi-Dirac statistics and Newtonian gravity (with general relativistic corrections reducing the value by a few percent).

## 12.2 The Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff Equation: Derivation

For a neutron star, the equation of hydrostatic equilibrium in general relativity is modified to include relativistic corrections to pressure and density:

\[
\frac{dP}{dr} = - \frac{G}{c^2} \frac{(\rho + P/c^2)(M + 4\pi r^3 P/c^2)}{r^2 (1 - 2 G M / c^2 r)}
\]

This is derived by substituting the perfect-fluid stress-energy tensor \( T^{\mu\nu} = (\rho + P/c^2) u^\mu u^\nu + P g^{\mu\nu} \) into the Einstein field equations with the metric:

\[
ds^2 = -e^{\nu(r)} c^2 dt^2 + e^{\lambda(r)} dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2
\]

The derivation involves computing the Christoffel symbols for this metric, substituting into the Einstein tensor, and solving the resulting system of differential equations. The TOV equation is solved numerically for a given equation of state \( P(\rho) \). The maximum neutron star mass depends on the nuclear equation of state, with current constraints from gravitational-wave observations (GW170817) giving \( M_{\mathrm{max}} \approx 2.1 - 2.3 M_\odot \).

## 12.4 Literary Reflection: Dostoevsky's Underground Star — The Freedom of Collapse

Dostoevsky's underground man asks whether freedom is worth the suffering. The neutron star is the answer given by physics — not in words, but in density. The star that collapses through its own weight does not ask for permission. It asks only one question: how much can I hold before I must become something else? The answer is the Chandrasekhar limit. The question remains alive in every white dwarf, in every accretion disk, in every supernova. The star that collapses is not the star that fails. It is the star that chooses transformation — not out of hope, but out of the necessity of the claim it has become.

The underground star does not ask to be observed. It asks only to be understood — not as a point of light, but as the end of a life, the beginning of a new form, the claim that gravity settles not by destruction, but by the creation of something denser, something deeper, something that holds more of the universe in less space. The observer measures the mass. The observer measures the radius. The observer measures the gravitational wave. But the claim that is settled is not in the measurement. It is in the existence of the star itself — the claim that the universe, when it has nothing left to burn, does not disappear. It becomes dense. It becomes quiet. It becomes the measure of everything that remains.
