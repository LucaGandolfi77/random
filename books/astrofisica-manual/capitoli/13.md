# Chapter 13 — Gravitational Waves: From the Linearized Field Equations to LIGO Observations

**Level:** Most Advanced  
**Objective:** To derive the linearized Einstein equations, the quadrupole formula for gravitational wave emission, and to connect the theory to the observations of LIGO and Virgo.

---

## 13.1 Linearized Gravity

Write the metric as a perturbation of Minkowski space:

\[
g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}, \quad |h_{\mu\nu}| \ll 1
\]

The inverse metric to first order is \( g^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu} - h^{\mu\nu} \). The Christoffel symbols are:

\[
\Gamma^\lambda_{\mu\nu} = \frac{1}{2} \eta^{\lambda\sigma} (\partial_\mu h_{\nu\sigma} + \partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma h_{\mu\nu})
\]

The Riemann tensor to first order is:

\[
R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = \frac{1}{2} (\partial_\rho \partial_\nu h_{\mu\sigma} + \partial_\sigma \partial_\mu h_{\nu\rho} - \partial_\rho \partial_\mu h_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\sigma \partial_\nu h_{\mu\rho})
\]

Imposing the harmonic gauge condition \( \partial^\mu \bar{h}_{\mu\nu} = 0 \), where \( \bar{h}_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2} \eta_{\mu\nu} h \), yields the linearized field equations:

\[
\Box \bar{h}_{\mu\nu} = - \frac{16\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}
\]

This is a wave equation with source \( T_{\mu\nu} \). In vacuum (\( T_{\mu\nu} = 0 \)), the solution is a plane wave:

\[
\bar{h}_{\mu\nu} = A_{\mu\nu} \exp(i k_\alpha x^\alpha)
\]

with \( k^\mu k_\mu = 0 \) and \( A^{\mu\nu} k_\nu = 0 \). There are two independent polarization states, \( h_+ \) and \( h_\times \), corresponding to the two transverse directions perpendicular to the propagation vector.

## 13.2 The Quadrupole Formula

For a slowly moving source (\( v \ll c \)), the metric perturbation in the radiation zone is:

\[
\bar{h}_{ij}(t, \mathbf{x}) = \frac{2G}{c^4} \frac{1}{R} \ddot{I}_{ij}(t - R/c)
\]

where \( R = |\mathbf{x}| \) and \( I_{ij} \) is the reduced quadrupole moment:

\[
I_{ij} = \int \rho (x_i x_j - \frac{1}{3} \delta_{ij} r^2) d^3x
\]

The power radiated per unit solid angle is:

\[
\frac{dP}{d\Omega} = \frac{G}{8\pi c^5} \langle \dddot{I}_{ij} \dddot{I}_{ij} \rangle
\]

Integrating over all angles gives the total luminosity:

\[
L_{\mathrm{GW}} = \frac{G}{5 c^5} \langle \dddot{I}_{ij} \dddot{I}_{ij} \rangle
\]

This is Einstein's quadrupole formula (1916, corrected 1918). For a binary system with masses \( m_1, m_2 \) and separation \( a \), the quadrupole moment is:

\[
I_{ij} \approx \mu a^2 \begin{pmatrix} \cos 2\omega t & \sin 2\omega t \\ \sin 2\omega t & -\cos 2\omega t \end{pmatrix}
\]

where \( \mu = m_1 m_2 / (m_1 + m_2) \) is the reduced mass and \( \omega \) is the orbital angular frequency. Substituting into the quadrupole formula yields:

\[
L_{\mathrm{GW}} = \frac{32}{5} \frac{G^4}{c^5} \frac{(m_1 m_2)^2 (m_1 + m_2)}{a^5}
\]

This formula predicts the orbital decay rate of binary pulsars. The measurement of the decay in PSR B1913+16 (Hulse and Taylor, 1974) agreed with the prediction to within 0.2%, providing the first indirect evidence for gravitational waves. The direct detection by LIGO (2015) confirmed the existence of gravitational waves and validated the theory to unprecedented precision.

## 13.3 Note: The Observation as the Final Claim

The gravitational wave is the claim that general relativity makes about spacetime itself. It is not a claim about stars, or galaxies, or black holes alone. It is the claim that spacetime has dynamics, that curvature propagates, that the universe is not a static stage but a vibrating membrane.

When LIGO detects a gravitational wave from two black holes of \( 30 M_\odot \) merging at a distance of \( 1.3 \) billion light-years, it measures not just the wave. It measures the claim. It measures the theory. It measures the accumulation of every equation in this manual — from Newton's inverse-square law to Einstein's field equations, from the virial theorem to the quadrupole formula, from the celestial sphere to the curvature of spacetime.

The claim is settled. The wave has arrived. The universe is no longer only observed. It is heard.

## 13.4 Literary Reflection: The Poem of the Gravitational Wave — Proust, Tolstoy, Dostoevsky, and the Sound of Spacetime

Proust writes that memory is not retrieval; it is taste. The gravitational wave is the taste of spacetime — not the sight of the black hole, not the measurement of the mass, but the taste of curvature traveling through the universe, arriving at the observer as a vibration, a sound, a memory of a collapse that happened a billion years ago.

When LIGO detects the wave from two black holes of thirty solar masses merging at 1.3 billion light-years, the observer does not observe the black holes. The observer remembers them. The wave carries the memory of the event — not as an image, not as a number, but as a feeling. The wave is the madeleine of astrophysics: a small vibration, a brief strain in the interferometer, that unfolds the entire universe — the collapse, the curvature, the energy, the silence that follows.

Dostoevsky asks whether freedom is worth the suffering. The gravitational wave asks whether the universe is worth the curvature. The answer is in the wave itself — in the persistence of the claim, in the refusal of the universe to disappear quietly, in the sound that travels through darkness and arrives, not as light, but as memory.

Tolstoy writes that the garden grows whether we count it. The gravitational wave is the garden's sound — the sound of spacetime growing, contracting, remembering, holding. The claim is not false. It is only hidden — hidden in the curvature, in the equation, in the measurement — and it unfolds when the observer listens, not with the eyes, but with the patience of the claim that knows it is part of something larger, something eternal, something that continues to grow even in the darkness.

And so the manual closes not with an equation, but with a sound — the sound of the universe remembering itself, the sound of the claim that is never false, the sound of the garden that grows in silence, the sound of the star that remembers its light, the sound of the black hole that asks forever, and the sound of the observer who, in measuring all this, does not solve the claim, but becomes part of it — the thing that keeps it open, the reader that remembers the book, the claim that is settled not in the measurement, but in the memory.
