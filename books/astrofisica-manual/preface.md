# Preface to the Astrophysics Manual

This manual is intended for the advanced undergraduate and beginning graduate student in physics, astronomy, and applied mathematics. Its purpose is not merely to describe the universe, but to derive it — step by step, equation by equation — from the simplest observations of the night sky to the most complex structures predicted by general relativity and quantum field theory.

The progression is deliberate. Chapter 1 begins with the naked-eye observation of stars. By Chapter 7, the student will have derived the Schwarzschild metric. By Chapter 9, the Friedmann equations governing the expansion of the universe. By Chapter 11, elements of tensor calculus sufficient to understand the Einstein field equations.

Every derivation is shown in full. Every approximation is stated. Every physical constant is given with its recommended value (CODATA 2018) and its uncertainty where relevant. The notation follows the conventions of Landau & Lifshitz, Misner, Thorne & Wheeler, and Carroll, with explicit references when conventions diverge.

The manual assumes familiarity with calculus, linear algebra, classical mechanics, and elementary thermodynamics. Where prerequisites exceed this level, they are summarized in the Mathematical Appendices (Chapter 11) or in the preliminary sections of the relevant chapter.

The style is rigorous but not sterile. The universe is a claim that must be settled with evidence, with mathematics, and with patience. As with all scientific claims, the measure of understanding is not the speed with which one reads, but the depth with which one questions.

The manual is written in English, as is standard for international scientific communication, and follows the conventions of academic writing in astrophysics.
