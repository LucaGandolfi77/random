# Chapter 1 — The Gate

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (the warehouse of the Germoglio, the gardens) · **Story time:** March–spring 2023
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/01.md` ✅

---

## Draft

The gate opened at half past seven, and the half past seven bus was full as always, and full, in Milan, isn't called full: it's called normal. I stood in line with the folded sheet, and the sheet said my name pronounced wrong, and the writing: *work release*. Work release. At thirty-four, work release is the thing you keep: you keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.

The cooperative's warehouse was in Bresso, and the cooperative was called Il Germoglio (the sprout), and the sprout, in the barra, I didn't know: I only knew the one that grows from the asphalt, and the asphalt doesn't sprout: it cracks. The warehouse was full of plants: the plants in pots, the plants in sacks, the plants that wait. The plants that wait, in the morning, are the plants you don't buy: you plant them. The warehouse's smell was the smell of fresh soil, and the soil, in the morning, smelled of those things you don't describe: you feel them. In the barra the smell of the soil was the smell of the pier, and the pier, in the morning, smelled of salt. Here the soil smelled of rain. The two smells were the same smell, said in two languages.

Vanda was the colleague, and Vanda was twenty-nine, and she knew all the plants: she knew them the way you know the things that are needed: perfectly. She gave me the gloves, and the gloves were the ones for those who carry: they're given, and they're kept. She said one thing only, the first morning:

— Plants aren't asked. They're planted. —

It was the first thing she said to me, and the thing, in the morning, felt like the opposite of the code. The code, in the barra, said: things aren't asked. They aren't said. Vanda said: plants aren't asked — they're planted. The two sentences resembled each other, and they resembled each other in nothing: mine didn't end, hers did. The sentence that ends, in the morning, is the sentence you keep: you keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.

The gardens of Milan we did every day: the public gardens, the private gardens, the gardens you don't see. The garden of via Borsieri was the one we did most, and via Borsieri, in Milan, is the neighbourhood you don't choose: you take it. Pruning, in the morning, is the thing you don't think: you do. And the pruning done, in the morning, holds your life: it holds it the way doors that close hold.

I had done gardening since March, and gardening isn't the trade you believe: it isn't the green. It's the cutting, the sack, the planting. The seven hours I did with my hands, and hands, in gloves, are the hands of someone who does everything: they keep. At noon I ate standing with the two-euro sandwich, and the sandwich, in the garden, is the sandwich of those who come back: you eat standing, and you don't know what you're eating.

The return was at half past five, and the gate, in the evening, closed to make you come back in. The count was at six, and the count, at six, is the thing that isn't contested: it's taken. I got used to it in two weeks, and habit, when you get used to it, doesn't end: it lowers. It stayed there. I knew it.

The cell was 23, and cell 23 had a window that gives onto the inner courtyard, and the inner courtyard, in the evening, is the courtyard you don't choose: you put up with it. On the window there was the usual light, and the light, in the evening, says neither day nor night, and it's fine, because in cells time isn't needed: hours are. The cell was a cell for one, and a cell for one, at thirty-four, is the richest thing in the system: you have it, or you don't, and you have it with the things you keep.

In the evening, on the bed, I looked at the folded sheet. The sheet said my name pronounced wrong, and my name pronounced wrong, in the evening, is the name you keep: you keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.

The code, in the barra, said one thing only: heavy things aren't said. I said it again, and I said it the way you say the things that aren't said: for real.

The gate closed at half past five, and the gate, in the evening, closed to make you come back in.
