# Chapter 2 — The House of the Dead

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (the library of Bollate, cell 23) · **Story time:** summer 2023
**Words:** ~1.200 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/02.md` ✅

---

## Draft

The library of Bollate opens on Thursday, and the library, on Thursday, is the room you don't choose: you take it. I stood in line with the folded sheet, and the library's queue is the shortest queue in the prison: two people. The library's smell was the smell of paper, and paper, in the morning, smelled of those things you don't describe: you feel them. In the barra paper smelled of nothing: in the barra things didn't smell, they were paid for.

Adele was behind the desk, and Adele was fifty-seven, grey hair gathered with a pencil, and the hands of someone who leafs through: the hands of someone who leafs through, in the morning, are the hands you don't buy: they're kept.

— What do you read? — Adele said.

It was the first question in six years that wasn't the rule. Questions, in prison, are the usual questions: the name, the number, the purpose. Adele's question was different: it was a question you don't answer with a number. I didn't know what to answer. The true answer was one: nothing. I didn't read. I had left school at fourteen, and reading, in the barra, wasn't done: the code said one thing only, and the code wasn't a book.

— Nothing — I said.

It was the first true thing I said in six years, and the true thing, in the library, isn't contested: it's listened to. Adele said nothing. She went to the shelves, and the shelves were full, and the hands of someone who leafs through took a book, and the book she put on the desk, in front of me, without asking.

— Read this — she said. — It's given by someone who lived it. —

The book was *Memories from the House of the Dead*. The cover was worn at the corners, and a worn cover, in the morning, is the cover of books that are read: they're read and they aren't bought. I read it in the cell, at night, with the usual lamp, and reading, at night, in the cell, is the reading you don't expect: you do it. The book said a thing I didn't know: that there was a man who had lived what I was living, and had written it. The house of the dead: the cold, the count, the gate, the work. My life, written by a man who had lived it (Dostoevsky), and the writing, at night, in the cell, is the thing you keep: you keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.

Man, the books say, gets used to everything (Dostoevsky), and the sentence, in the cell, felt like the truest sentence of the prison: I had heard it all my life, and I didn't know it was a sentence from a book. It was the sentence of my life, said by someone else, and the sentences of life said by someone else, at night, aren't forgotten: they're kept. The sentence said the other thing too, which I had heard without hearing: that habit, when you get used to it, doesn't end. It lowers. And its lowering, at night, in the cell, was the lowering of someone who doesn't sleep: you feel it.

The Thursday after I went back to the library, and the library, on Thursday, is the room you don't choose: you take it. Adele was behind the desk, and she looked at me, and Adele's look was the look of someone who knows: she knew I had come back, and didn't ask anything. Heavy things aren't asked: they're listened to. She took another book, and the book she put on the desk, in front of me, without asking.

— Read this — she said. — It's given by someone who waited. —

It was the second true thing of the week, and the second, usually, is the time you learn to stay. The book was another, and the book, in the cell, at night, was the book you keep: you keep it the way you keep doors that close. The library was the room you don't choose: you take it, and you take it every week, and the week, in the library, was the week you don't cross: you live it.

In the evening, on the bed, I looked at the window. The window gave onto the inner courtyard, and the inner courtyard, at night, is the courtyard you don't choose: you put up with it. On the window there was the usual light, and the light, at night, says neither day nor night, and it's fine, because in cells time isn't needed: hours are.

The library closed at five, and the library, unlike the gate, closed to make you go out.
