# Chapter 3 — The Pruning

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (the gardens, the warehouse of the Germoglio) · **Story time:** autumn 2023
**Words:** ~1.100 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/03.md` ✅

---

## Draft

> "Still dear to me was this solitary hill, / and this hedge, that from so great a part / of the last horizon excludes the sight."
>
> — Giacomo Leopardi, *L'infinito*

Autumn came with the leaves, and the leaves, in Milan, fall where things fall: for real. Pruning began in October, and pruning, in October, is the thing you don't think: you do. The gardens' hedges were the hedges you don't choose: you prune them. The smell of fresh cutting, in the morning, was the smell I didn't know: you feel it. The smell of fresh cutting smelled of green, and green, at thirty-four, was the colour I didn't have: you have it, or you don't, and I was having it.

Vanda taught me the cutting, and the cutting, in the morning, is the thing you don't imagine: the shears, the line, the hand that doesn't shake.

— Cutting doesn't kill — Vanda said. — It makes grow. Whoever prunes knows what the plant will do in spring. —

It was the first lesson I received from outside, and the lesson, in the morning, felt like the opposite of the prison: in the prison cutting doesn't make grow. Cutting takes away. But Vanda's lesson said the opposite, and the opposite, in the morning, isn't contested: it's learned. And learning, at thirty-four, is the thing you don't buy: you receive it.

The hedges I pruned were the hedges of via Borsieri, and the hedges of via Borsieri were tall, and tall hedges look beyond. A boy, in the books Adele had given me, climbed a hill and looked beyond the hedge: unending spaces, superhuman silences (Leopardi, *L'infinito*). And he understood a thing nobody understands at twenty-two: that the infinite doesn't free. It frightens. The hedge wasn't a cage: it was the measure that made the infinite possible.

I had pruned the hedges for a month, and the hedge, in the morning, was the thing I didn't think: I did it. And the thing you don't think, in the morning, is the thing that holds you: it holds it the way doors that close hold. The gate of Bollate, in the evening, closes to make you come back in, and the gate was a hedge: the measure that made the outside possible. Ten hours a day, or nine and a half, which is the same account: and the nine and a half, without the gate, were the infinite. And the infinite, without a hedge, frightens.

The code, in the barra, said one thing only: heavy things aren't said. The code was a hedge, and the hedge, in the barra, wasn't a measure: it was a wall. And walls aren't pruned: they crack, and cracking, in the barra, is the thing you don't ask: it's paid for. The hedge of via Borsieri, in the morning, was pruned: and the pruning, at thirty-four, was the pruning of things that are kept: they're kept and they're pruned.

The garden, at thirty-four, was the clock you don't buy: you prune it. The four seasons, in Milan, weren't waited for: they were pruned. In October you prune, in March you plant, in June you water, in January you cover: the four seasons were the four hours of the day, and the four hours of the day, at thirty-four, were the hours the code hadn't taught me: the hours that are planted. In the barra time wasn't planted: it was finished. The eleven years of the code were the years that are finished, and finishing, in the barra, is the finishing that isn't counted: it's put up with. The garden was planted, and the planting, at thirty-four, was the planting of things that are kept: they're kept and they're pruned.

In the evening, in the cell, I looked at the book Adele had given me, and the book was the book you keep: you keep it the way you keep doors that close. Reading, at night, in the cell, is the reading you don't expect: you do it. And the reading done, at night, holds your life: it holds it the way the hedge holds the horizon.

The hedges, in Milan, weren't cages: they were measures. And the measures, in the morning, you prune.
