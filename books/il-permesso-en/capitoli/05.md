# Chapter 5 — The Smell

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (the gardens, cell 23); the barra (memories) · **Story time:** summer 2024
**Words:** ~1.100 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/05.md` ✅

---

## Draft

The smell came on a Friday in June, in the garden, and the smell was of rain on asphalt, and asphalt, under the rain, smelled of those things you don't describe: you feel them. The past doesn't call: it arrives, by smell (Proust), and the smell took me to the barra, to Assunta's house, to my father's pier.

The past, in the barra, wasn't called: it was had. I lived the barra from when my father fell, and my father fell in 2007, and I was sixteen. My father was a worker at the pier, and the pier, in Naples, is the pier of those who carry: you carry and you don't keep. My father fell, and falling, at the pier, is the falling you don't ask: it's paid for. Assunta said nothing. Heavy things aren't said: they're kept, and they're kept at the bottom of the house, the way you keep the things that aren't needed.

My father, in the evening, came back with salt, and salt, at the pier, isn't measured: it's felt. In the books Adele had given me there was a man who waited for salt: he waited for it the way you wait for the things you don't buy. It was the same salt. The world, in the evening, has one salt, and nobody chooses it: and the things nobody chooses are the things that hold you.

The barra began after. Not at once: after. The year after, and the year after, in the barra, is the year in which heavy things are kept: they're kept and they're weighed, and the weight, in the barra, you don't feel: you notice later. Dealing was the trade you don't choose: you take it. The code said one thing only: don't speak. And the code, in the barra, was the only school: I followed it for eleven years, and the eleven years, in the barra, were the years that aren't counted: they're finished. The code, in the barra, was taught: it was taught without being asked, and without opening: it was the school that didn't have books, and books, in the barra, weren't there: there was the code. The school of the code was the school of those who don't choose: you take it, and you follow it, and you finish it. The school of the library was the school of those who don't complain: you take it, and you follow it, and you don't finish it. The two schools were the two schools of my life, and the two schools don't trade places: they're kept. One closed, the other opens: and the school that opens, at thirty-five, is the school you keep.

The arrest was in 2019, and the arrest, in the barra, is the arrest you don't expect: you put up with it. Six years of sentence, and the six years, in the cell, are the six years you don't cross: they're kept. Habit is an anaesthesia, it puts your days to sleep one by one (Proust), and the days put to sleep, in the cell, look like a life. I got used to it in two months. Man, the books say, gets used to everything (Dostoevsky), and the sentence, in the cell, was the sentence of my life: I had heard it all my life, and I didn't know it was a sentence from a book.

The smell, in the garden, stayed. I didn't say it to anyone. Heavy things aren't said: they're kept, and they're kept at the bottom of the garden, the way you keep the things that aren't needed. The smell, in the evening, in the cell, was still there: the smell of asphalt, of salt water, of the pier. The pier, at night, in the cell, smelled of those things you don't describe: you feel them.

Assunta, on Sunday, didn't ask anything. The mothers of the barra don't ask: they know. The answer, on the phone, isn't said: it's sent in instalments. And instalments, on the phone, are the instalments of life: they're sent and they aren't counted.

In the evening, on the bed, I looked at the window. The window gave onto the inner courtyard, and the inner courtyard, at night, is the courtyard you don't choose: you put up with it. The smell, in the evening, doesn't call: it arrives. And the smell, in the evening, is kept: you keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.
