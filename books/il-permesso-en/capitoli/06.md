# Chapter 6 — The Thought

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (Bollate, the course room) · **Story time:** autumn 2024 – winter 2025
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/06.md` ✅

---

## Draft

The notice was on the corridor wall, and the notice said a thing I didn't know: that university was held inside. The Statale of Milan, the course in Italian literature, the room was in the prison, and the prison, at seven in the evening, was the prison you don't choose: you take it. I stood in front of the notice, in the morning, and the notice was the thing I didn't expect: you put up with it, or you take it. I took it.

I enrolled with Nunzio, and Nunzio signed, and Nunzio's hands, on the sheet, were the hands of someone who gives: they give without asking. The enrolment queue was the usual queue: the name pronounced wrong, the number, the purpose. The purpose, in the form, was the usual purpose: work. And work, in the form, was the usual work: work release. I wrote the other thing, and the other thing was outside the form: study. And study, outside the form, to the educator, is the thing you keep: you keep it the way you keep doors that close.

The course room was a room with six tables, and the six tables were six men with a purpose, and the six men spoke softly: you speak softly, as is their habit, the men who study — you speak softly because getting it wrong out loud costs, and the cost, in the room, is the only thing that isn't paid.

Professor Malerba was fifty, and Italian she knew everything: she knew it the way you know the things that are needed: perfectly. The first lesson began with a book, and the book was the book I already had: Leopardi. Professor Malerba read *To Silvia*, and reading, in the room, is the reading you don't expect: you do it. I had read it alone, in the cell, and reading alone, in the cell, is the reading you keep; reading in the room, in the room, is the reading you say: you say the things you don't say alone.

— O nature, o nature, why do you not deliver what you promise then? — Professor Malerba said, and the sentence, in the room, was the truest sentence of the prison: the sea that promised fish didn't give it anymore, and nature, at thirty-five, is the thing that isn't contested: it's crossed.

— And why doesn't it deliver? — I asked, once, with my hand raised. A raised hand, in the room, is the hand you don't buy: you raise it.

— Because it promises in spring — Professor Malerba said, laughing softly. — And delivers in autumn. And autumn, sometimes, isn't there. —

It was the truest answer of the lesson, and the answer, in the room, isn't contested: it's listened to. Professor Malerba knew it: promising, at school, is done in spring; delivering is done in autumn, and autumn, sometimes, isn't there. It's the thing nobody says at school: that autumn, sometimes, isn't there. I knew it. In the barra autumn wasn't there: there was the year after, and the year after, in the barra, was the year in which heavy things are kept.

Salvatore was the boy in the class, and Salvatore was twenty-two, and twenty-two, in the room, is the twenty-two that wasn't counted: it was counted. Salvatore was the one I was, ten years before: the boy who doesn't ask, the boy who keeps, the boy who doesn't complain. The mirror, in the room, is the mirror you don't choose: you look at it. And I looked at it, and I looked at it the way you look at rare things: and rare things, at night, don't answer: they're kept.

The new dominant thought came in the evening, in the cell, with the book open. Every human being has a thought that governs him (Leopardi, *Zibaldone*), and the sentence, in the cell, was the sentence of my life: mine, for thirty-four years, had been the code. The code was the thought that governed, and the thought that governs isn't a cage: it's the thing that makes you walk straight. And the code, in the barra, made me walk straight on the wrong straight path. The new thought was one, and it was simple: the degree, the books, the lesson. And the new thought, at thirty-five, made me walk straight on the right straight path.

The subjunctive came in January, in the room, with the voice of someone who doesn't complain. *If I were, if I had, if I could*: and the subjunctive, at thirty-five, bends: and bending is the thing I am learning.
