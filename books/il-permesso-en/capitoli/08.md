# Chapter 8 — The Old Ones

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (the warehouse of the Germoglio, the gardens); the barra (memories) · **Story time:** spring 2025 (April)
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/08.md` ✅

## Draft

> "O nature, o nature, / why do you not deliver / what you promise then? why do you deceive / your children so?"
>
> — Giacomo Leopardi, *A Silvia*

Gennaro called on a Friday in April, from the barra, and Gennaro was the usual old one: the old one who calls when the sentence ends, and the old one who calls, on the phone, is the old one you don't buy: you kept him. I had kept Gennaro for eleven years, and the eleven years, in the barra, were the years of the code: don't speak. Gennaro spoke, and Gennaro's speaking, on the phone, was the code's speaking: softly, as is their habit, the old ones — you speak softly because shouting tires, and tiredness, in the barra, is the only thing that isn't bought.

— Your sentence is over — Gennaro said. — We need you. The shop, the place. Things are like before. —

Things are like before. It was the barra's promise, and the barra's promise, on the phone, was the promise that isn't kept: nature, they said at school, promises: and nature doesn't deliver what it promises then (Leopardi, *A Silvia*), and the barra that promised things like before didn't give them anymore. The shop, in the barra, is the shop you don't buy: you take it. And the things you take, in the barra, aren't kept: they're paid for.

— I can't — I said, with the voice that doesn't complain.

It was the first time I said *I can't*, and *I can't*, on the phone, was the heaviest thing of the month: you feel that the other knows. Gennaro knew it. He didn't answer. Heavy things aren't said: they're kept.

— And the code? — Gennaro said, in the end, with the voice of someone who says the thing he never says.

— The code was the only school I had — I said. — Now I have another. —

It was the truth, and the truth, on the phone, you feel the way you feel damp in walls: you don't see it. You feel it. Gennaro felt it, and didn't answer. The phone closed, and the phone, in the barra, closes to not come back: the code's rule. But the phone, in Milan, closes to come back: the Sunday after, Assunta.

Gennaro, though, called again one evening in May, and calling again, in the barra, is the calling that isn't contested: it's kept. — You've thought about it — Gennaro said, and the thinking, on the phone, was the thinking that wasn't there. — And the place, next summer. —

— I can't — I said. — I said I can't. —

— The I can't — Gennaro said, laughing softly, and the laugh was the code's laugh: softly, as is their habit, laughs — you can't, they last too long. — The I can't, at thirty-six, isn't a no. It's a thought. —

— It's a no — I said.

It was the truth, and the truth, said twice, isn't contested: it's kept. Gennaro didn't call again, and the not calling again, at thirty-six, is the thing you keep: you keep it the way you keep doors that close. The no, in the evening, is kept: you keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.

My father, at the pier, was a man like any other, living as he should (Tolstoy): the pier, the work, the house. A man like any other, in books, dies on the bed of an apartment he has paid for all his life. My father died at the pier, and the pier isn't an apartment: it's work. My father died in 2007, and the barra began after, and the barra, in the barra, was the opposite of the pier: the pier was had, the barra was taken. The things you have, in the morning, aren't bought: they're kept. The things you take, in the morning, are bought with the price: and the price, in the barra, was the code.

The shop, in the barra, I knew it the way you know the things you don't have: perfectly. The things you study, when they aren't needed, you know perfectly: it's their secret. The shop wasn't needed: the shop was the code's trade, and the code, at thirty-six, was the school I had left.

In the evening, in the cell, I looked at the book Adele had given me, and the book was the book you keep: you keep it the way you keep doors that close. The no, in the evening, is kept: you keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy. And the things you don't buy, in the evening, are the things of life: they're kept and they aren't counted.
