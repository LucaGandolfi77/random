# Chapter 9 — The Strike

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (the gardens, the stop, the gate of Bollate) · **Story time:** summer 2025 (June)
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/09.md` ✅

---

## Draft

The strike came on a Friday in June, and the strike, in Milan, is the thing you don't choose: you put up with it. The ATM was stopped, and the stopped ATM, in the morning, is the city you don't cross: you run. The half past seven bus wasn't there, and the bus that wasn't there, in the morning, was the heaviest bus in the world: emptiness weighs. Run, the sheet said, and the sheet said the opposite of the code: the code said don't speak, the sheet said come back. And the sheet, at thirty-six, was the thing you keep: you keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.

I ran on foot, for five kilometres, with the sack and the gloves, and running, in the morning, is the running you don't think: you do it. Sweat, in the morning, wasn't counted: it was put up with. I arrived at the warehouse at nine, and nine in the morning, in Bresso, was the latest nine in the world: you feel it. Vanda was at the warehouse, and Vanda had the face of someone who knows: she knew I had arrived late, and didn't ask anything. Heavy things aren't asked: they're listened to.

— Strike — Vanda said, and the word was the word that isn't contested: it's put up with.

— Strike — I said.

— We'll do the garden — Vanda said. — The return, though, is yours. —

It was the truest lesson of the morning, and the lesson, at the warehouse, isn't contested: it's listened to. The garden is done, and the return is yours: the two things were the two things of my life, and the two things, at thirty-six, don't trade places: they're kept. The garden was Vanda's, and the return was mine: and the return, at thirty-six, was the thing you don't buy: you keep it.

Time melted. Not the time of always, which is crossed (Proust): the time of the strike, which melts. The eight hours in the garden I did all of them, and the eight hours, at thirty-six, were the eight hours of someone who doesn't have the bus: they're done. And melted time, in the morning, wasn't counted: it was awaited.

In the evening the return was at half past five, and the ATM was still stopped, and the stopped ATM, in the evening, is the city you don't cross: you run. I ran on foot, for ten kilometres, and running, in the evening, is the running you don't think: you do it. Sweat, in the evening, wasn't counted: it was put up with. The sky, in the evening, turned: the sun went and the dark came, and the dark, in Milan, is the dark you don't expect: you put up with it. I arrived at the gate at seventeen forty-seven, and seventeen forty-seven, at thirty-six, was the heaviest seventeen forty-seven in the world: the rule said lateness is the revocation, and the revocation, at thirty-six, was the thing you don't get used to.

Man, the books say, gets used to everything (Dostoevsky): the cold, the count, the gate. Everything, except the revocation. The revocation was the thing you don't get used to: you fear it, and the fear, at thirty-six, was the fear of someone who has one thing to lose: the sheet. The sheet was my home, and home, at thirty-six, was the thing you don't buy: you keep it.

Nunzio was at the gate, with the register in his hand, and the register said the hours, and the hours said seventeen forty-seven. The rule said the revocation. The gate was there, and the gate, at seventeen forty-seven, was the gate that doesn't open: it closes to make you come back in, and the return, at seventeen forty-seven, wasn't there: there was the lateness.

Heavy things aren't said: they're kept, and they're kept at the bottom of the register, the way you keep the things that aren't needed. And the register, that evening, was the register that's kept: it's kept and it doesn't open.

The gate opened at seventeen forty-seven, and the gate, that time, opened to make you come back in.
