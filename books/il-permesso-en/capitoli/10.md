# Chapter 10 — Not Today

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (the gate of Bollate, cell 23) · **Story time:** summer 2025 (June)
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/10.md` ✅

---

## Draft

Nunzio looked at the register, and the register said seventeen forty-seven, and seventeen forty-seven, at thirty-six, was the seventeen forty-seven of the revocation. The rule said the revocation. Nunzio was forty-five, and he signed the sheets: he signed them and didn't look at them, as is their habit, sheets.

— Strike — Nunzio said, and the word was the word that isn't contested: it's put up with.

— Strike — I said.

— Not today — Nunzio said.

Not today. Two words, and the two words, at thirty-six, were the truest two words of the month: the rule said the revocation, and Nunzio said not today. The rule, that time, bent: and a man bent it. In the books of the girl I read, the rule didn't bend: the pending sheet didn't allow you to travel, and the rule, at the Questura, isn't negotiated with anger. The reversal, at thirty-six, is that the rule can be bent: and a man bends it, not anger. Anger, in the barra, bent nothing: it was paid for. The man who bends the rule, in the evening, is the man who gives: and the man who gives, at thirty-six, isn't bought: he's received.

— The rule says the revocation — I said, with the voice that doesn't complain.

— The rule says — Nunzio said. — But the rule doesn't breathe. I, at seventeen forty-seven, breathe. —

It was the truest lesson of the month, and the lesson, at the gate, isn't contested: it's listened to. The rule doesn't breathe: the man breathes. It's the thing nobody says about the system: that the system doesn't breathe, and those who breathe, in the evening, are the thing you keep. You keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.

— And the register? — I asked, and the question is the question that isn't made: it's made, at thirty-six, when the register is home.

— The register says the hours — Nunzio said. — And the hours, sometimes, are wrong. The register isn't wrong: the hours are. —

It was the truest answer of the evening, and the answer, at the gate, isn't contested: it's listened to. The register isn't wrong: the hours are. It's the thing nobody says about the register: that the hours, sometimes, are wrong, and those who know the hours are wrong, in the evening, are the thing you keep. I knew it. The hours, in the barra, were all wrong: the eleven years of the code were the wrong hours of a life.

Nunzio's hands, on the register, were the hands of someone who gives, and the hands of someone who gives, at thirty-six, are the hands you don't buy: you feel them. The register closed, and the register, that evening, closed to not open: the revocation wasn't there. The revocation wasn't there, and the not being there, at thirty-six, is the thing you feel: you feel it the way you feel damp in walls.

— Go — Nunzio said, with the voice of someone who says heavy things lightly. — The count is at six. —

The count was at six, and the count, that evening, was the usual count: the name pronounced wrong, the number, the purpose. And the purpose, that evening, was the purpose that wasn't in the form: study. And study, outside the form, at the count, is the thing you keep: you keep it the way you keep doors that close.

The cell was 23, and cell 23 had a window that gives onto the inner courtyard, and the inner courtyard, in the evening, is the courtyard you don't choose: you put up with it. On the window there was the usual light, and the light, in the evening, says neither day nor night, and it's fine, because in cells time isn't needed: hours are.

The day was kept. Not the day of always, which is crossed: the day of the strike, which is kept. And the day that's kept, in the evening, is the day of life: it's kept and it isn't counted.

The rule, that time, bent: and a man bent it. And the man who bends the rule, in the evening, is the man who gives.
