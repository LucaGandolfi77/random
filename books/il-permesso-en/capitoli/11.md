# Chapter 11 — The Truth

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (the warehouse of the Germoglio, cell 23); the barra (on the phone) · **Story time:** summer 2025 (August–September)
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/11.md` ✅

## Draft

The end of the sentence was the twenty-fifth of September, and the twenty-fifth of September, at thirty-six, is the date you don't forget: you keep it. The weeks missing were six, and the six weeks, at thirty-six, were the six weeks of life: they all aimed. The weeks that aim, at thirty-six, are Leopardi's weeks: happiness was in the waiting (Leopardi, *The Village Saturday*), and the waiting, at thirty-six, was the happiness. In the barra, on Saturday, happiness was the Village Saturday: it was waited for, and the waiting was the happiness. Here happiness was the waiting for the twenty-fifth of September: and the waiting, at thirty-six, isn't contested: it's lived.

The Sunday call I made at noon, and noon, in Milan, is noon in the barra too: the same sun, the same clock, and no time difference. Assunta answered, and Assunta didn't ask *are you well?*: the mothers of the barra don't ask, because they know. The answer, on the phone, isn't said: it's sent in instalments.

— Hi, mum — I said, with the voice that doesn't complain.

— Hi, my love — Assunta said.

The thread of the lies stopped. For six years I had said *I'm well* to Assunta, every Sunday, and the lies, on the phone, stop: they stop the way true things stop, and true things, at thirty-six, were beginning to arrive. The truth, on the phone, was one: the end of the sentence. And the truth, said for the first time, on the phone, at thirty-six, felt like the heaviest thing in the world. The weight, though, was mine: it wasn't the sheet, it wasn't the gate, it wasn't the count. It was mine, and my weight, in the evening, you don't take off: you keep it. I still keep it.

— Mum — I said, and the word is the word you don't buy: you receive it. — On the twenty-fifth of September the sentence ends. —

Assunta said nothing, and the unsaid, on the phone, lasted a minute, or a life; at the end Assunta said the thing she hadn't said for six years:

— But you though, at least be well, eh. —

It was the question she never asked, and she asked it that Sunday, and the question, asked that Sunday, was the truest question of the month: you feel that the other knows. Assunta knew it: she knew the voice had changed, and the changed voice, on the phone, is the thing you notice: you notice it the way you notice damp in walls. And the question, asked that Sunday, was the question I answered:

— Now yes — I said.

It was the truth, and the truth, on the phone, you feel the way you feel damp in walls: you don't see it. You feel it. Assunta felt it, and didn't say anything. Heavy things aren't said: they're kept. But the truth, at thirty-six, wasn't heavy: it was the thing you keep.

— And the unsaid? — Assunta said, in the end, with the voice of someone who says the thing she never said. — The unsaid, in the barra, I have kept for six years. —

— I have kept it too — I said. — For eleven. —

It was the truest truth of the Sunday, and the truth, on the phone, isn't contested: it's listened to. The barra's unsaid, kept for eleven years, was kept by her too: the mothers of the barra keep the unsaid of their children, and the unsaid, on the phone, was kept by two, and keeping it by two, at thirty-six, is the thing you don't buy: you receive it.

The "bricks" were the bricks of saving, and the bricks, at thirty-six, were the bricks of the after: the room, the deposit, the key. I put aside four hundred euros a month, and the four hundred euros a month, for thirty months, were twelve thousand euros: twelve thousand euros, at thirty-six, were the bricks of the house. In the barra the code wasn't bought: it was taken. In Milan the house is bought with the deposit: and the deposit, at thirty-six, is the brick you keep. You keep it the way you keep the things you don't buy.

The weeks that aim, at thirty-six, are the weeks of life: they're kept and they aren't counted.
