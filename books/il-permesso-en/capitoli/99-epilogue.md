# Epilogue — The Sprout

**POV:** Ciro · **Place:** Milan (Bollate, the library, the gardens); the barra (on the phone) · **Story time:** autumn 2026
**Words:** ~1.300 / translation of `il-permesso/capitoli/99-epilogo.md` ✅

---

## Draft

I am in Milan, and Milan, in the morning, has the gate of Bollate. The gate opens at half past seven, and the gate, at half past seven, opens to make you come in and go out: the same door, two directions. The door, at thirty-seven, is the door you have: you have it, or you don't, and you have it with the things you keep, and the things I keep, at thirty-seven, are the things you plant.

The room was on via Padova, fourth floor, with the window that gives onto the inner courtyard, and the room, at thirty-seven, was the room of the bricks: four hundred euros a month, for thirty months, twelve thousand euros. The bricks, at thirty-seven, were the house: they're kept the way the things you don't buy are kept. On the shelf there were the contract, the folded sheet, Adele's books — and the shelf, at thirty-seven, is the thing you didn't have: you have it. The shelf, in the evening, is seen from the bed, and the things on the shelf aren't counted: they're kept. The degree, on the desk, is the degree you don't buy: you receive it, ten hours a day, with the garden, with the books, with the subjunctive.

I go back to Bollate on Thursday, and Thursday, at thirty-seven, is the library's Thursday: the library, on Thursday, is the room you don't choose: you take it. But I take it with books, and books, at thirty-seven, are the books you keep: you keep them the way doors that close are kept. Nunzio opens the gate for me, and Nunzio's hands, on the register, are the hands of someone who gives: they give without asking. The register says my name pronounced right, and the right name, at thirty-seven, is the thing you keep: you keep it the way the things you don't buy are kept.

Salvatore is in the course room, and Salvatore is twenty-three, and twenty-three, in the room, is the twenty-three that wasn't counted: it was counted. Salvatore is the one I was, eleven years before: the boy who doesn't ask, the boy who keeps, the boy who doesn't complain. The mirror, in the room, is the mirror you don't choose: you look at it. And I looked at it, and I looked at it the way you look at rare things: and rare things, in the evening, don't answer: they're kept.

— And what do you read? — I said to him, with the voice that doesn't complain.

— Nothing — Salvatore said.

It was the answer I had given, eleven years before, at the library, to Adele. And Adele gave me the book, and the book was the house of the dead, and the house of the dead was my life, written by a man who had lived it (Dostoevsky). I took the book, and the book I put on the desk, in front of Salvatore, without asking.

— Read this — I said. — It's given by someone who lived it. —

It was Adele's same sentence, and the sentence, in the room, is the sentence that's kept: it's kept and it's passed on. The circle closed, and the circle, at thirty-seven, is the circle you don't buy: you make it. You make it ten hours a day, with the garden, with the books, with the subjunctive.

— And the book, then? — Salvatore said, the Thursday after, with the voice of someone who doesn't ask: he asks.

— The book you give — I said. — To someone who doesn't ask. —

It was the truest lesson of the year, and the lesson, in the room, isn't contested: it's listened to. The book you give: it's the thing nobody says about reading, that reading isn't kept: it's passed on. And reading passed on, in the evening, is the reading of life: it's kept and it's passed on.

The Sunday call I make at noon, and noon, in Milan, is noon in the barra too: the same sun, the same clock, and no time difference. Assunta answers, and Assunta asks *are you well?*: the mothers of the barra, at thirty-seven, ask: they know the answer is the thing you build, and the answer, on the phone, is said.

— Now yes — I said.

It was the truth, and the truth, on the phone, you feel the way you feel damp in walls: you don't see it. You feel it. Assunta felt it, and didn't say anything. Heavy things aren't said: they're kept. But the truth, at thirty-seven, wasn't heavy: it was the thing that's kept.

The moral, in the evening, on the notebook, I wrote it: the straight path isn't found again. It's remade. Ten hours a day, with the garden, with the books, with the right name. The straight path, in the barra, was a line: people spoke of it, and didn't take it. I remake it for a year, ten hours a day, and I haven't found it yet: the garden remakes it, the books remake it, the subjunctive remakes it. And the subjunctive, at thirty-seven, bends: and bending is the thing I learned.

The broom flower, in the books, was the flower that grows on the volcano (Leopardi, *La ginestra*): the flower of people who stand together, and people who stand together, in the morning, are the cooperative: the cooperative was the flower that grew from the asphalt, and the asphalt, in Milan, doesn't sprout: it cracks. But the cooperative sprouted, and the sprout, at thirty-seven, is the flower of people: it's kept and it's planted.

The things you plant aren't bought. They're planted.

I am writing a book, in the evening, at the desk of the room, with the folded sheet next to it. I write it for the first time, and the first time, at thirty-seven, is the truest thing: I didn't know it. The book is called *The Permit*, and the book I write for the straight path: the straight path, in the evening, isn't found again. It's remade, and it's remade with the things you plant.
