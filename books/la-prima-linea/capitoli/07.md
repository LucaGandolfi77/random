## Draft

> "I sometimes despise myself. Isn't that why I despise others too?"
>
> — Mikhail Lermontov, *A Hero of Our Time* ("Princess Mary"; translation, to verify)

The Russian, Nina Petrovna said, is a language that forgives nothing: it asks who does, and what it's done to, and to whom, and with what, and about what, and where. Six cases. The Italian asks nothing: the Italian lets the things stand, and the standing, in Italian, is a standing that doesn't say where the thing is going. The Russian doesn't let anything stand without saying where it goes.

"A thing cannot just be," said Nina Petrovna. "A thing is toward something. If you say the word and don't say the direction, you have said nothing."

And the saying was the saying of the lessons: a letter, a case, the direction. The language, at thirty-two, was the first thing in my life that asked me to say where I was going, and the asking, in the kitchen of Nina Petrovna, was the asking that the months had waited for: nobody had asked me where I was going. The foundry didn't ask. The bottle didn't ask. Vera had asked, and I had answered "nothing". The Russian asked, and the Russian didn't accept "nothing": you had to decline the nothing.

The first sentence I built alone was in March, at the lesson, and the sentence was the sentence of the law: *Ya ne p'yu*. I don't drink. Nina Petrovna looked at me, and the looking lasted the length of the sentence, and the length of the sentence was the length of a life.

"The grammar is right," she said. "The sentence will be tested."

And the sentence will be tested: I didn't understand it then. I understand it now.

The pride was the pride of the ones who learn, and the pride of the ones who learn is a pride that doesn't shout: it walks. I walked the language through the city: the bazaar, the bus, the queue of the post. The words came out one at a time, and the words, at the bazaar, were the words of the things: the sack, the weight, the price. Gadzhi didn't correct me. Gadzhi was sixty and had forty years of the bazaar, and the forty years had taught him the patience of the ones who sell: the customer is always learning.

On Sunday there was the second friendly, and the second friendly was at home, against the team of the port, and the team of the port was the team of the ones who lift: they lifted, and we pushed, and the push, that Sunday, was the push of the things that work. We won 14 to 6, and the winning was the winning of the third division: the earth, the cold that was going, the tea. In the scrum, against the head, we took a ball that wasn't ours, and the ball against the head, in the language of the props, is the ball of the eight: you take it because the eight push, and the eight push because the first row holds, and the holding is the work that nobody sees: it's the work of the men who play so that the others can play.

At the third half, Suleyman called me Etor. And the calling, in front of the men, was the calling of the names: in the Caucasus the names are shortened, and the shortening is a form of belonging. I had been Ettore for thirty-one years, and the thirty-one years had called me Ettore the way the forms call you: whole, and correct. Etor was the name of the field, and the name of the field, at thirty-two, was the first name that was mine without the papers.

Zarema met me at the beach south of the city, at five, and the beach was the beach of nobody: the cold kept it, and the cold was on our side. The seafront of Makhachkala ends where the city ends, and where the city ends the sand begins, and the sand, in March, is the sand of the ones who don't come: the season wasn't there. The season, in Makhachkala, is July, and July was far, and the far was on our side too.

She corrected the alphabet the way Nina Petrovna corrected it, without mercy, and the correcting, from her, was done with the hand: she took the page of the reader and she wrote the letters with her finger on the sand, and the letters, in the sand, were the letters of a language that was hers twice: Russian at the hospital, and the other language, the one of the notebook, at home.

The notebook was a notebook of poems, and the poems were in Avar, and the Avar is the language of the mountains: she didn't read it to anybody. She read one, that evening, to the sea, and the reading was done the way Nina Petrovna read Chekhov: with a voice that doesn't give. I didn't understand a word. I understood the direction: the poems went toward the mountains, and the mountains, in the poems, were the mountains of the ones who don't come down.

"Whose are they?" I asked.

"Mine," she said, and closed the notebook. "Not mine. The names are not for the beach."

And the not saying was the saying, and the saying was the saying of the things that have a house: the poems had a house, and the house was the family, and the family was the thing that the beach didn't know.

The meetings were the meetings of the ones who steal: the Thursday of the lessons, the corridor of the hospital, the beach at five. We didn't hold hands in the city. The city doesn't hold hands: the city sees, and the seeing, in Makhachkala, is the seeing of the aunts, and the aunts see everything, and the aunts tell the mothers, and the mothers tell the house. We were careful, and the careful was the careful of the ones who have something to lose, and the losing, for me, was the team, and for her was the house.

She gave me the Lermontov in April, at the beach, and the Lermontov was a thin volume, the Russian, with the pages read by somebody before: the pencil was the pencil of a student, and the student, in the pencil, was Zarema, at twenty, at the university. She had read it at twenty and had written in the margins, and the margins, at twenty, are the margins of the ones who believe: you write the things you would do. At twenty-seven, she said, the margins embarrass her, and the embarrassment, in a woman who is frank, is the thing she doesn't keep: she gives it away.

"Read it in Russian if you can," she said. "If you can't, read it in the coward's language. The story doesn't care."

The Italian was on the shelf of Nina Petrovna, and the shelf of Nina Petrovna had the Italian of the Union: the editions of the fifties, the ones that were printed for the ones who studied the foreign literatures when the foreign literatures were the window. Nina gave it to me without a comment, and the giving without a comment was the giving of the ones who know: the book was the book, and the book doesn't need the comment.

Pechorin was a man who owed something, and the owing was the owing of the ones who don't pay: he used the people the way you use the tools, and the tools let him, and he despised them for letting him, and he despised himself for using them, and the despising was the despising of the ones who look at themselves every morning and don't like the looking. I knew the looking. The bottle was my Pechorin: it used me, and I let it, and I despised it, and I despised myself, and the two despisings were the despising that Lermontov had written a hundred and eighty years before, and the writing was the writing of a man who had died at twenty-six and had understood at twenty-six the thing I was understanding at thirty-two.

I didn't want to be Pechorin. Pechorin dies coming back, in the fortress of the road, and the dying coming back is the dying of the ones who leave and don't arrive. I wanted to arrive. The arriving, for a prop, is the arriving of the ball: you hold, and the eight push, and the ball arrives. The arriving doesn't have the literature. The arriving has the table.

The night was in May, in the dunes south of the beach, and the dunes were the dunes of the ones who don't come: the season was close but wasn't there, and the sand, at night, kept the day's heat the way the earth of the foundry keeps the metal's heat. The sweetness was the sweetness of the things with a time limit: you know the time limit, and the knowing is the sweetness, and it's the fear. She had to be back by ten. The being back by ten was the being back of the daughters of the houses, and the daughter of the house, at twenty-seven, is still the daughter of the house: the frankness is a luxury, and the luxury has the hours.

Suleyman was her cousin. I knew it from the papers of the club, and the knowing didn't have a weight until the night of the dunes: the cousin of the blood, at the Kasbek, is the cousin of the blood, and the blood, in the Caucasus, is the thing that doesn't translate. If Suleyman knew, the knowing was the team's, and the team's knowing was the city's, and the city's knowing was the end of the guest. The guest who looks at the cousin is the guest who leaves. I was the guest who looked. The looking didn't have a fix. The looking was mine, and it was hers, and the two lookings were the only thing in Makhachkala that didn't have a law.

I believed I was cured. The believing was the believing of the ones who have the list: I didn't drink — five months, and the five months were the months of the field; I played, and the playing was the playing of the ones who have a name, Etor; I spoke, and the speaking was the speaking of the ones who decline; I loved, and the loving was the loving of the ones who have a second more. The list was the list of a man who is cured, and the list didn't have the thing: the cure of the ones who are cured is the thing you don't count, and I counted everything. The counting was the disease, and the disease, at thirty-two, was the disease I had carried from the foundry: I counted the bottles, I counted the months, I counted the second more. The ones who are cured don't count. The ones who are cured stand in the sentence and say the direction.

At the lesson of May, Nina Petrovna said the thing she said once a month, and the thing was the thing of the teachers:

"You read like a man who is in a hurry. The language is not a race."

And the sentence was the sentence that stays, and I didn't stay in it: I was in a hurry. The summer was coming, and the summer, in Makhachkala, is the summer of the mountains: Ruslan had promised the aoul, the hay, the flock of the grandfather. I was in a hurry, and the hurry was the hurry of the ones who think they're cured: the ones who are cured don't hurry. They walk. They read like the ones who walk: without the hurry, and the language is not a race.
