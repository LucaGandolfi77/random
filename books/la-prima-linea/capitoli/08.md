## Draft

> "…a thistle bush. It had been run over by a wheel, and still it stood up from the black earth."
>
> — Leo Tolstoy, *Hadji Murat* (translation, to verify)

The Niva went up on the Sunday, and the going up was the going up of the families: Ruslan, Patimat, Timur, and me, and the car was full of the things of the summer — the sacks of the flour, the sugar, the tea, the presents of the city — and the things of the summer, in the car, were the things of the ones who go up to the ones who stayed. The road was a road of turns, and the turns were the turns of the roads of the mountains: you turn, and the valley goes down, and the valley, from up there, is the country you came from, and the country, from up there, is small. Makhachkala, from the turns, was a line of grey on the grey of the sea, and the line was the city, and the city was the place of the field, and the field, from the turns, didn't exist.

The aul was at two thousand metres, and the aul was a aul of stone: the houses of stone, the walls of stone, the fountain of stone, and the stone, in the aul, is the thing that stays. The stone houses were closed, half of them, and the closed houses, in the aul, are the houses of the ones who went: to Makhachkala, to Moscow, to Turkey, to the places that have the money. The village empties the way the foundries empty: the young go, and the houses close, and the closed houses, behind their doors of iron, keep the things of the ones who left: the carpets, the photos, the stoves. The village keeps them the way the wardrobe keeps the overalls: you don't throw them away, and you don't open.

Magomed was on the pasture above the aul, and Magomed is eighty-one, and Magomed had the flock and the hut and the dog, and the dog barked, and the barking was the barking of the dogs of the mountains: they bark at the ones who come, and they stop when Magomed says the word. Magomed didn't come down to the aul in the summer: he went up in June and came down in September, and the going up and the coming down were the calendar of the flock, and the calendar of the flock is the calendar that doesn't have the Sundays: it has the grass.

Magomed's hands were the hands of the shepherds: the hands that have held the rope for sixty years, and the rope, in the hands, has made the calluses that the soap doesn't take. I knew the calluses. The calluses, in my family, were the calluses of the foundry, and the calluses of Magomed were the calluses of the rope, and the two calluses were the same calluses: the calluses of the work, said in two languages.

The great-grandfather of Ruslan had followed Shamil, and the following, in the middle of the nineteenth century, was the following of the mountains: the war of the Caucasus, the thirty years of the ones who didn't come down. Magomed told it at the fire, in the evening, in the Avar, and Ruslan translated the parts that needed translating, and the parts that needed translating were the names: Shamil, the murids, the villages that were taken and the villages that weren't. The telling was the telling of the things that are kept: Magomed had the story from his father, and the father had it from his, and the story, in the families of the mountains, is the thing that goes down with the flock: it comes down in September, and it goes up in June.

"In forty-four," said Magomed, and Ruslan translated, and the translating was slow, "they took the Chechens. The whole people, in the wagons, through the plain. My father saw them from the road. He didn't speak of it except once, and the once was enough."

And the once, in the telling of Magomed, was the once of the things that are heavy: the wagons went, and the plain was the plain, and the fathers saw. The Caucasus keeps the heavy things the way the houses keep the carpets: behind the doors, and the doors are closed, and the things don't go out. They come out once.

Zaur came up for the telling. Zaur Omarov, fifty-five, the teacher of history of the school of the aul, retired, and the retirement of Zaur was the retirement of the ones who stayed: he taught for thirty years, and the school had the children of the ones who stayed, and the children were fewer every year, and the fewer, in the school of the aul, is the number of the village. Zaur had the memory that doesn't sleep: the dates, the names, the treaties. He spoke Russian with me — he had the Russian of the university, of the years of the Union, of the ones who studied the history in the language of the history.

"The mountains were written by the ones who came," he said, at the fire, and the saying was the saying of a man who has taught the history for thirty years. "Pushkin came. Lermontov came. Tolstoy came. They wrote the mountains, and the mountains, in the books, are the mountains of the ones who wrote them. And the books travel: the books are in your language, in the language of the ones who read. Our mountains are in the songs, and the songs don't travel: the songs are in Avar, and the Avar doesn't have the books. The Avar has the grandmother."

And the grandmother, in the mountains, is the library that doesn't have the catalogue: she has the songs, and the songs die with her, and the dying, in the aul, is the dying of the language. I didn't know the language. The not knowing was the not knowing of the ones who come with the books.

"You came with the books," said Zaur, and he said it without the irony, the way the true things are said. "You came with the books and the books came before you. The Caucasus that you have in your head is the Caucasus of Lermontov: the mountains, the men on the horses, the soul. The soul of the books. And the soul of the books doesn't carry the hay."

And the saying was the saying that costs, and the cost was paid the way the costs are paid in the mountains: with the work. The next morning, at five, there was the hay.

The hay was the work of the aul, and the hay doesn't have the literature: it has the scythe, the sun, the back. We cut from five to ten, before the heat, and the cutting was the cutting of the lines: you cut, and the line goes, and the line, in the field, is the line of the ones who cut together. Ruslan cut the way the aul cuts: with the swing that doesn't stop, and the swing is the swing of the years: you don't learn the swing in a summer. I cut the way the foundry cuts: with the force, and the force, in the scythe, is not the thing: the scythe wants the swing, and the swing wants the years. Magomed watched me cut for ten minutes, and the watching was the watching of the ones who know, and then he took the scythe from my hands and gave it back after the correcting of the grip, and the correcting was done without a word: the hands of Magomed spoke.

In the field, at the edge of the cut, there was the thistle: the thistle that had been run over by the wheel of the tractor in the spring, and the thistle had stood up again, and the standing up was the standing up of the things that don't yield. I looked at the thistle the way you look at the things you will find in a book after: I didn't know the book. The book knew me.

The dream came at the third day. The dream was the dream of the books: the simple life, the flock, the hut, the mountain, and the man who leaves the things and stays. The dream, in the books of the Russians, is the dream of the ones who have the money and the city and the lung: they come to the mountain to be simple, and the mountain lets them, and the mountain, in the books, is the cure. I had the dream the way you have a dream that you read: I had read it, and I hadn't read it, and the reading was in me anyway, the way the books are in the ones who came before: I looked at the pasture, and the pasture was the cure, and I thought the thing: stay. Stay here. Be the shepherd.

I said it to Ruslan at the fire, and the saying was the saying of a man who is thirty-two and has the lung of his father and the bottle of the years and a language of three hundred words. Ruslan didn't laugh. The men of the Caucasus don't laugh at the dreams of the guests: the dreams are a debt too.

"You think this is simple," he said. "This is not simple. This is work, and the work doesn't have the literature. Magomed has the flock because Magomed is Magomed: he went up at twenty and didn't go down. You would go down in October."

"You went down," I said.

"I was born here," said Ruslan. "The going down of the ones who are born here is a going down that keeps the going up. The going down of the ones who arrive is a going down that forgets."

And the sentence was the sentence that costs, and the cost was the cost of the true things: the dream of the simple life was the dream of a man who had read the books, and the books, in the dream, had come before me. The mountain didn't cure. The mountain was a mountain: it had the hay, and the hay didn't have the literature, and the man who cut the hay with the swing of thirty years was not a cure and was not a book: he was Magomed, and Magomed was eighty-one, and the flock didn't care about the books.

The nights in the mountains were the nights of the stars, and the stars, in the aul, are the stars of the ones who don't have the light of the cities: they are many, and they are cold, and they are the stars that the books describe. The books, even there, were before me. I sat at the fire with Magomed, and Magomed said the things that the shepherds say at the fire, and the things were the things of the flock: the grass, the wolves, the winter. And then he said the thing that the shepherds say once, and the once was enough:

"The mountains don't cure," he said, in Avar, and Ruslan translated. "The mountains are not a doctor. The mountains are a mountain. The ones who come to be cured go down sicker, because the mountain shows you what you are, and what you are is not a cure."

And the showing was the showing of the nights: I was a man of a foundry, of a language of three hundred words, of a lung, of a bottle that I had left in Sesto and carried anyway. The stars didn't lie. The stars, at two thousand metres, are the stars of the ones who don't have the fog: everything is visible, and the visible, that night, was me.

The homesickness came the night after, and the homesickness was the homesickness of the things you didn't know you had: the bar of Sandro with the wine in the small glasses, the risotto of Vera with the butter that doesn't hide, the fog of January, the white of the pour. The homesickness doesn't ask permission: it arrives, and it arrives at the fire, in the mountains, in the place of the dream, and the arriving is the arriving of the truth: the things you have, you have them, and you don't have them where you are. The mountain was the mountain, and the things were the things, and I was the man of the things.

We came down in July. The coming down was the coming down of the flock and the family, and the coming down was done in the turns, and the turns gave back the country: the line of grey, the sea, the city. The friendly of July was at Derbent, and Derbent, on the map, was the sea, and the sea was where the road goes.

I didn't know that the sea, that month, had my head in it. I knew it after.
