# Chapter 7 — The Nights of the Living

**POV:** Teo · **Place:** Turin (my house, the seventh floor) · **Story time:** January–February 2024
**Words:** ~1.250 / translation of `la-vita-minore/capitoli/07.md` ✅

---

## Draft

Cohabitation never begins: it's noticed.

That's how Nora came to live with me, without either of us saying it: her things took the places of the things I didn't have — the blue toothbrush instead of the void, the t-shirt instead of the other t-shirt, the book open at half instead of the book you don't touch. A house full of two is a thing I didn't know: it smelled of coffee in the morning and of fog in the evening, and it was the first time in years that a house of mine smelled of someone.

We did the things that are done in two, and none was big. We did the Saturday shopping at Porta Palazzo, as is their habit, the things that are kept: the tomatoes of stall three, which lasted a whole week, the bread of the stall downstairs, and the flowers — the flowers we put on the kitchen windowsill, and the glass glasses we never threw away. The dinner at nine we made together, she who cuts and I who watch, as is their habit, people who have trades: they divide, and nobody asks why. The small things, in two, multiply — it's a rule nobody writes, and I write it. Two coffees aren't the double of one coffee: they're a different thing, that has no name. We called it with the hands.

We had inverted the seasons: I made my life by day, and she hers by night; at midday we crossed, the way trains cross, and the crossing lasted a coffee. Nora slept by day with the shutters lowered, and the light that made a stripe on the floor was the only thing that smelled of gold in that house. She slept with her hands outside the blanket, as is her habit: the hands of those who keep things are visible even when they sleep.

I did for her the things she did for others. I left her a dish in the lit oven, with the low flame that holds without consuming. I left her the light of the corridor, the one that at three at night in a hospital is an honour and in a house is a promise. I left her notes with the small things written on them: *the coffee is the good one*, *don't wait for the fog*. Nora said nothing. But she didn't throw the notes away: she kept them in the kitchen drawer, as is her habit, keeping things in drawers. And I knew it, and I knew she knew it. That's how love works, in its own way: it isn't said, it's put in drawers.

The nights were Nora's: she left at nine and came back at seven, and I stayed awake until I heard the key. The nights of the living, I called them. In Petersburg once there were the white nights, the ones in which it doesn't get dark; ours were the ones in which you don't sleep, and not from fear: from waiting. It's the only thing that changes, in the mourning that passes: fear becomes waiting. It isn't better. But it's kept.

The day Ivo retired was a Friday, and the seventh floor did for him the thing factories do for their own: an applause, with hands that open once in thirty years.

Ivo didn't greet anyone. He had emptied his desk the evening before — the plastic cactus, the photo of the woman, the rolls of files — and he had left everything except the headset. He took his headset — thirty years of headset, with the ears worn — and put it in my hand, without looking at me, as is his habit:

— Your turn. —

And then, with the coffee theory said for the last time:

— The ones from the machine are like us. They happen to you. —

— And the photo? — I asked. — Your woman. —

— You keep her — Ivo said, and the saying was the saying of the thirty years: the heavy things aren't said, they're kept. — She's in the drawer, at home, with the things. You keep her. —

And he went to the lift, and the lift, at the seventh floor, was the lift of the things that are kept: it took him, and the taking was the thing I saw — the taking of the man who leaves, and the leaving was the thing I kept. The seventh floor did the applause, and the applause, at the seventh floor, was the applause of the things that are kept: it was made once, in thirty years, and it was the thing I kept. I kept the headset, and the headset, at home, was on the shelf: next to the father's book, and the two things — the headset, the book — were the things of the men who kept.

The night of the retirement, Nora came back at seven, and the key, at seven, was the key of the things that are kept: it opened, and the opening was the thing I heard — the opening of the house that smelled of someone. I kept the key, and the keeping was the thing I did: the keeping of the things that are kept.

The nights of the living, in February, were the nights that didn't sleep: and the not sleeping, at thirty-three, was the thing Nora and I did instead of the saying — the saying, in February, was the thing we did with the hands: the hands that don't close. And the hands, in February, were the things I kept: they were Nora's, and the keeping was the thing I did for the first time — the keeping of the hands of someone else.
