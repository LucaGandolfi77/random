# Chapter 8 — Beauty Will Save the World

**POV:** Teo · **Place:** Turin (my house, the hospital) · **Story time:** February–March 2024
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `la-vita-minore/capitoli/08.md` ✅

---

## Draft

> "Beauty will save the world."
>
> — Fyodor Dostoevsky, *The Idiot*

True things, when they are big, are said with firm hands on the table.

Nora had known it for a week. She told me at dinner, on a Thursday, with firm hands on the table and eyes that didn't look at the dish — they looked at me, in the face, as is her habit: big things are said in the face.

— We have a small thing coming — she said.

It was the first small thing that was big. For thirty-three years the small things had been small: the coffee, the fog, the notes in the drawer. And then a small thing arrived that was everything else. I didn't say it at once. I looked at her hands — firm, as is their habit — and I understood that the fear was firm too, and that firm fear can be kept.

— A small thing — I said. — The true one. —

— The true one — Nora said.

That night we didn't sleep. Not for the mourning and not even for the fear: for the joy, which is the only thing that keeps you awake more than both, and it's its secret — nobody knows it, because those who are happy say they sleep. We lay with the light off and counted the things that are kept: a thimble, an unfinished shirt, a beat that wasn't there yet. They were enough. For the first time in years they were enough with the fear next to them, as is its habit, fear: you don't take it off, you keep it.

The first beat we heard in February, in a hospital.

The hospitals of the first beats are the hospitals of the things that are counted: the machine that counts, the screen that counts, the doctor who counts. The doctor said the thing the doctors say: — It's a boy. Everything is fine. — And the *fine*, in a hospital, is the word that counts, and the counting was the thing I did: I counted the beat, and the beat, on the machine, was the thing that was counted. The machine said 140, and the 140 were the thing I kept: the beat of the things that are kept.

Nora, in the hospital, had the hands of the things that are kept: the hands on the belly, still, and the stillness was the thing I saw. And the stillness, at thirty-three, was the thing I learned: the stillness of the things that are kept — it isn't the stillness of the fear, and it isn't the stillness of the death. It's the stillness of the things that arrive.

I told Dario the truth the week after, at dinner, and the dinner was the dinner of the things that are said: the lasagna, the baby monitor, the truth. Dario had called me in January, for the dinner, and I had said *I'm fine* for six months, and the saying of the lie was the thing the floor gave me. The truth, at dinner, was the thing I said for the first time: — No. I'm not fine. — And the saying was the thing I did: the saying of the things that are kept, said at last.

Dario didn't say anything, and the nothing was the thing Dario did: the nothing of the man who keeps. And the keeping, at thirty-three, was the thing we shared: the nothing, the truth, the lasagna. Chiara, at the baby monitor, had the baby sleeping, and the baby, at one, was the thing that didn't count: it was the thing that is kept.

— You said it — Dario said, at the door, with the shoulder in my hand — and the saying was the thing I kept: the keeping of the truth, said to the man who keeps. The truth, said to Dario, was the first truth of the year, and the year was the year of the things that are kept: the beat, the truth, the thimble.

Beauty will save the world — the books say it (Dostoevsky), and the books say it about the beauty that doesn't count. The beauty, at thirty-three, was the thing I had instead of the fear: the beat, the stillness, the hands. And the beauty, in the books, is the thing that saves, and the saving, at thirty-three, was the thing I didn't know: I had the beauty, and the beauty was the thing that was kept.

The things that are kept, in March, were the things that arrived: the beat, the truth, the shirt — the shirt that Nora was sewing, the way my mother sewed, and the sewing was the thing Nora did for the first time: the sewing of the things that arrive. And the shirt, unfinished, was on the chair, with the needle in it, and the needle was the thing my mother had: the needle of the things that are kept. And the keeping, at thirty-three, was the thing I learned: the things that arrive, you keep, and the keeping warms them. It's their secret: they're kept, and the keeping warms them — it's the thing Nora said, in the empty house, with the iron: *it's still warm*.
