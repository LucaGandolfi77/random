# Chapter 10 — The Dark of May

**POV:** Teo · **Place:** Turin (the hospital, the corridor) · **Story time:** May 2024
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `la-vita-minore/capitoli/10.md` ✅

---

## Draft

> "An hour is not merely an hour: it is a vase full of perfumes, of sounds, of projects and of climates."
>
> — Marcel Proust, *Pleasures and Days*

May arrived without knocking, as is its habit, life.

For a week it was the continuation of the last spring: the lists, the azure of the wall, the dinners at nine. Then it was at night, in May, that Nora woke — and not for the key: for the thing that hurt her below. The fabric tears, as is their habit, things: they don't tear at the right moment, and you don't ask them to wait.

We called. At two at night the city has no names for ambulances: one comes, and you follow it, and following it is the only thing you know how to do. I saw it arrive from beyond the window, with the siren off — sirens are turned off at night, as is their habit, cities: they don't wake those who aren't needed, and they wake those who are needed anyway, and the difference isn't felt. They took us to the same hospital. We didn't say it to each other: we both knew it. Hospitals keep everything, as is their trade, and this time they kept this too.

It was a birth of those that last a whole night, and I stood where I always stood: in a corridor, counting the tiles. Forty-two, up to the corner, then forty-two again — the same measure as always. The measure doesn't change, as is its habit, fear: the world changes and it doesn't change. There were the other fathers of the corridor: one who walked back and forth with his hands in his pockets, one who spoke on the phone with the voice that doesn't shake, and me. The fathers of corridors are all the same person, as is their habit, fathers: they walk, and you don't ask them to stand still, because standing still you feel the nothing.

In the end a lever in a green coat came whom I didn't know, and didn't say the thing with *don't be frightened*: she said it without anything.

— The father can come in. —

I went in. I don't tell what happened at dawn: it isn't told, it's kept. I tell only one thing, because it's the only one I can: a hand small as a thimble, which held my finger with the whole hand.

I had the thimble in my pocket, and I didn't say it. Things that are kept are kept by two, sometimes: one in the world, one in the pocket.

The hand that held my finger was the hand of the things that arrive, and the arriving, at thirty-three, was the arriving of the things you keep: you keep them, and the keeping is the thing I did — I kept the hand, and the hand was the thing that didn't count: it was the thing that is kept. And the keeping, at dawn, in a hospital, was the thing I did for the first time: the keeping of the hand of someone else — the hand small as a thimble, and the thimble was in my pocket, and the thimble was the thing my mother had: the thimble of the things that are kept.

Then it happened, and I don't tell it. It isn't told: it's kept. And the keeping, at thirty-three, is the thing I did instead of the telling — the telling, in a hospital, at dawn, is the thing you don't do: you keep. And the keeping, in May, was the thing Nora and I did: we kept, and the keeping was the thing that didn't make light — the keeping is the thing that is done in the dark, and the dark of May, at thirty-three, is the dark of the things that are kept.

May, at thirty-three, was the month of the things that are kept: the hand, the thimble, the dark. And the keeping, in May, was the thing I did instead of the living — the living, in May, wasn't there: it was crossed. The days, in May, were crossed, and the crossing was the thing I did the way you cross the things that don't count: without stopping. And the not stopping, at thirty-three, was the thing I learned: the days of May, you don't cross them — you keep them. And the keeping, in May, was the thing Nora and I did: we kept, and the keeping was the thing that didn't count.

The hand small as a thimble, at dawn, held my finger with the whole hand, and the holding was the thing I kept: I keep it still. It's the only thing of May I tell, and I tell it because it's the only one that can be told: the rest isn't told. It's kept, and the keeping is the thing that is done in the dark, and the dark of May, at thirty-three, is the dark of the things that are kept.
