# Chapter 8 — The Call

**POV:** Amadou · **Place:** Milan (the warehouse, via Padova) · **Story time:** autumn 2026
**Words:** ~1.100 / translation of `lattraversamento/capitoli/08.md` ✅

## Draft

The call came on a Friday in October, from Joal, with the time difference, at six in Milan which was five in Joal. My mother had the voice of someone who doesn't sleep every evening for her children, and the voice, on the phone, you feel the way you feel damp in walls: you don't see it. You feel it.

— Your father isn't well — my mother said. — The heart. —

The heart. My father's heart, which you don't scan: you feel it. The heart, in Joal, is the thing you don't buy: you keep it. The medicine cost fifty euros a month, and my father didn't take the medicine: it's the secret my mother said on the phone, on the Friday in October. Two years of medicine not taken, and medicine not taken, at twenty-nine, was the line that wasn't there: the line that wasn't there, at twenty-nine, was my father's margin. The sendings were three hundred euros a month, and the sendings aren't cut: you cut where it doesn't count. And the not counting, at twenty-nine, was the medicine.

I counted the medicine, in the evening, on the notebook. Excessive consciousness is a sickness (Dostoevsky), and I had been sick for four years: I did the calculation every evening, on the notebook, with the pencil. The regular work gave one thousand three hundred euros a month, and the one thousand three hundred euros divided like this: the room four hundred and fifty, the food one hundred and fifty, the phone twenty, the sending three hundred and ten. Three hundred and fifty euros were left, and the three hundred and fifty euros, at twenty-nine, were the margin. The margin, at twenty-nine, is the thing you keep: you keep it the way you keep doors that close. The medicine was fifty euros a month, and the fifty euros, added to the margin, were four hundred: and the four hundred, at twenty-nine, were the margin minus the medicine. And the margin minus the medicine, at twenty-nine, was the margin of someone who has no margin: you keep it, and you weigh it in the evening, and the weight, in the evening, you don't feel: you notice later.

My father didn't take the medicine. I write it here, because it's the thing I understood late, and I understood it two years late: my father knew, and my father didn't take it, because the sea is paid for first. The three hundred euros a month, in Joal, were more important than the medicine: my father knew it, and my father didn't say it. Heavy things aren't said: they're kept, and they're weighed in the evening, and the weight, in the evening, you don't feel: you notice later. I send the three hundred euros, and my father doesn't take the medicine: it's the reversal of the girl in the book, who ran away to save. The reversal, at twenty-nine, is that those who send don't know what they send: the sending is kept, and the medicine, instead of the sending, wasn't there. The sea is paid for first.

The work slowed in November. The warehouse cut the hours, and cut hours, in Milan, aren't asked for: they're taken. The schedule was reduced, and the reduced schedule, at the warehouse, is the schedule of those who don't choose: it's taken. One thousand three hundred euros a month became nine hundred, and the nine hundred euros, at twenty-nine, were the nine hundred euros of the girl in the book: you live and they aren't enough. The regular work isn't contested, and the reduced schedule isn't contested: it's taken.

Moussa called one Sunday in November, from Joal. Moussa was thirty, like me, and his father's pirogue, and the wife, and a child. Moussa spoke of the sea with the voice of someone who has long stopped explaining: — There's no fish — he said. — The big boats took it. I have to cross too. —

It was the sentence I had said, four years before, with the phone in my hand: *I will send three hundred euros a month*. The sentence, said by the other, sounded different, and the difference, at twenty-nine, I felt: it was the same sentence, with the fear in more, and the fear in more, at thirty, is the heaviest thing. In Joal you believe that here you earn, and here you earn, and earning, at twenty-nine, isn't the thing that holds: the thing that holds is the purpose. Moussa had the purpose: cross. I couldn't stop him: the sea is the only road, and the road, in Joal, isn't contested.

— And the pirogue? — I asked, and the pirogue is the thing you don't ask: you ask it, at twenty-nine, when the other says he crosses.

— I sold it — Moussa said. — The sea paid with the sea. —

It was my father's sentence, and it wasn't my father's sentence: it was the sentence of a man who had found it at thirty, and the discovery, on the phone, with the wife in his hand, felt like the truest thing of the Sunday. The sea paid with the sea, at twenty-nine, is the reversal of the girl in the book: she ran away to save; Moussa left because the sea didn't give anything anymore. And nature, in November, doesn't deliver what it promises then (Leopardi, *A Silvia*): the sea that promised fish didn't give it anymore, and nature, at twenty-nine, is the thing that isn't contested: it's crossed.

My father, in Joal, was a man like any other, living as he should (Tolstoy): thirty years of sea, one trade, and the sea, in the evening, erased itself and came back. A man like any other, in books, dies on the bed of an apartment he has paid for all his life. My father didn't die on the bed of an apartment: he died at home, with the sea at the window, and the sea, in Joal, isn't an apartment: it's home.

In the evening, coming back from the warehouse, I looked at the sheet. The sheet was folded in four, and the sheet said *provisional*, and the renewal was pending. The sheet pending, in Milan, isn't contested: it's awaited.

And waiting, at twenty-nine, is the longest thing in the world.
