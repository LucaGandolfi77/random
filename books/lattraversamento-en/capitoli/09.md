# Chapter 9 — The Pending Sheet

**POV:** Amadou · **Place:** Milan (via Padova, the warehouse, the Questura) · **Story time:** November 2026
**Words:** ~1.050 / translation of `lattraversamento/capitoli/09.md` ✅

## Draft

The renewal was pending from November, and the pending sheet is the sheet that isn't contested: it's awaited. The rule, at the Questura, is the rule of always: the sheet pending renewal doesn't allow you to travel. Those who have a pending sheet, and have to go back, don't go back: it's the rule, and the rule, at twenty-nine, is the thing you don't negotiate with anger. I made the anger, the first time, at the Questura: I said *my father is ill*, and I said *why?*, and the man behind the glass didn't laugh, and the not laughing, at the Questura, is the heaviest thing: you feel that the other knows, and the other doesn't say. Heavy things aren't said: they're kept.

My father got worse in the second week of November. My mother called, and my mother had the voice of someone who doesn't sleep every evening for her children, and the voice, on the phone, you don't see: you feel it. — Your father isn't well — she said. — The heart. —

The heart. My father's heart, which you don't scan: you feel it. The medicine was fifty euros a month, and my father hadn't taken the medicine for two years: the sea is paid for first. I knew it from October, and only I knew it. The margin was three hundred and fifty euros a month, and the margin, at twenty-nine, is the thing you keep: you keep it and you don't touch it. The medicine, instead of the margin, wasn't there: the sea is paid for first. I knew it, and my father knew it, and my father didn't say it: heavy things aren't said.

My father died on the Sunday, at home, with the sea at the window. My father was fifty-eight, thirty years of sea, and the pirogue sold, and the house of bricks and tin that cost nothing. A man like any other, living as he should (Tolstoy): the sea, the fish, the house. A man like any other, in books, dies on the bed of an apartment he has paid for all his life. My father died at home, and the home cost nothing: and it cost everything else. My father, the first four years, never asked me to come back: the *inshallah*, on Sunday, was the answer to everything, and the answer to everything answers nothing. But my father, the Sunday before he died, said the thing he never said:

— Come back — my mother said, who reported it on the phone, with the voice of someone who says heavy things lightly. — He says: come back. —

It was the heaviest thing of the evening, and the heavy thing, on the phone, isn't asked: it's listened to, and it stays. Come back. The word, at twenty-nine, was the word you don't buy: you receive it. And I received it on Sunday, and Sunday was the day my father said heavy things lightly.

I made the call on Monday, from Milan, to my mother. My mother answered, and my mother didn't cry: heavy things aren't said on the phone, they're kept. — Inshallah — I said, and the *inshallah* was the word I had learned from my father, and the word, said at home, was the heaviest thing in the world: you feel that the other knows, and the other doesn't say.

The funeral was on Friday. The funeral, in Joal, is the thing you do: you do it with the village, with the sea, with hands. I didn't go. The pending sheet didn't let me leave: it's the rule, and the rule, at twenty-nine, is the thing you don't negotiate with anger. I asked at the Questura, in the queue, on Thursday: I said *my father is dead*, and I said *the funeral is tomorrow*, and the man behind the glass looked at the sheet, and the sheet said *pending renewal*, and the man said nothing. The unsaid, at the Questura, is the heaviest thing: you feel that the other knows, and the other doesn't say. Heavy things aren't said: they're kept.

The ticket to Dakar cost seven hundred euros. Seven hundred euros, at twenty-nine, were the margin plus the margin: and the margin, at twenty-nine, was the ticket that isn't bought. The pending sheet, anyway, didn't let me leave: the rule. Seven hundred euros, and the rule: the two things together, at twenty-nine, are the funeral that isn't done.

The days melted. Not the days of always, which are crossed (Proust): the days of November, which are kept. The days, at twenty-nine, are crossed when there's nothing to do; the days of November, at twenty-nine, are kept because there's nothing to say. Habit, which anaesthetises the days one by one (Proust), in November anaesthetised nothing: November was felt, and November, at twenty-nine, is the heaviest November in the world.

The work, in November, didn't slow: it continued. The scans, at the warehouse, were all done, and the scans, at twenty-nine, are the scans of someone who has nothing to say: they're done. Bakary, at the warehouse, in the evening, didn't ask anything. Heavy things aren't asked, and the heavy things of brothers of the road are the heavy things that aren't asked: they're listened to, and they stay.

In the evening, coming back to via Padova, I looked at the fog. The fog of Milan, at twenty-nine, doesn't come back: it stays. In Joal the sea erased itself every evening, and every evening it came back: it's the law of the sea. The fog isn't the sea, and November isn't the evening, and the question, at twenty-nine, formed by itself, the way true things form: all at once, and without being asked.

Was it better to stay? The question, at twenty-nine, I didn't ask anyone. I asked it to myself, in the evening, with the fog at the window, and the question, in the evening, answers nothing.

The phone rang on Friday, at noon in Milan which was one in the afternoon in Joal. It was Moussa, and the phone, on Friday, was the funeral.
