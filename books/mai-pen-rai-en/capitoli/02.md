# Chapter 2 — The Thirty Per Cent

**POV:** Ludovica · **Place:** Milan (Mercurio, via Borsieri) · **Story time:** September 2025, three weeks
**Words:** ~1.350 / translation of `mai-pen-rai/capitoli/02.md` ✅

---

## Draft

> "Excessive consciousness is a disease, a real, thoroughgoing disease."
>
> — Fyodor Dostoevsky, *Notes from Underground*

That was the month of the thirty per cent campaign. IperRisparmio had decided to lower everything, and we lowered everything on paper: the prices, the tags, the photos. I spent my days writing *-30%* in big letters, and the thirty per cent was the number of the month: I wrote it on presentations, on flyers, on stickers. The thirty per cent, written enough times, looks like a trade.

I had written the concept on a Tuesday, at my desk, at seven in the evening, when the office was empty and the extra hours didn't get billed: it was taken for granted. It had come all at once, the way true things come: I hadn't thought it, I had written it. It said that people don't want low prices: they want the pleasure of low prices, and pleasure isn't calculated, it's shown. I had written *-30%* next to a roast chicken, and below, in small, *the pleasure of paying less*. To myself, that evening, I said nothing. But I had written *the pleasure of paying less*, and the sentence, reread the next day, seemed written by someone else.

At the client meeting there was the thing I no longer tell anyone, because by now I tell it only here. The room was the usual one: the long table, the seats, the projector. The client had said he liked the campaign, that the concept was strong, that they had finally understood people. The concept was mine. I had written it at seven in the evening, and at seven in the evening there was nobody: so it had been born from the team. And the team was Renzo. Renzo presented it with his voice, the voice of someone who presents, entering the room the way you take things: without asking. The client said *well done everyone*, and the *well done everyone* was for Renzo, and Renzo didn't correct it. Nobody ever corrected it. In Milan credits, like rents, go up every month: it's the thing you don't discuss.

That same week there was the budget meeting. The numbers were mine, I made them, and the numbers said that to do the thirty per cent you had to cut. We cut where we could: you always cut where you can, and it's where it doesn't count. The thing that got cut, that week, was me: they hired an intern at six hundred euros a month, and the intern would do my part. They told her to sit next to me, and for two weeks I taught her the trade they were taking from me: the papers, the tags, the lists. The intern was called Giorgia, she was twenty-two, and she studied communication, like I had studied. She was me, twenty-two years before, at the price of six hundred euros. I cost one thousand four hundred and eighty. The difference was seven hundred and eighty euros a month, and the difference, in the budget, was a saving.

I trained my own replacement. I say it like this, because it's like this: I told her where the files were, how you make yellow tags, what the client wants. She said *thank you*, and the *thank you* was sincere, and it was the cruellest thing: the sincerity of people who are taking your place without knowing it. The third week Giorgia made the tags alone, and Giorgia's tags were mine, made by someone else, at someone else's price. In Milan people go down in price too, and nobody writes it on the flyer.

It was on a Friday, in that state of things — the stolen concept, the trained replacement, the unbilled extra hours — that the email came. The email was from the building administrator, and it said that from January the rent would go up by one hundred and twenty euros. One hundred and twenty euros a month, for the same studio as always, with the same window onto the inner courtyard. In Milan rents go up like credits: it's the same thing, done to people. And the market values of the neighbourhood, read twice, seemed to me the values of a merchant: Mercury again, fixing things without asking.

That evening I did the shopping at the supermarket downstairs, and the receipt said double. Not double of nothing: double of two years before. The same shopping, the same products, double. I looked at the receipt for a minute, the way you look at rare things, and the receipt was a rarest thing: it was the proof. I kept it. It's still here, in a pocket of a notebook, and I keep it the way you keep proofs: you don't use them, you show them.

That night I did the calculation. Excessive consciousness is a disease (Dostoevsky), and I had been sick for years: I did the calculation every evening, on the apps, on the receipts, on the flyers. That night I did it for a different reason: not to know how much was missing, but to know how much was left. Thirteen thousand five hundred euros were left. Thirteen thousand five hundred euros put aside in five years, living the reduced version of myself: the gym suspended, the discount shopping, dinners at home, presents made with reason. Thirteen thousand five hundred euros: at nine hundred and twenty euros a month — the new rent, life over there — that was almost fifteen months. Almost fifteen months of life, at flyer prices.

It seemed a better deal. In Milan the deal was: you work to pay, and they leave you alone. There the deal would have been: you don't work, and you're fine. Choosing between two deals isn't freedom: it's changing masters. But in the moment I did it, it seemed like freedom. I didn't say it to myself with these words: I said it with the app, booking the flight.

I handed in my resignation on a Friday, the way you hand in resignations: with the closed envelope and the hand that doesn't shake. The letter said I was leaving for "personal reasons", and personal reasons, in resignation letters, are the reasons you can't say: the stolen concept, the trained replacement, the hundred and twenty euros, the receipt. Renzo said it was a shame, that I had potential, that if I wanted to... He said the things people say, and none of them were true. Giorgia said *good luck*, and her *good luck* was sincere, and it was the cruellest thing of the day.

The friends' reactions all came, and they were all different. Martina said *are you sure?*, and repeated it three times, the way you repeat the things you don't understand: at twenty-nine, leaving a job that pays without having another, usually, isn't done. Sara said *but the CV?*, and she was right: a CV that stops at twenty-nine is a CV you don't take up again — I knew it, and she knew it, and neither of us said the rest. Giulia said *what will you do?*, and the question was the true one: in Berlin she had a job and a plan, and escaping, she said, is done well when you have a job and a plan. I had neither. I had thirteen thousand five hundred euros and a flight.

Happy lives, on stories, are all alike (Tolstoj — Tolstoy): the gym at six, the Sunday brunch, the dog, the sunset seen from a terrace that rents at a high price. Mine resembled nobody's: there was the sea over there, and there was nothing of mine. In Recanati, once, a boy climbed a hill on a Village Saturday (Leopardi — *Il sabato del villaggio*) and happiness was in the waiting: the Saturday that aims, the week that aims at Saturday. My friends' lives all aimed: at the mortgage, at the exam, at the contract. Mine aimed at nowhere anymore. It seemed like a victory.

There is, though, one thing I must say here, because it's the thing I understood late, and I understood it seven months late: a man, in books, did the most stupid things on purpose, just to prove to himself he was free (Dostoevsky, *Notes from Underground*). I resigned on purpose. Not for a better job, not for a love, not for a plan: to prove to myself I could. It seemed like a victory. It seemed like a victory for three weeks, which is the time it takes to understand that victories without an opponent aren't won: they're given, and they stay in your hand.

The flight left on the twelfth of October, from Malpensa. I still had six months to turn thirty. I would turn thirty in Thailand, but I didn't know that: only the flight knew it, and the flight says nothing.
