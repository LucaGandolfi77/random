# Chapter 8 — The Burning Season

**POV:** Ludovica · **Place:** Chiang Mai (Hang Dong, Marcello's bar) · **Story time:** March 2026
**Words:** ~1.350 / translation of `mai-pen-rai/capitoli/08.md` ✅

---

## Draft

> "O nature, o nature, / why do you not deliver / what you promise then? why do you deceive / your children so?"
>
> — Giacomo Leopardi, *A Silvia*

In March the smoke came. Not smoke of nothing: the smoke of the burnt fields, that comes from the mountains and stays, and the smog, in March, in Chiang Mai, is the season that doesn't say its name: it's called the burning season, and those who live it call it smoke. The air quality app said one hundred and eighty, and one hundred and eighty, in Italy, I had never seen: in Milan the smog said one hundred, and one hundred, in Milan, is the smog of the neighbourhoods you don't choose. There the smog said one hundred and eighty, and the mountains, which the smoke always excludes, disappear (Leopardi, *L'infinito*): the mountains were there, behind the smoke, and the smoke didn't let you see them, and the things you don't see, in March, are the things you lose.

The postcard paradise didn't come. O nature, why do you not deliver what you promise then? (Leopardi, *A Silvia*): nature, in March, doesn't deliver the blue sky of the postcards, and the postcards promise, and nature's promises, in March, are the promises that aren't kept. I had bought paradise for two hundred and fifty euros a month, with the pool, and paradise, in March, was a grey sky with the smell of burning. The grey sky, in Milan, I knew it: it was the sky of Mondays. There it was the sky of every day, and the sky of every day, without the Monday, is a sky you don't know: you put up with it.

The budget, in March, said nine thousand two hundred euros. Nine thousand two hundred euros put aside, and the thirteen thousand five hundred of September had gone down, and the going down, in March, you don't notice: you notice later, and later is made of receipts. Saving was my new job, and that one didn't pay me either: it was the job I did every evening, on the apps, on the receipts, and the unpaid job, in March, felt like the most mine thing. It wasn't the most mine thing: it was the thing you don't live. You count. And the accounts, in March, are the accounts of someone who does nothing: you count because there's nothing else to count.

I had let the insurance lapse, and I knew it, and I didn't say it to anyone. Thirty-nine euros a month, and the saving, in March, felt like the wisest thing in the world. Saving, as always, you notice later: and later, in March, was the later of someone who has the accounts in her hand and not the life. Only I knew it. The things you know and don't say, in March, are the things you weigh: you weigh them every evening, and the weight, in March, you don't feel: you notice later.

Ploy called one afternoon in March, from Bangkok. Ploy was nineteen, was Patcha's daughter, and studied commerce at university, and the university, in Bangkok, was the thing Patcha paid on her feet from five in the morning. Ploy spoke a flat Thai English, like her mother, and said the sentences all lightly, and the light sentences, in March, were the sentences you hear:

— I want to go to Milan — she said. — In Milan you earn. I have to build something. —

It was the sentence I had said, two years before, at sea, with the phone in my hand: *there it's cheap to live*. The sentence, said by the other, sounded different, and the difference, in March, I felt: it was the same sentence, with the hope in more, and the hope in more, at nineteen, is the richest thing. She didn't know it. At nineteen you believe that there you earn, and there you earn, and earning, in March, isn't the thing that holds: the thing that holds is the purpose. Ploy had the purpose: build something. I didn't have it anymore: I had left it in Milan, with the thirty per cent, and the thirty per cent, in March, felt like the thirty per cent of someone who wasn't there anymore.

— And in Milan what would you do? — I asked, and the question was mine: in Milan, at twenty-nine, I hadn't asked it to myself.

— Work — Ploy said, and the word was simple, and the simple words, in March, are the words you don't say. — Work, and learn. And send money to my mother. —

Send money to my mother. The university, in Bangkok, was the thing Patcha paid on her feet from five in the morning, and Ploy, at nineteen, wanted to do the same thing the other way round: send. In Milan, at twenty-nine, I sent the money to my master, and it was called rent. It felt like a true thing, said by Ploy, and the truth, at nineteen, is the thing you don't weigh: you say.

I met Marcello at the bar, in the evening, for the first time. The bar was the usual one, with the Chang beer, and Marcello was sixty-two, and Livorno, he said, was his country: — I've been running away for thirty years — he said, without my asking. — You know what I found out? That I carry the same stuff with me, always. —

The same stuff. I asked which. — My evenings — he said. — My empty evenings. In Livorno they had a shape: the shape was the factory, was the wife, was the bar. Here they have no shape. And evenings without shape, my friend, are the longest evenings in the world. — Marcello had thirty years of Thailand, and Thailand, for thirty years, was the thing you don't say: you live. And life, for thirty years, without a purpose, was Marcello's life: the same stuff, renewed in instalments, like the stamps. — Nobody runs away from their own life — he said. — And those who run without a why, run towards it. —

It was the sentence of the book, and it wasn't the book: it was the sentence of a man who had found it in thirty years, and the discovery, at the bar, with the Chang beer in his hand, felt like the truest thing of the evening. A man, in books, did the most stupid things on purpose to prove he was free (Dostoevskij — Dostoevsky, *Notes from Underground*): Marcello had done it for thirty years, and hadn't proved it. Freedom isn't proved: you have it, or you don't, and you have it with the things you keep, and the things I kept, in March, were mine: the Thai notebook, the guesthouse photos, the mango at double minus a little.

— And you? — Marcello said. — How old are you? —

— Thirty — I said. — I turn thirty in April. —

— Thirty — Marcello said. — I was thirty-two when I came. You know what I had? A job. And the job, when it's there, you don't feel it. When it isn't, you feel it every evening. —

The factory was a cage, I said in Milan. I still say it, but I say it now with Marcello's sentence in my hand: the factory was a cage, but it was a factory. Inside there was a purpose, and the purpose, in March, is the thing that's missing. My father, in Cesano Maderno, had forty years of the same factory, and didn't complain: he complained, but he knew why he worked. And someone who knows why he works, in March, doesn't feel the evenings: the evenings are the garden's, and the garden is a purpose.

I went home in the white air, with the red scooter, and the street didn't end, and the street, in March, felt like the longest street. For the first time in seven months I did the calculation I didn't want to do: how many days were left. Fifty-two days were left, and fifty-two days, with nine thousand two hundred euros in hand, were the days of a life, not the days of a holiday.

The difference, in March, I felt. And I felt it the way you feel damp in walls: you don't see it. You feel it.
