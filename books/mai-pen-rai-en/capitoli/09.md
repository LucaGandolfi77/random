# Chapter 9 — Other People's Water

**POV:** Ludovica · **Place:** Chiang Mai (the Songkran, Hang Dong) · **Story time:** April 2026
**Words:** ~1.150 / translation of `mai-pen-rai/capitoli/09.md` ✅

## Draft

The Songkran comes in mid-April, and it's the Thai new year, and the Thai new year isn't celebrated: it's done. It's done with water, and the water is everywhere: in the buckets, in the water guns, in the trucks that pass with full barrels, and the city, for three days, is a city in the water, and other people's water, for three days, touches you the way things touch you: without asking. In Milan other people's water is the rain, and the rain, in Milan, is the thing you don't choose: you put up with it, under the porticoes, with the phone in your hand. There other people's water was a feast, and the feast, for three days, was everyone's. The water was cold, and the cold, in April, is the cold you don't expect: you put up with it, the way you put up with other people's happiness.

I turned thirty on the fourteenth of April, and the fourteenth of April was the second day of the Songkran. I turned thirty in other people's water, and I didn't say it to anyone: I said it only to the app, and the app says nothing. In Milan birthdays are the things you celebrate: you celebrate them with friends, with the cake, with the evening that aims at the birthday. My evening aimed at nowhere anymore, and the birthday, in other people's water, is a birthday that doesn't aim: it's crossed.

The stories, that day, were full. Martina had made the party for a colleague's thirtieth, and the party was full of things you put: the cake, the balloons, the people. The empty pool, on stories, isn't put, and the full party, on stories, is always put. Sara had sent an invitation: *save the date*, June, the wedding in Ostia, and the invitation, on the phone, had arrived with email, and email isn't an invitation: it's an announcement. Announcements, at thirty, are the things you receive, and you receive them from afar, and distance, at thirty, is a thing you don't feel: you feel it later.

Giulia had done nothing, and Giulia's nothing, on stories, was the nothing of those who don't put: it's the truest thing. In Berlin there was no Songkran, and the Songkran, on stories, was mine: I didn't put it. The void isn't put on stories, and the birthday in other people's water, on stories, isn't put: it's the thing you don't photograph. Nobody photographs it.

Excessive consciousness is a disease (Dostoevskij — Dostoevsky), and I had been sick for seven months, and the disease, in April, had risen: I counted the budget app hourly, and the counted hours, in April, were the hours of a sick woman. Nine thousand euros, and the nine thousand euros were counted the way you count days: backwards, and without mercy. In Milan I counted the billed hours, and the billed hours were a trade; there I counted the baht, and counted baht aren't a trade: they're a disease. The disease, in April, isn't said: it's counted.

And while all the people celebrate in the streets (Leopardi — *Il sabato del villaggio*), I looked from the water. I didn't look from the water by choice: I looked from the water because other people's water isn't chosen, and the feast of others, at thirty, is a feast you don't live: you cross it. The days, in April, weren't lived: they were crossed (Proust), and crossing, at thirty, is the thing you feel in your legs: you don't feel it. You notice later, and later, in April, was made of evenings.

The mango cake Patcha brought on the evening of the fourteenth. She didn't ask: she arrived with the cake, and the cake was of mango and sticky rice, with a candle, and the candle, in the guesthouse, was the smallest thing. — Happy birthday — she said, with her voice, and the *happy birthday* was true, and it was the truest thing of the evening: it was made with hands, and hands, in the evening, are the hands you see. I blew out the candle, and the wish I had I didn't say to myself: the wish, at thirty, in other people's water, isn't said. It's kept. I still keep it.

Ploy called from Bangkok, and Ploy's *happy birthday* was light, like the light sentences, and lightness, at nineteen, is the richest thing. — Come to Milan — Ploy said, laughing, and the sentence, at thirty, isn't a joke: it's a question. I didn't answer. The questions you don't answer, in April, are the questions you keep: you keep them under things, and things, in April, are weighed.

The Songkran ended on the fifteenth, and the city came out of the water, and the water stayed in the streets, and the streets, on the sixteenth, were dry, and dry, after water, is the thing you feel: you feel it the way you feel the void after the party. The void after the party, at thirty, is the truest void: the party ends, and the void stays, and the void isn't put on stories.

On the evening of the sixteenth, the fever came. I thought it was the heat. It wasn't the heat.
