# Chapter 12 — The Sky of Austerlitz

**POV:** Ludovica · **Place:** Chiang Mai → Malpensa → Milan · **Story time:** late May 2026
**Words:** ~1.350 / translation of `mai-pen-rai/capitoli/12.md` ✅

---

## Draft

> "How could I not have seen that lofty sky before?"
>
> — Leo Tolstoy, *War and Peace*

The last days were the days you count, and the days you count, in May, are the days you don't live: you cross them (Proust). I returned the condo, and the condominium returned the deposit, and the deposit, in May, is the thing you counted: it went away, the way counted things go. I returned the scooter, and the red scooter I returned with the full tank, and the full tank, in May, is the thing you give: you give so as not to owe anything. The red scooter, in the morning, felt like the most mine thing: it wasn't mine. It was rented, like the stamp, like life. Rented things, in May, you return, and returning is the thing you do: you do it so as not to owe anything. In Milan I owed nine hundred euros a month, and the nine hundred euros, in May, were the price of meaning.

I said goodbye to Patcha in the evening, on the step, and the step was the usual one, and Patcha's smile was the usual one. She didn't say *mai pen rai*: it was the thing I had learned in seven months, and I had learned it late. For big things it isn't said: it's done. Patcha gave me the cooking notebook, and the notebook was handwritten, in Thai, and the recipes were Patcha's: the morning soup, the sticky rice, the pad thai. The recipes, in Thai, I couldn't read all of them: I could read half, and the half I couldn't read, in May, was the half you do by hand. Things you do by hand, in May, are the things that stay: you keep them.

— For the kitchen — Patcha said, with her voice, and her voice, in the evening, was the voice that says heavy things lightly. — You cook. In Milan. —

— In Milan — I said.

It was the truest thing she had said to me in seven months, and the thing, said by Patcha, felt like the heaviest: it was the kitchen speaking, and the kitchen, on the phone, says nothing: it says *cook*, and *cook*, in the morning, you don't take off: you keep it.

— And if I can't read half the recipes? — I said. — What do I do? —

— What everyone does — Patcha said. — Cook, and taste. The kitchen isn't read: it's tasted. —

And the sentence was the truest sentence of the evening, and the evening, on the step, with the notebook in my hand, felt like the thing you keep: the things you keep, in the evening, you don't count.

Ploy said goodbye on the phone, from Bangkok, and Ploy's *see you in Milan* was light, like the light sentences, and lightness, at nineteen, is the richest thing. — See you in Milan — Ploy said, laughing, and the sentence, at nineteen, isn't a joke: it's a hope. Hope, at nineteen, you keep, and you keep it for years: it's the thing you don't buy.

I did the last market in the morning, and the mango I bought from the mango woman. The mango woman recognised me, and she didn't recognise me by my face: she recognised me by the tone. I said *mai* with the right tone, and the right tone, in the morning, was the tone that has no way: it's the tone you say without thinking. — New — I said, and *new* was right, and the woman laughed, and the laugh was the usual one, and the mango cost the right price. The right price, in the morning, felt like the richest thing in the country: it wasn't rich. It was the thing you give to those who stay, and I wasn't someone who stays: I was someone who comes back. And to those who come back, in May, you give things: the right price, the mango, the *new*. *New* was the thing I carried: I didn't know it. *New*, in Thai, is the word you say to those who come back.

I watched the last sunset from the wall of the old city, in the evening, and the sunset was beautiful, and the sun set at half past six, and the sky was high. At Austerlitz, once, a wounded prince, on a lost battle, looked at the high sky and found it beautiful (Tolstoj — Tolstoy, *War and Peace*). Not because the sky was beautiful: because underneath there was a battle. The sky without a battle you don't look at: you cross it. The sky of Chiang Mai, in May, was beautiful, and I looked at it, and the sky told me nothing: there was no battle. The battle, in May, was in Milan, and Milan, in May, felt like the richest thing: it wasn't rich. It was the thing you fight, and it's its secret: the job, the M5, the nine hundred euros, the billed hours. The battle isn't beautiful. It's a battle. And battles, in May, are fought, and those who fight them, in the evening, look at the high sky and find it beautiful. I had no battle anymore, and the sky, in May, was beautiful and told me nothing.

The flight left on the evening of the twenty-ninth of May, from Chiang Mai, and Bangkok arrived at night, and the return flight took eleven hours. The descent onto Malpensa I saw from the window, and the descent, in the morning, was the descent into the fog. The fog of Milan had been with me every morning for five years, and I had left it in October, and I hadn't felt it missing: I felt it only then, and I felt it the way you feel true things. All at once, and without being asked. The lost paradise, the books say, is the paradise that we have lost (Proust), and my paradise had the smell of the fog: I knew it only then. The fog, in the morning, was the most beautiful thing I had ever seen. It was grey, and it wasn't rich, and it was the thing you don't choose: you put up with it. And the things you don't choose, in the morning, are the things that keep you.

The taxi took me to Isola, and Isola took me home, and home was the usual one: the studio on via Borsieri, with the window onto the inner courtyard. It cost nine hundred euros a month, and the nine hundred euros, in the morning, were the price of meaning. The price of meaning, in the morning, you don't count: you pay. I paid it, and I paid it gladly, and the gladly, in the morning, was the richest thing I had: I didn't know it.

At home there was the shelf, and on the shelf there was the Zibaldone, and next to the Zibaldone I put the cooking notebook. The things of the places you go to, you keep next to the things of the places you come back to.
