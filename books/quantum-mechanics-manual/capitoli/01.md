# Chapter 1 — The Hilbert Space: States, Operators, and Measurement

**Level:** Introductory — Rigorously Mathematical  
**Prerequisites:** Linear algebra, calculus, elementary probability  
**Objective:** To construct the mathematical framework of quantum mechanics — Hilbert spaces, linear operators, spectral theory, and the postulates of measurement — with complete derivations and explicit references to the physical interpretation of each mathematical object.

---

## 1.1 The State Space: A Complex Hilbert Space

The state of a quantum system is represented by a vector \( |\psi\rangle \) in a complex Hilbert space \( \mathcal{H} \). The space is complete (Cauchy sequences converge) and separable (there exists a countable orthonormal basis \( \{|n\rangle\} \)). The inner product is linear in the second argument and antilinear in the first:

\[
\langle \phi | \psi \rangle = \int d\tau \, \phi^*(\tau) \psi(\tau)
\]

where \( d\tau \) is the appropriate measure (discrete sum for spin, continuous integral for position). The norm is \( \|\psi\| = \sqrt{\langle \psi | \psi \rangle} \). Normalization requires \( \|\psi\| = 1 \), which is not a physical constraint but a convention that allows the interpretation of \( |\langle \tau | \psi \rangle|^2 \) as a probability density.

The completeness relation for an orthonormal basis \( \{|n\rangle\} \) is:

\[
\sum_{n} |n\rangle \langle n| = \mathbb{I}
\]

In the continuous position representation, this becomes:

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |x\rangle \langle x| \, dx = \mathbb{I}
\]

with \( \langle x | x' \rangle = \delta(x - x') \). The wave function is \( \psi(x) = \langle x | \psi \rangle \). The probability density is \( \rho(x) = |\psi(x)|^2 \), and the expectation value of an observable \( \hat{A} \) is:

\[
\langle \hat{A} \rangle = \langle \psi | \hat{A} | \psi \rangle = \int d\tau \, \psi^*(\tau) (\hat{A} \psi)(\tau)
\]

## 1.2 Linear Operators: Self-Adjointness and the Spectral Theorem

An observable is represented by a linear operator \( \hat{A} \) that is self-adjoint (Hermitian): \( \hat{A} = \hat{A}^\dagger \). This condition ensures that the eigenvalues are real, which is necessary for the physical interpretation of measurement results.

The eigenvalue equation is:

\[
\hat{A} |a_n\rangle = a_n |a_n\rangle
\]

where \( a_n \in \mathbb{R} \). The spectral theorem states that any self-adjoint operator can be decomposed as:

\[
\hat{A} = \sum_{n} a_n |a_n\rangle \langle a_n| + \int d\alpha \, a(\alpha) |a(\alpha)\rangle \langle a(\alpha)|
\]

The discrete part corresponds to bound states (quantized energies, discrete angular momenta). The continuous part corresponds to scattering states (continuous spectra, unbound particles). The completeness relation is:

\[
\sum_{n} |a_n\rangle \langle a_n| + \int d\alpha \, |a(\alpha)\rangle \langle a(\alpha)| = \mathbb{I}
\]

This decomposition is not an approximation. It is exact. It demonstrates that any observable measurement can be decomposed into a sum (or integral) of projections onto eigenstates, weighted by the eigenvalues.

## 1.3 The Measurement Postulate: Projection and Probability

The measurement postulate (von Neumann, 1932) states that if the system is in state \( |\psi\rangle \), the probability of obtaining the eigenvalue \( a_n \) is:

\[
P(a_n) = |\langle a_n | \psi \rangle|^2
\]

and the state after measurement collapses to:

\[
|\psi\rangle \xrightarrow{\mathrm{measurement}} |a_n\rangle
\]

This is not a physical process. It is a mathematical rule that connects the pre-measurement state to the post-measurement state. The collapse is not dynamical (it does not follow from the Schrödinger equation). It is an update of information. The claim that the wave function collapses is the claim that the observer's knowledge changes — not that the physical world jumps discontinuously.

The expectation value can also be written using the density operator:

\[
\hat{\rho} = |\psi\rangle \langle \psi|
\]

Then \( \langle \hat{A} \rangle = \mathrm{Tr}(\hat{\rho} \hat{A}) \). For a mixed state (statistical ensemble of pure states \( \{|\psi_k\rangle\} \) with probabilities \( w_k \)), the density operator is:

\[
\hat{\rho} = \sum_{k} w_k |\psi_k\rangle \langle \psi_k|
\]

This formalism is essential for describing systems that are not in pure states — thermal ensembles, open quantum systems, and systems with incomplete information.

## 1.4 The Uncertainty Principle: A Mathematical Derivation

The Heisenberg uncertainty relation is not a statement about measurement error. It is a statement about the fundamental structure of the Hilbert space. For any two self-adjoint operators \( \hat{A} \) and \( \hat{B} \) that do not commute, define:

\[
\Delta A = \sqrt{\langle \hat{A}^2 \rangle - \langle \hat{A} \rangle^2}, \quad \Delta B = \sqrt{\langle \hat{B}^2 \rangle - \langle \hat{B} \rangle^2}
\]

Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the state \( |\phi\rangle = (\hat{A} - \langle \hat{A} \rangle) |\psi\rangle \), one derives:

\[
(\Delta A)^2 (\Delta B)^2 \geq \frac{1}{4} |\langle [\hat{A}, \hat{B}] \rangle|^2
\]

For position and momentum, \( [\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar \), yielding:

\[
\Delta x \Delta p \geq \frac{\hbar}{2}
\]

This is not an approximation. It is an exact lower bound that holds for all states \( |\psi\rangle \). It demonstrates that the claim of quantum mechanics is not that we cannot measure position and momentum precisely. It is that the concepts of precise position and precise momentum are mutually exclusive in the structure of the Hilbert space.

## 1.5 The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation: Derivation from Unitarity

The time evolution of a quantum state must be linear (superposition principle) and must preserve normalization (unitarity). The only linear, unitary evolution law is:

\[
i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} |\psi(t)\rangle = \hat{H} |\psi(t)\rangle
\]

where \( \hat{H} \) is a self-adjoint operator (the Hamiltonian). This is derived by requiring that the norm \( \langle \psi(t) | \psi(t) \rangle \) is constant:

\[
\frac{d}{dt} \langle \psi | \psi \rangle = \left\langle \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} \bigg| \psi \right\rangle + \left\langle \psi \bigg| \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} \right\rangle = 0
\]

Substituting \( \frac{\partial}{\partial t} |\psi\rangle = \hat{U} |\psi\rangle \) and requiring unitarity \( \hat{U}^\dagger \hat{U} = \mathbb{I} \) yields \( \hat{U} = -i\hat{H}/\hbar \) with \( \hat{H} = \hat{H}^\dagger \).

The solution is:

\[
|\psi(t)\rangle = \hat{U}(t, t_0) |\psi(t_0)\rangle = e^{-i\hat{H}(t - t_0)/\hbar} |\psi(t_0)\rangle
\]

This is the time-evolution operator. It is unitary, it preserves probability, and it connects the state at one time to the state at any other time. The claim is not that the state jumps. The claim is that the state evolves continuously, guided by the Hamiltonian, and that this evolution is the deepest form of memory — the memory of the initial conditions, folded into the present state.

## 1.7 Literary Reflection: Proust and the Wave Function — Memory as Superposition

Proust writes that memory is not retrieval; it is the unfolding of a page that was always folded. The wave function is the same: it is not a description of the particle's position at a single point. It is the description of all possible positions, folded into a single mathematical object — the superposition. When the observer measures the position, the wave function does not jump. It unfolds — the superposition collapses to a single value, not by force, but by the act of observation, the act of reading.

The claim of Proust is that the past is hidden in the things we keep without knowing. The claim of quantum mechanics is that the future is hidden in the states we measure without knowing. Both claims are settled not by solving them, but by reading them — with patience, with attention, with the recognition that the claim is not false, but only folded, and that the observer's role is not to solve the claim, but to unfold it, to keep it open, to become the reader that remembers it.

And so the wave function is not a mathematical abstraction. It is the madeleine of physics — the taste of all possible worlds, folded into one, unfolding into the measurement, remembered not as a single value, but as the claim that holds all values, all possibilities, all memories, in the quiet patience of the Hilbert space.
