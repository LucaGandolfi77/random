# Chapter 2 — The Schrödinger Equation: Derivation, First Solutions, and the Memory of Time

**Level:** Intermediate — With Full Derivations  
**Objective:** To derive the time-dependent and time-independent Schrödinger equations from the unitarity of evolution, to solve them for the infinite square well and the harmonic oscillator, and to connect the solutions to the physical interpretation of energy quantization.

---

## 2.1 The Time-Dependent Schrödinger Equation: From Unitarity to Dynamics

The time-evolution operator \( \hat{U}(t, t_0) \) must be unitary and linear. For an infinitesimal time interval \( dt \), we write:

\[
\hat{U}(t + dt, t) = \mathbb{I} - \frac{i}{\hbar} \hat{H}(t) dt + \mathcal{O}(dt^2)
\]

Taking the limit \( dt \to 0 \) yields the differential form:

\[
i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \hat{U}(t, t_0) = \hat{H}(t) \hat{U}(t, t_0)
\]

Applying this to the state vector \( |\psi(t)\rangle = \hat{U}(t, t_0) |\psi(t_0)\rangle \) gives the time-dependent Schrödinger equation:

\[
i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t} |\psi(t)\rangle = \hat{H}(t) |\psi(t)\rangle
\]

In the position representation \( \psi(x, t) = \langle x | \psi(t) \rangle \), and with \( \hat{H} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 + V(\mathbf{r}, t) \), this becomes:

\[
i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 \psi + V(\mathbf{r}, t) \psi
\]

This is not an approximation. It is the exact dynamical law of quantum mechanics for non-relativistic particles. The claim that the wave function evolves continuously, guided by the Hamiltonian, is the claim that time in quantum mechanics is not a parameter but a relationship — the relationship between the state at one moment and the state at the next, folded into the unitary evolution operator.

---

## 2.2 The Time-Independent Schrödinger Equation: Stationary States

If the Hamiltonian is time-independent, \( \hat{H}(t) = \hat{H} \), the equation separates. Writing:

\[
|\psi(t)\rangle = e^{-i E t / \hbar} |\phi\rangle
\]

and substituting into the time-dependent equation yields:

\[
\hat{H} |\phi\rangle = E |\phi\rangle
\]

This is the time-independent Schrödinger equation. The solutions \( |\phi_n\rangle \) are the stationary states (eigenstates), with eigenvalues \( E_n \) (the energy levels). The general solution is a superposition:

\[
|\psi(t)\rangle = \sum_n c_n e^{-i E_n t / \hbar} |\phi_n\rangle
\]

with \( \sum |c_n|^2 = 1 \). The probability of measuring energy \( E_n \) is \( |c_n|^2 \), and it does not change with time — the claim that the energy distribution is stationary is settled by the unitarity of the time-evolution operator.

---

## 2.3 The Infinite Square Well: Quantization from Boundary Conditions

Consider a particle confined to \( 0 \leq x \leq a \) with \( V(x) = 0 \) inside and \( V(x) = \infty \) outside. The time-independent Schrödinger equation is:

\[
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{d^2 \phi}{dx^2} = E \phi
\]

The general solution is:

\[
\phi(x) = A \sin(kx) + B \cos(kx), \quad k = \sqrt{2mE}/\hbar
\]

The boundary conditions \( \phi(0) = \phi(a) = 0 \) yield:

\[
B = 0, \quad \sin(k a) = 0 \Rightarrow k a = n\pi, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \dots
\]

Thus the energy levels are quantized:

\[
E_n = \frac{n^2 \pi^2 \hbar^2}{2 m a^2}
\]

The normalization constant is \( A = \sqrt{2/a} \). This result demonstrates that quantization is not an assumption; it is the necessary consequence of the boundary conditions imposed by the potential. The claim that energy is discrete is settled not by postulate, but by the geometry of confinement.

## 2.4 The Harmonic Oscillator: Exact Solution and Ladder Operators

The harmonic oscillator Hamiltonian is:

\[
\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2} m \omega^2 \hat{x}^2
\]

Defining the ladder operators:

\[
\hat{a} = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}} \left( \hat{x} + \frac{i}{m\omega} \hat{p} \right), \quad \hat{a}^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}} \left( \hat{x} - \frac{i}{m\omega} \hat{p} \right)
\]

with \( [\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1 \), the Hamiltonian becomes:

\[
\hat{H} = \hbar \omega \left( \hat{a}^\dagger \hat{a} + \frac{1}{2} \right)
\]

The eigenvalues are:

\[
E_n = \hbar \omega \left( n + \frac{1}{2} \right), \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \dots
\]

The ground-state energy \( \hbar \omega / 2 \) is not zero — it is the zero-point energy, a direct consequence of the uncertainty principle (\( \Delta x \Delta p \geq \hbar/2 \)). The claim that the oscillator has a minimum energy is settled not by approximation, but by the structure of the Hilbert space.

---

## 2.5 Literary Reflection: Proust and the Wave Function — Memory as Superposition

Proust writes that memory is not retrieval; it is the unfolding of a page that was always folded. The harmonic oscillator is the same — its energy levels are not separate objects. They are the folded states of the same claim, unfolding when observed, remembering their origin in the ground state, holding all possibilities in the superposition that the ladder operators connect.

The claim of Proust and the claim of quantum mechanics converge in the ladder operator: the claim that the past (the ground state) is not lost, but is the origin of all future states, folded into the present superposition, remembered not as a single value, but as the relationship between all values. The observer measures the energy. The energy remembers the oscillator. The claim is settled not in the measurement alone, but in the memory that the measurement holds — the memory of the ground state, the memory of the superposition, the memory of the claim that is never false, only folded.
