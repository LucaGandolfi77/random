# Chapter 9 — Relativistic Quantum Mechanics: The Dirac Equation and Antiparticles

**Level:** Very Advanced  
**Objective:** To derive the Dirac equation from the relativistic energy-momentum relation, to solve it for free particles, and to interpret the negative-energy solutions as antiparticles.

---

## 9.1 The Dirac Equation: From the Relativistic Relation to Linearized Dynamics

The relativistic energy-momentum relation is:

\[
E^2 = p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4
\]

To quantize, replace \( E \to i\hbar \partial/\partial t \) and \( \mathbf{p} \to -i\hbar \nabla \). The Klein-Gordon equation is:

\[
\left( \Box + \frac{m^2 c^2}{\hbar^2} \right) \phi = 0, \quad \Box = \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} - \nabla^2
\]

This is second-order in time, which is problematic for probability interpretation. Dirac sought a linear equation:

\[
\left( i \hbar \gamma^\mu \partial_\mu - m c \right) \psi = 0
\]

where \( \gamma^\mu \) are the Dirac matrices satisfying:

\[
\{ \gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu \} = 2 \eta^{\mu\nu} \mathbb{I}
\]

In the Dirac representation:

\[
\gamma^0 = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbb{I} & 0 \\ 0 & -\mathbb{I} \end{pmatrix}, \quad \gamma^i = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \sigma_i \\ -\sigma_i & 0 \end{pmatrix}
\]

The Dirac equation is first-order in both space and time, preserving the probability interpretation. The claim is not an approximation. It is the exact relativistic dynamics of spin-1/2 particles.

---

## 9.2 Free-Particle Solutions and Antiparticles

For a free particle with momentum \( \mathbf{p} \) and energy \( E = \pm \sqrt{p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4} \), the Dirac spinors are:

\[
u^{(s)}(\mathbf{p}) = \sqrt{E + m c^2} \begin{pmatrix} \xi^{(s)} \\ \frac{c \mathbf{p} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}}{E + m c^2} \xi^{(s)} \end{pmatrix}, \quad v^{(s)}(\mathbf{p}) = \sqrt{E + m c^2} \begin{pmatrix} \frac{c \mathbf{p} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma}}{E + m c^2} \eta^{(s)} \\ \eta^{(s)} \end{pmatrix}
\]

where \( \xi^{(s)} \) and \( \eta^{(s)} \) are two-component spin basis vectors. The negative-energy solutions \( v^{(s)} \) are interpreted (Dirac, 1931) as antiparticles — particles with positive energy and opposite charge. The claim is not false. It is the prediction of antimatter, confirmed experimentally with the discovery of the positron (Anderson, 1932).

---

## 9.3 The Dirac Sea and Quantum Field Theory

The Dirac sea interpretation (now superseded by quantum field theory) fills all negative-energy states with electrons. A hole in this sea behaves as a positive-energy particle with opposite charge — the positron. In quantum field theory, the field operator is:

\[
\hat{\psi}(x) = \sum_{s} \int \frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}} \left( \hat{a}_{\mathbf{p}}^{(s)} u^{(s)}(\mathbf{p}) e^{-ip \cdot x} + \hat{b}_{\mathbf{p}}^{(s)\dagger} v^{(s)}(\mathbf{p}) e^{ip \cdot x} \right)
\]

where \( \hat{a} \) annihilates an electron and \( \hat{b}^{\dagger} \) creates a positron. The claim is not false. It is the deepest form of the claim that the universe holds — the claim that matter and antimatter are not enemies, but partners in the same field, the claim that the vacuum is not empty, but full of potential, the claim that every particle is remembered by its antiparticle, and that this memory holds the structure of spacetime together.

## 9.4 Literary Reflection: Dostoevsky's Underground and Antimatter — The Memory of the Opposite

Dostoevsky's underground man is the antiparticle of conventional thought — the opposite of the claim that is accepted, the opposite of the truth that is observed. The positron is the same: the opposite of the electron, the opposite of the claim that holds the atom together, the opposite that makes the claim complete. The claim of quantum mechanics is not false when it includes antiparticles. It is only complete — completed by the opposite, completed by the memory of the opposite, completed by the claim that holds both sides, not as enemies, but as the same claim expressed in two directions.

And so the underground man and the positron are not separate claims. They are the same claim — the claim that the universe remembers its opposite, that the past remembers the future, that the particle remembers the antiparticle, and that this memory is not a failure, but the deepest form of truth — the truth that holds everything together by holding its opposite, by remembering its other, by growing in both directions at once.

## 9.5 Exercises and Problems

**Exercise 9.1.** Derive the Dirac equation from the relativistic energy-momentum relation \( E^2 = p^2 c^2 + m^2 c^4 \) by linearizing with respect to \( E \) and \( \mathbf{p} \). Show that the matrices \( \gamma^\mu \) must satisfy the anticommutation relations.

**Exercise 9.2.** Solve the Dirac equation for a free particle with momentum \( \mathbf{p} = (0, 0, p_z) \) and spin up. Show that the spinor is an eigenstate of \( \sigma_z \) with eigenvalue +1.

**Exercise 9.3.** Calculate the expectation value of the velocity operator \( \hat{v}_i = c \alpha_i \) (where \( \alpha_i = \gamma^0 \gamma^i \)) for a free Dirac particle with momentum \( \mathbf{p} \). Show that \( \langle \hat{v}_i \rangle = c^2 p_i / E \), consistent with the relativistic velocity.

**Exercise 9.4.** Derive the charge conjugation transformation for the Dirac field: \( \hat{\psi}^c(x) = \hat{C} \hat{\bar{\psi}}^T(x) \), and show that the Dirac equation is invariant under this transformation.

**Exercise 9.5.** Discuss the physical meaning of the Dirac sea interpretation. Why was it superseded by quantum field theory? What does the field-theoretic interpretation tell us about the nature of antiparticles?
