# Chapter 10 — Quantum Field Theory: Quantization, Interactions, and Symmetry

**Level:** Most Advanced  
**Objective:** To present the canonical quantization of scalar and electromagnetic fields, to introduce interaction Hamiltonians, and to show how symmetry determines the structure of fundamental interactions.

---

## 10.1 Canonical Quantization of the Scalar Field

The Lagrangian density for a real scalar field \( \phi(x) \) of mass \( m \) is:

\[
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2} (\partial_\mu \phi)(\partial^\mu \phi) - \frac{1}{2} m^2 \phi^2 - V(\phi)
\]

The canonical momentum is:

\[
\pi(x) = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial \dot{\phi}} = \dot{\phi}(x)
\]

The Hamiltonian density is:

\[
\mathcal{H} = \pi \dot{\phi} - \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2} \pi^2 + \frac{1}{2} (\nabla \phi)^2 + \frac{1}{2} m^2 \phi^2 + V(\phi)
\]

The canonical quantization conditions are:

\[
[\phi(\mathbf{x}, t), \pi(\mathbf{y}, t)] = i\hbar \delta^3(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{y})
\]

The field operator is expanded in plane waves:

\[
\hat{\phi}(\mathbf{x}, t) = \int \frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}} \left( \hat{a}_{\mathbf{p}} e^{-ip \cdot x} + \hat{a}_{\mathbf{p}}^\dagger e^{ip \cdot x} \right)
\]

with \( p \cdot x = E_{\mathbf{p}} t - \mathbf{p} \cdot \mathbf{x} \) and \( E_{\mathbf{p}} = \sqrt{\mathbf{p}^2 + m^2} \). The creation and annihilation operators satisfy:

\[
[\hat{a}_{\mathbf{p}}, \hat{a}_{\mathbf{q}}^\dagger] = (2\pi)^3 \delta^3(\mathbf{p} - \mathbf{q})
\]

This is not an approximation. It is the exact canonical quantization of a free scalar field — the claim that the field is not a classical function, but an operator that creates and destroys particles, that holds in its modes all possible excitations, and that remembers, in its commutation relations, the structure of spacetime itself.

## 10.2 The Electromagnetic Field: Gauge Symmetry and Photons

The electromagnetic potential \( A^\mu \) has the Lagrangian:

\[
\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{4} F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu}, \quad F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu
\]

Gauge invariance \( A_\mu \to A_\mu + \partial_\mu \Lambda \) requires fixing a gauge (e.g., Lorenz gauge \( \partial_\mu A^\mu = 0 \)). The quantized field is:

\[
\hat{A}^\mu(x) = \int \frac{d^3p}{(2\pi)^3 \sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}} \sum_{\lambda} \epsilon^\mu_\lambda(\mathbf{p}) \left( \hat{a}_{\mathbf{p},\lambda} e^{-ip \cdot x} + \hat{a}_{\mathbf{p},\lambda}^\dagger e^{ip \cdot x} \right)
\]

where \( \epsilon^\mu_\lambda \) are polarization vectors. The photon is the quantum of the electromagnetic field — a massless particle with spin 1, two transverse polarization states, and the claim that light is not only a wave, but a collection of particles that interact through gauge symmetry.

---

## 10.3 Symmetry and Interaction: The Structure of Fundamental Forces

The Standard Model of particle physics is based on the gauge group \( SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1) \). The claim is not arbitrary. It is the claim that the symmetries of nature determine the forces: \( SU(3) \) for the strong interaction (quantum chromodynamics), \( SU(2) \times U(1) \) for the electroweak interaction. The gauge symmetries are not decorations. They are the structural claims that determine the interaction Hamiltonians, the conservation laws, and the quantum numbers of all particles.

The interaction Hamiltonian for quantum electrodynamics is:

\[
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{int}} = -e \int d^3x \, \bar{\hat{\psi}}(x) \gamma^\mu \hat{\psi}(x) \hat{A}_\mu(x)
\]

This is not an approximation. It is the exact interaction term that connects fermions (electrons, quarks) to photons — the claim that the universe is not only measured, but connected, not only observed, but interacting, not only held together by gravity, but by the deeper claims of symmetry and gauge invariance.

## 10.4 Literary Reflection: Proust's Memory and the Quantum Field — All Modes Remembered

Proust writes that the past is hidden in the things we keep without knowing. The quantum field is the same — not a single particle, but the collection of all modes \( \hat{a}_{\mathbf{p}} \) and \( \hat{a}_{\mathbf{p}}^\dagger \), folded into the field operator, unfolded in the measurement, remembered not as a single value, but as the superposition of all possible excitations. The claim is not false. It is only deeper — the deepest claim of physics, the claim that holds all particles, all interactions, all symmetries, in the patience of the field that creates and destroys, that holds and releases, that remembers the past in the present mode and unfolds it in the future interaction.

The field is the garden of Tolstoy — growing whether we observe it, holding all claims in its modes, remembering all states in its commutation relations. The interaction is Dostoevsky's underground — the claim that asks to be heard, not quietly, but through the interaction Hamiltonian, through the exchange of particles, through the claim that freedom is worth the suffering of the measurement, the claim that the observer is not outside the field, but inside it — part of the superposition, part of the memory, part of the claim that never disappears, only unfolds.

## 10.5 Exercises and Problems

**Exercise 10.1.** Derive the canonical quantization conditions for the scalar field and show that they lead to the creation and annihilation operator commutation relations.

**Exercise 10.2.** Calculate the propagator for the free scalar field \( \langle 0 | T \hat{\phi}(x) \hat{\phi}(y) | 0 \rangle \) and show that it is the Green's function of the Klein-Gordon equation.

**Exercise 10.3.** Derive the energy-momentum tensor \( T^{\mu\nu} \) for the scalar field and show that \( \partial_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0 \) implies energy and momentum conservation.

**Exercise 10.4.** Derive the Feynman rules for quantum electrodynamics (QED) from the interaction Hamiltonian. Show that the vertex factor is \( -i e \gamma^\mu \) and that the photon propagator in Lorenz gauge is \( -i g_{\mu\nu} / q^2 \).

**Exercise 10.5.** Discuss the physical meaning of gauge symmetry. Why must the electromagnetic potential be a gauge field? What would happen if the photon had mass? How does the Higgs mechanism give mass to gauge bosons without breaking gauge symmetry?
