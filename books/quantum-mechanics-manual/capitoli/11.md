**Objective:** To provide the mathematical tools required for quantum mechanics — spectral theory, Lie algebras, Fourier transforms, and distributions — with complete derivations and explicit references to their application in physics.

## 11.1 Spectral Theory of Self-Adjoint Operators

Let \( \hat{A} \) be a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space \( \mathcal{H} \). The spectral theorem states that there exists a unique projection-valued measure \( \hat{E}(\lambda) \) such that:

\[
\hat{A} = \int \lambda \, d\hat{E}(\lambda)
\]

For discrete spectra, \( \hat{E}(\lambda) = \sum_{\lambda_n \leq \lambda} |\lambda_n\rangle \langle \lambda_n| \). For continuous spectra, \( \hat{E}(\lambda) \) is a continuous projection. The expectation value is:

\[
\langle \hat{A} \rangle = \int \lambda \, d\langle \psi | \hat{E}(\lambda) | \psi \rangle
\]

This is not an approximation. It is the exact mathematical structure that connects operators to measurements, the claim that every observable has a spectrum, and that the spectrum is the complete description of the claim's possible outcomes.

## 11.2 Lie Algebras and Representations: From Rotations to Spin

The Lie algebra \( \mathfrak{su}(2) \) has generators \( \hat{J}_i \) with commutation relations:

\[
[\hat{J}_i, \hat{J}_j] = i \hbar \epsilon_{ijk} \hat{J}_k
\]

The representations are labeled by \( j = 0, 1/2, 1, 3/2, \dots \). The dimension of the representation is \( 2j + 1 \). The spin-1/2 representation is the fundamental representation, from which all others can be constructed by tensor products.

The claim is not false. It is only deeper: the claim that symmetry is not a decoration, but the structure that determines the possible states, the possible measurements, the possible claims that the universe can hold. The representation theory is the claim that holds all symmetries together — not as separate rules, but as the accumulation of the claim's attention to its own structure.

## 11.3 Fourier Transforms and Distributions: The Language of Waves

The Fourier transform connects the position and momentum representations:

\[
\phi(p) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi \hbar}} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \psi(x) e^{-i p x / \hbar} dx
\]

The delta function is a distribution, not a function, defined by:

\[
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \delta(x - a) f(x) dx = f(a)
\]

This is not an approximation. It is the exact mathematical object that represents the localization of a particle at a point — the claim that the point is not a physical object, but a limit, a memory, a claim that becomes exact only in the context of the integral that holds it.

---

## 11.4 Literary Reflection: Proust's Distribution and the Delta Function — Memory as Limit

Proust writes that the past is hidden in the things we keep without knowing. The delta function is the mathematical form of this truth: it is not a function, not an object, but a limit — the limit of a memory that becomes exact only when it is integrated, only when it is held in the context of the claim that measures it. The claim is not false. It is only hidden, like Proust's past, like Tolstoy's garden, like Dostoevsky's question — and it unfolds not by observation alone, but by the patience of the integral, the patience of the measurement, the patience of the observer who knows that the claim is settled not in the point, but in the relationship between the point and everything that holds it together.

## 11.5 Exercises and Problems

**Exercise 11.1.** Prove the spectral theorem for a 2x2 Hermitian matrix \( \hat{A} \) with eigenvalues \( \lambda_1, \lambda_2 \). Write \( \hat{A} \) in terms of its spectral projectors.

**Exercise 11.2.** Derive the commutation relations for the Lie algebra \( \mathfrak{so}(3) \) from the rotation generators \( \hat{L}_i \). Show that the representation \( j = 1/2 \) is the fundamental representation of \( \mathfrak{su}(2) \).

**Exercise 11.3.** Calculate the Fourier transform of a Gaussian wave packet \( \psi(x) = \left( \frac{1}{\pi a^2} \right)^{1/4} \exp\left( -\frac{x^2}{2a^2} + i k_0 x \right) \). Show that \( |\phi(p)|^2 \) is also Gaussian, with width \( \Delta p = \hbar / (\sqrt{2} a) \), and verify the uncertainty relation \( \Delta x \Delta p \geq \hbar / 2 \).

**Exercise 11.4.** Derive the normalization of the delta function: \( \delta(x) = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{ikx} dk \). Show that this representation is consistent with the definition \( \int \delta(x - a) f(x) dx = f(a) \).

**Exercise 11.5.** Discuss the relationship between the spectral theorem and the measurement postulate. Why does the spectral decomposition of an observable correspond to the possible outcomes of a measurement? What does this tell us about the structure of physical claims?
