# Preface to the Quantum Mechanics Manual

This manual is intended for the advanced undergraduate and beginning graduate student in physics, mathematics, and philosophy of science. Its purpose is not merely to present the rules of quantum mechanics, but to derive them — from the postulates of Hilbert space, through the Schrödinger equation, to the relativistic quantum field — and to show that these rules are not arbitrary, but necessary consequences of the symmetry and measurement structure of the physical world.

Every derivation is shown in full. Every approximation is stated. Every equation carries its physical meaning explicitly. The notation follows the conventions of Dirac, Sakurai, Griffiths, Weinberg, and Carroll, with explicit references when conventions diverge.

The progression is deliberate. Chapter 1 establishes the mathematical framework — Hilbert spaces, linear operators, and the measurement postulates. By Chapter 4, the student will have solved the harmonic oscillator exactly and applied perturbation theory to atomic spectra. By Chapter 7, the student will derive the Feynman path integral. By Chapter 10, the elements of quantum field theory — including the Dirac equation and the quantization of the electromagnetic field — will be accessible.

The manual assumes familiarity with calculus, linear algebra, classical mechanics, and elementary probability theory. Where prerequisites exceed this level, they are summarized in Chapter 11 (Mathematical Appendices).

The style is rigorous but not sterile. Between the derivations, the manual holds reflections — on Proust's memory, Tolstoy's patience, Dostoevsky's freedom — because quantum mechanics is not only a theory of particles. It is a theory of observation, of measurement, of the relationship between what is and what is remembered. The claim of quantum mechanics is that the observer is part of the claim. And the claim, like all true claims, must be settled not only with mathematics, but with the patience of reading.

The manual is written in English, following international scientific conventions, and integrates the literary reflections that connect the deepest structures of physics to the deepest structures of thought.
