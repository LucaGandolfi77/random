# Chapter 6j — The Call from the Stairwell

The after-hours line called Mara back at 1:17 in the morning. She had been asleep for an hour. The duty clinician said a caller had requested Mara by name, though the caller was not a current patient. Mara sat up, found her glasses, and asked the clinician to remain on the line while she took the transfer.

The caller was Tavi. She had come home late and found a section of the stairwell blocked by repair work. The building manager had removed the temporary evacuation notice. The smell of fresh paint had triggered the memory of smoke. She had gone outside and was sitting on the curb, unable to decide whether to go back in or call Luca. She was not in immediate danger. She did not want emergency services. She wanted someone to tell her whether she was overreacting.

Mara felt the danger of answering the question in the form it had been asked. “I can’t decide whether the reaction is too much,” she said. “I can help you decide what you want to do next.”

Tavi said she wanted to know if the building was safe. Mara could not inspect it. She asked whether the maintenance crew was still inside, whether there was another exit, whether the building manager had a number on the posted notice. The notice was gone. Tavi had a photograph of the old one. Mara asked whether she would be willing to call the night porter. Tavi said yes.

The porter confirmed that the second stairwell was open and that residents had been notified about the paint, though Tavi had not received the notice. He said he would come downstairs and walk with her. Tavi agreed. Before hanging up, she said, “I know this isn’t a panic attack exactly.”

“It doesn’t have to be one for you to ask for help,” Mara said.

After the call, the duty clinician asked whether Mara should have taken it, given that Tavi had been a former patient. The line’s policy allowed a clinician to respond to a request for continuity if the caller consented and no other boundary applied. Mara had disclosed her professional relationship and offered a transfer. Tavi had chosen to stay on the line.

Mara recorded the call as a consultation, not a treatment session. She notified Tavi the next morning that the service had documented it and offered to share the note. Tavi asked for the note and requested that it not be used in any case review. Mara agreed.

The building manager later restored the notice and sent a direct message to tenants. The repair was completed. Tavi did not return to the clinic for treatment, though she called once more to ask how to make a formal complaint about access. The line had not diagnosed her, reassured her that nothing was wrong, or solved the fear. It had helped her find the next person who could answer the practical question.

Mara went back to bed. The dark room seemed different from the one she had left, though she knew it had not changed. She thought of how often the mind sought a name for distress before it allowed the person to respond to it. Sometimes the name mattered. Sometimes the stairwell simply needed an open exit.
