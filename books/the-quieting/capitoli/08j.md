# Chapter 8j — The Partner’s Question

Tavi’s partner, Luca, came to an appointment after she invited him. He brought a list of questions and sat close enough to read the clinician’s notes. Tavi placed the folder with her assessment on the table and kept one hand over it.

Luca had been afraid since the fire. He had called the clinic twice, once when Tavi was asleep and once when she had asked him not to. The second time, he had asked whether the service could tell him what signs to watch for. The receptionist had refused to discuss Tavi’s care without permission. Luca understood the rule and still felt shut out by it.

He asked Chen whether Tavi was at risk of the Quieting. Chen explained the limits of the assessment again. Luca listened, then asked whether the clinic could guarantee that the memory problems would not get worse.

“No,” Chen said.

“Then what can we do?”

Tavi answered before Chen could. “You can stop testing me at breakfast.”

Luca looked hurt. He said he had been trying to help. Tavi said she knew, but the questions made her feel that every conversation had become an exam. She wanted him to notice if something changed; she did not want him to correct every pause.

Mara watched the exchange carefully. The couple were not arguing about whether Luca loved Tavi. They were arguing about the form love should take when fear entered the house. Luca believed attention was protection. Tavi needed attention that did not turn her into an object of surveillance.

Chen asked if they would be willing to make an agreement about what to do if Tavi noticed new symptoms. Tavi said she would tell Luca if she became worried and ask him to help arrange care. Luca could ask once whether she wanted support, then would not repeat the question unless there was a clear change in safety. They would review the agreement in a month. It was not a treatment contract; it was a way to keep concern from becoming an unending test.

Luca apologized. Tavi accepted, then said she was still angry. He said he understood. She asked if he did. He said he was trying to.

After the appointment, Tavi told Mara she had invited him because she wanted him to hear the limits from someone besides her. She had also wanted the clinic to witness that the limits mattered. Mara said that was reasonable. Tavi asked whether the note would mention that she had been angry. Mara asked if she wanted it to. Tavi said no; she did not want an emotion added to her file as if it explained the whole relationship.

The couple left together. At the reception desk, Luca held the door while Tavi put the assessment folder into her bag. He did not ask whether she had remembered everything. The gesture was small and did not settle their conflict. It was an attempt at a different kind of attention, one that let her walk through the door without having to prove she could.
