# Chapter 9h — The Box Marked Restricted

A courier brought Jonah a box from the former records office with the words **RESTRICTED MATERIAL—REVIEW BEFORE ACCESS** stamped on the lid. It had been transferred during the archive’s reorganization and then forgotten in a storage room. Inside were staff notes, old patient letters, and a set of recordings from the first public meetings about the Stillness Protocol.

The recordings mattered. They showed that objections to the recovery measure had been raised years before the public summaries changed. One clinician warned that return to work could reflect financial pressure. A patient representative asked that the service distinguish between a person who chose to return and one who had no paid leave. The committee chair said the point was noted. The final minutes did not include it.

Jonah listened to the recording with Elias. He felt the familiar shock of hearing a past voice state what the archive had implied. The evidence was powerful. It also contained the names of patients and staff who had not agreed to become witnesses in a current political argument.

Elias wanted to make a transcript available to the review panel immediately. Jonah said they needed to identify and redact names first. Elias said the redaction process could take weeks, while the council was voting in three days. Jonah said speed had already been the excuse for turning private records into public evidence without consent.

The argument sharpened. Elias accused Jonah of protecting the dead from the only truth that could help the living. Jonah accused Elias of believing that harm became acceptable when it served a good cause. Neither man had forgotten that the records had been hidden by a process of small decisions, each one defended as temporary.

Mina joined them for the afternoon review. She recognized one voice as a former patient who had later joined the advisory board. The person was still alive. Mina contacted them through the board’s advocate and asked whether they wanted the recording included. The person agreed to the transcript but not to public audio. Another speaker could not be located. Their words would be paraphrased without identifying details, and the uncertainty about attribution would be clearly stated.

The work took longer than Jonah wanted. The council postponed the vote by one day after receiving the authenticated transcript. The public report now had evidence that objections had existed and been omitted from later summaries. It also explained which parts were withheld and why.

Elias apologized for wanting to rush. Jonah apologized for assuming that urgency meant he could not be trusted. Their collaboration did not return to its earlier ease. It became more deliberate, which was perhaps what the archive required of both of them.

That night Jonah read the transcript alone. He heard the room in the voices: the scrape of a chair, a microphone failing, someone asking for water. The past did not become a single villainous scene. It became a meeting of tired people who had heard a warning and failed to give it enough weight. Jonah understood why that was harder to hate. He also understood why it had to be recorded.
