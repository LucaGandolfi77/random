# Chapter 10 — The Night Tide

The panic arrived on the night the council announced a special hearing.

Mara was alone in her kitchen, reading the draft testimony. Ada’s name appeared in the appendix, redacted but recognizable to anyone who knew the dates. Mara read the sentence about the deferred appointment, and the room narrowed to a strip of light beneath the door.

Her heart struck hard. Her hands went numb. She thought: *This is it. This is how it begins.*

The thought was familiar enough to be dangerous. It carried every old fear in its pocket: that feeling too much would take her away from herself, that a mind was a glass vessel, that one more crack would finish it.

She called Sera, not because Sera could rescue her, but because Sera had taught her to ask for company before the room became an emergency.

“I’m here,” Sera said. “Are you in immediate danger?”

“No.”

“Have you taken anything? Are you thinking of hurting yourself?”

“No. I’m frightened.”

“Then we’ll begin there.”

Sera stayed on the phone. She did not order Mara to breathe more slowly. They named the kitchen, the blue cup, the rain on the fire escape. Mara let the panic move through her without bargaining with it. It did not vanish. It changed shape, as weather did.

When the worst had passed, she cried for Ada. Not as a case, not as a warning, not as the sister whose death proved a point. She cried for the girl who had stolen cherries and the woman who had once stayed up all night helping Mara sew a dress for an interview. She cried for the apology that had never arrived in time.

Sera was quiet on the line.

“I’m sorry I didn’t come sooner,” Mara said.

“You came now.”

The sentence did not absolve her. It made a place for the present.

The next morning Mara met her own psychiatrist, Dr. Chen, and told her the whole story: the panic, the old fear, the testimony. They spoke about sleep, support, and whether she should take leave. Mara chose two days off and asked Jonah to stay that evening. Dr. Chen made no prediction about what the episode meant for Mara’s future. She helped Mara make the next day possible.

In the afternoon, Nell sent a photograph of her map. She had drawn the clinic door open wide enough for a person carrying a wheelchair, a suitcase, or nothing at all.

Beneath it she had written: *Not fixed. More accessible.*

Mara saved the picture. Then she closed the file and went outside.
