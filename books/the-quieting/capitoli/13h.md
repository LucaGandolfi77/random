# Chapter 13h — Six Months Later

Six months after Jonah gave Mara the key, they met at the kitchen table to discuss the apartment question. The table was not his or hers; they had bought it secondhand and carried it upstairs together. Its surface bore three pale circles from hot cups and a narrow scratch from the day they tried to cut a loaf without a board.

Mara had expected the conversation to feel like a decision. Instead it felt like checking the weather before a journey: relevant, incomplete, not in itself a command to go or stay. She said she still wanted her own apartment. She also wanted a place where they could be together without negotiating which door to use every time. They considered a larger apartment with two work spaces and a small room that could be left empty until they knew what it was for.

Jonah wanted to live together. He also wanted to know that Mara’s choice was not made from fear of disappointing him. She wanted him to have a preference without turning that preference into pressure. They struggled to say these things without converting the conversation into a test of who was more generous.

At last Mara said, “I think I want to try it for a year, with the understanding that either of us can change our mind.”

Jonah asked what changing their minds would mean. Would one person move out? Would they keep the old apartments? What would happen to the furniture? The practical questions interrupted the romantic mood, which was perhaps why they were useful. A decision that affected daily life deserved details as well as feeling.

They agreed to look for a place and not sign anything until they had both slept on the choice. They would keep Mara’s apartment for the first three months if they could afford it. The plan cost more than either liked. Jonah said they could revisit it after they saw the numbers. Mara said she did not want the expense used later as proof that she owed the arrangement a success.

He took her hand. “I don’t want a year from now to be a verdict on whether we love each other.”

“Neither do I.”

“Then what is it?”

“A year we try to live together.”

They found a flat above a print shop, with two small rooms and a narrow balcony. Mara liked the light; Jonah liked the window seat. They signed the lease after three visits and one argument about the noise from the street. On the day they moved in, they brought only half their books. The other half remained in separate apartments, where they could be retrieved if needed.

The empty room stayed empty for a month. Then Mara put a desk in it. Jonah put a chair beside the desk, leaving enough space for someone to work alone. It was not a symbolic gesture, though it became one when they noticed it. It was a room with a desk, a chair, and a door that closed.
