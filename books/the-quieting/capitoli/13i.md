# Chapter 13i — The First Morning in the New Flat

The first morning in the flat above the print shop, Mara woke before Jonah and did not know where she was. The room smelled of paper dust and the coffee he had ground the night before. She saw the unfamiliar ceiling, the narrow balcony, the chair by the window, and then remembered the move. For several seconds the memory did not arrive as comfort. It arrived as information.

She had chosen this. She had signed the lease. Her books were in boxes in the hall. The decision had not made her certain; it had made her responsible for the next day of it.

Jonah woke when she went to the kitchen. He asked if she wanted coffee. She said yes. He asked if she wanted to talk. She said not yet. He made the coffee and left it beside her without asking what not yet meant.

The first week revealed the ordinary things they had never needed to negotiate while living apart: who bought soap, whether the window should be left open, how many books could occupy the table before a meal, whether silence meant concentration or anger. The first argument was about a coat hung over the back of a chair. Jonah moved it; Mara put it back. They stopped speaking for an hour, then laughed when they saw that the coat had fallen to the floor between them.

The second argument was not funny. Jonah found Mara’s appointment card from Dr. Chen and asked whether she wanted a ride. She heard in the question an assumption that her care was now a shared responsibility. He meant only to offer. She said she could manage her own appointments. He replied that he knew, and that offering a ride was not the same as managing her.

They did not resolve the distinction that night. The following morning Jonah asked if she still wanted the ride. Mara said yes, but asked him not to ask again unless she brought it up. He agreed. They drove in silence, the city passing beyond the windows.

At the clinic entrance, Mara paused before getting out. She had the sudden thought that she had brought Jonah into a life he could not understand and would eventually resent. She told him. He said he might resent parts of it. He might also love parts she had not yet shown him. He did not promise to feel only what she hoped.

Mara felt the old grief in the honesty, because honesty did not protect her from the possibility of loss. It also gave her something more solid than reassurance. She touched his cheek and went inside.

That evening they unpacked a box marked BOOKS. They placed Proust beside Dostoevsky and Tolstoy, though the shelf was too small for all three. Jonah said it was an argument waiting to happen. Mara said it was a library. They left a gap between the books for the volume they had not yet bought.
