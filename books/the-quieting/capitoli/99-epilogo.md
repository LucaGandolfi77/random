# Epilogue — What Remains

A year later, the Room of Return at Bellwether Secondary had a wider door.

Nell’s map was printed on the wall beside it. The route included three benches, a public garden, and the bakery that sold pear pastries on wet days. She had finished her apprenticeship and was studying urban design. She still had difficult mornings. She had learned which people to call, which treatments helped, and that asking for help did not sign away the authorship of her life.

Mara worked four days a week at the clinic and one day with the independent review. The public report had named the failures without calling the whole service a fraud. Some people accused the city of weakness; others accused it of moving too slowly. Both were partly right. Sera returned to practice under supervision after the review. She and Mara had not become friends again, but they had learned to speak without pretending the past was finished.

Jonah kept a key to Mara’s apartment. He did not keep a drawer there. They were learning the difference between commitment and evidence of commitment.

One evening, Mara found him reading Proust at the kitchen table. A pear sat between them, bruised on one side.

“Does it taste like anything?” he asked.

She cut it in half. The flesh was sweet, then a little bitter near the skin. It reminded her of Ada. It reminded her of nothing. Both were true.

“Not everything has to be a doorway,” she said.

Jonah smiled. “You’re sure?”

“No. But I’m learning to leave some doors closed without calling them locked.”

They ate at the table. Through the open window came the harbor bells, and below them the city carried on: people crying in clinics, in kitchens, in the quiet rooms of buses; people laughing too loudly; clinicians staying late; friends learning not to fix what they could first witness.

No one had discovered how to live without pain. The city had only begun to understand that prevention was not the same as prevention of feeling, and that a person could be vulnerable without being a forecast.

Mara rested her hand on Jonah’s. Outside, the tide moved in its old direction, and the lamps held their small circles against the dark.

For tonight, they were enough.
