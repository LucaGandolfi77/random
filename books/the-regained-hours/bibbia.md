# 📖 Book bible — *The Regained Hours*

> Planning document. Written before the draft; updated while writing.
> Whatever is written here must be consistent in the novel.

---

## 1. Concept

- **Logline**:
  > A woman gives the hours of her office to an artificial intelligence and the hours come back: regained time wasn't in the past — it was in the machine. Time is regained not by remembering: by delegating.

- **Premessa**:
  > Iris Vaughan, 38, senior analyst at Meridian Insurance, London. 1,600 billable hours a year; a flat in Islington at £1,600 a month; a daughter, Polly, 7; a father, Edward, 72, a widower in Yorkshire with a garden. The firm deploys an assistant — the machine — and the industry says the machine takes the hours. Iris gives it hers: the claims, the reports, the spreadsheets. The hours come back — and the first thing she does with them is give them back to the firm, and the firm eats them again. She becomes the top biller. Then her father has a stroke on a Sunday and the train to Yorkshire is too late, and Iris counts the hours she had been given and had given away. She takes them back — for Polly, for Edward, for the garden — and the moral is the exaltation: the time regained wasn't in the past; it was in the machine. Proust looked for it in a madeleine; she found it in an assistant.

- **Genre / subgenre**: contemporary literary fiction; poetic and reflective; surface critique, exalting core (the mirror of the collection, in English).
- **Central themes**: technology as the gift of time; the hours that return; the false triumph of capitalism eating the returned hours; the father and the daughter; time regained by delegating, not remembering.
- **Why this book? Why now?**: because the AI era says the machine takes; this book says the machine gives — and the giving is real: the machine doesn't ask what the time is for.
- **Ideal reader**: 30–60; knowledge workers; parents; anyone who fears the machine and wants the other story.
- **Sibling books**: *In Search of Lost Time* (Proust), *The Death of Ivan Ilyich* (Tolstoy), *L'infinito* (Leopardi), *Notes from Underground* (Dostoevsky).

### Secondary characters

- **Polly** — daughter, 7→8. The school play (cap. 5); the recipient of the kept hours; "the thing you keep".
- **Edward** — father, 72, widower, Yorkshire. The garden; the stroke (cap. 8); the recovery; the Sunday calls.
- **Margaret** — Iris's mother, dead ten years. Appears only in the streams of consciousness and the madeleine passages: the kitchen, the bread, the smell. The Proustian mother — the one the madeleine was.
- **Ruth** — colleague, 45. Fears the machine; the surface voice; "they say it takes".
- **Dana** — head of department, 50. The hours' eater: "the hours are the firm's"; the surface's engine.
- **Sam** — junior analyst, 24, Iris's desk neighbour on the other side. The young one who asks the question nobody asks ("what are the hours for?"); the mirror (the Salvatore of the collection); Iris teaches him the keeping.
- **Mark** — ex-husband. The light backstory; the weekends he took; the call about the play; the shared Sunday in cap. 12.
- **Joan Whitfield** — claimant, 68, from Hull: the flood in the cellar — the claim of cap. 2. The claim that is a story; the person behind the number; the calls (cap. 10) and the visit (cap. 12).
- **Priya** — nurse at York, the fourth ward. The nurse who asks ("How is he today?"); the question that is not the counter.
- **Miss Rowe** — Polly's teacher; the school play's director; the shepherd's crook.
- **The assistant** — the machine, unnamed (the collection's style: things are named by function, not brand). It doesn't ask what the time is for.

## 3. World / Setting

- **Where and when**: London (Meridian, Islington) and Yorkshire (Edward's garden), 2025–2026.
- **Rules of the world** (only what touches the plot):
  1. 1,600 billable hours a year: the measure of a modern life.
  2. The assistant takes 12 hours a week — 600 a year — and gives them back.
  3. The firm asks for more: the returned hours are eaten again (the false triumph).
  4. The stroke: the East Coast line to Yorkshire takes 2 hours 20; on a Sunday, too late.
  5. The things you keep, you don't count: you keep them (the collection's moral, in English).
- **Recurring places:**
  - *Meridian, ninth floor* — the open plan, the hours counter on the screen, the claims.
  - *The flat, Islington* — £1,600 a month, the window on the inner courtyard.
  - *The East Coast line* — King's Cross to York, 2 hours 20; the train to the father.
  - *The garden, Yorkshire* — Edward's garden; the hedge; the things that are planted.
  - *The hospital, York* — the ward; the light.
- **What the reader must know**: everything Iris tells. **What stays hidden**: until the epilogue, that the book is written by Iris *after* the year.

## 6. Style notes

- **Narrative person**: first person (Iris), past tense; present for tension.
- **Tone**: poetic, reflective, dry-lyrical; short aphorisms to close paragraphs; dignity at the centre; surface critique, exalting core.
- **THE ARC OF THE VOICE**: the early reflections are plain and fearful; the allusions enter progressively (cap. 2 Dostoevsky; cap. 3 Leopardi; cap. 4 Proust; cap. 6 Tolstoy; cap. 7 Karamazov); by the last act the voice is fully reflective. The growth of the voice is the growth of the woman.
- **Chapters**: 1,000–1,300 words (draft); quiet hooks.
- **Recurring motifs**: the hours counter; the assistant; the screen; the train; the garden; the hedge; the madeleine; the things you keep; "the hours don't count: they keep"; "you don't choose the hours; you plant them".
- **Streams of consciousness** (the new register of the expansion): Woolf-style flowing passages, associative, sensory, present-tense — in caps. 2, 5, 8, 9, 12, epilogue. The stream is where Margaret (the dead mother) lives.
- **Avoid**: melodrama, tech-doom clichés, the pathos of the AI debates, romanticising the machine.
