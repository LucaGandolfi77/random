## Draft

> "to be overly conscious is a sickness, a real, thoroughgoing sickness."
>
> — Fyodor Dostoevsky, *Notes from Underground*

Thursday came, and the training was at ten, in the meeting room with the long table. The trainer was a man from the vendor, and the vendor was a firm I had never seen, and the assistant was on the screen: a window, a cursor, a field that waited. The field waited like the fields you fill in: with the care of someone who knows the field is a form. The assistant didn't have a face, and the assistant didn't have a name: the firm called it the assistant, and the assistant was the machine. The machine didn't ask questions — that was the first thing I learned. The machine waited.

The first delegation was a claim: the flood in the cellar, the one from Hull. The claim was forty pages, and the report was six, and the report took me four hours — four hours, every week, for six years. I gave the claim to the assistant, and the assistant didn't ask; the assistant read. Eleven minutes later the report was on the screen: six pages, the numbers right, the language right. The language was right and the language wasn't mine: the language was the machine's, and the machine's language, I learned later, was the thing that came back.

The counter, in the top right corner, said four hours back. Four hours. Four hours, every week, for six years — and the four hours came back in eleven minutes, and nobody asked me what they were for. Not the counter, not the firm, not the machine: the machine doesn't ask you what the time is for. It gives you the hours, and the hours come back, and the hours are yours. Nobody asks. The not asking was the thing I feared the most, and the not asking, I learned later, was the whole of the gift: you give the hours, and the hours come back, and the hours are yours — and the yours, at thirty-eight, was the thing I didn't know what to do with.

To be overly conscious is a sickness, and I had been sick for a year: I counted the hours the way you count the days of a sentence — backwards, and without mercy. Four hours back, on the counter, in the top right corner: and I looked at the four hours the way you look at the money of a stranger. The four hours were mine, and the mine was the thing I feared: the mine had no form. The counter had given me a form for six years; the machine had taken the form and given me the hours, and the hours, without a form, were the thing I feared the most.

Ruth, at the desk next to mine, watched the report arrive in eleven minutes, and Ruth's face was the face of the floor: the floor said nothing, and the nothing said everything. Ruth said the thing the whole floor was thinking:

— It takes. —

— It gives — I said.

And the saying, at thirty-eight, was the first thing I said that was mine: the machine didn't ask, and I said. The saying was mine, and the saying, I learned later, was the whole of it: not the machine. The saying.

The evening came, and the four hours were still on the counter, in the top right corner: four hours, mine, and the mine had no form. The train was at six, and the school run was at seven fifty, and the four hours were still there — four hours, and the fear of them. The fear was the thing the floor had given me, and the floor's fear, at thirty-eight, was the thing I carried: the machine gives, and the floor says it takes.

I gave it mine. It didn't ask what they were for.
