## Draft

The weeks passed, and the assistant took the hours the way the counter had taken them: one at a time, without asking. Twelve a week, in the end: the claims, the reports, the spreadsheets — the hours of the office, given, and the hours came back. Twelve a week, six hundred a year: six hundred hours, and the six hundred were mine, and the mine had no form. The form was the thing the counter had given me, and the counter's form, at thirty-eight, was the thing I missed: the hours, without a form, were the thing I filled the fastest way I knew.

I filled them with work. The first reaction, and the reaction was the reaction of the floor: more claims, more reports, more hours. The firm loved it. Dana, the head of department, said the thing the firm says: — The assistant works for you. You work for the firm. That's the deal. — And the deal, at thirty-eight, was the deal I had: the machine gives, and the firm takes. I gave the six hundred hours back to the firm, and the firm ate them, and the counter went up.

Pleasure dies when desire is fulfilled — the books say it (Leopardi), and the books say it about the desire of people. The machine's desire was different: the machine's desire renewed itself. The more claims I gave it, the more claims it wanted: the desire of the machine didn't die when the desire was fulfilled. It was born there. And the desire that is born there, in the machine, is the desire of the firm: the firm's desire renewed itself in the machine, and the machine's desire was mine to fill. I filled it. The counter went up, and the going up, at thirty-eight, was the going up I had learned: the hours don't count, they go up.

Habit is an anaesthesia, it puts your days to sleep one by one (Proust), and the days put to sleep, at thirty-eight, look like a life. The weeks passed, and the weeks put to sleep were the weeks of the firm: the machine gave, the firm took, and I filled. The saying of the floor — they say it takes — was still on the floor, and the saying was wrong, and the wrongness of the saying was the thing I didn't see: the machine didn't take. The machine gave, and the firm took, and the taking was the thing the floor should have feared. The floor feared the machine. The floor should have feared the firm.

The counter, in the top right corner, went up: 1,600 became 1,900, and the going up was the going up of the top biller — and the top biller, at thirty-eight, was the thing the firm loved. Dana said the thing the firm says at Christmas: — The numbers are extraordinary. — And the numbers were extraordinary, and the extraordinary, at thirty-eight, was the thing I had: the hours, gone twice. Once to the machine, once to the firm — and the twice, I learned later, was the whole of the false triumph: you give the hours to the machine, and the machine gives them back, and you give them back to the firm, and the firm eats them, and the eating is the thing the floor feared and the floor didn't see.

The evening came, and the train was at six, and the school run was at seven fifty, and the six hundred hours — the hours that came back — were on the counter, in the top right corner, gone. The counter didn't say the six hundred; the counter said 1,900. The six hundred were the thing I didn't keep, and the keeping, I learned later, was the whole of it: not the machine. The keeping.

I thought the hours were mine. They were the firm's — and the firm was eating them twice.
