## Draft

> "O nature, o nature, / why do you not deliver / what you promise then? why do you deceive / your children so?"
>
> — Giacomo Leopardi, *A Silvia*

The play was in March, and March, at Meridian, is the month of the quarterly report: the firm asks for the report, and the asking is the asking of the firm. The play was at two in the afternoon, on a Tuesday, in the school hall, and Polly was the second shepherd — the second shepherd, and the second shepherd, at seven, is the thing you keep. I had put the play in the calendar in September, with the care of someone who knows the calendar is a sieve, and the calendar kept the play, and the counter ate it.

Miss Rowe was the teacher who directs, and Miss Rowe had been directing the Nativity for eleven years, and the eleven years of Miss Rowe were the years of the shepherd's crook: the crook, in the hall, is the thing that doesn't change. Miss Rowe said the thing the teachers say: — The second shepherd doesn't have lines. The second shepherd has the crook. — And the crook, at seven, was the thing you keep: you keep it, and the keeping, at seven, is the whole of the play.

The report was due on the Tuesday, at five, and the report was the thing the firm asked for: the quarterly report, 2,200 hours, the objective. The firm's promise was the promise of the firm: the report, the objective, the numbers. And the promise of the firm, at thirty-eight, was the promise that doesn't deliver: O nature, why do you not deliver what you promise then? — the books say it (Leopardi), and the books say it about nature. The firm was my nature, and the firm's nature didn't deliver: the firm promised the numbers and gave me the hours, and the hours were the thing I didn't have on the Tuesday, at two.

Mark called on the Monday, from Leeds, and Mark's voice was the voice of the man who asks: — Are you going to the play? — And the going, at thirty-eight, was the going that wasn't mine: the going was the thing the counter ate. Mark was the ex-husband, and the ex-husband, at thirty-eight, was the man who took the weekends — the weekends, in the marriage, were the thing Mark took, and the taking of the weekends was the thing the divorce was: the divorce was the thing we made of the weekends. Mark took the weekends, and I took the counter, and the two takings were the same taking, said in two languages.

— I'm going to try — I said.

And the trying, at thirty-eight, was the trying that wasn't true: the trying was the thing I said instead of the no. The no was the thing I owed Mark, and the no, at thirty-eight, was the thing I didn't say. The trying was the thing I said.

The hall was at the school, at two in the afternoon, and the hall was full — and the fullness was the thing I crossed, in the stream, the way you cross a river: without stopping. The hall was full of the coats and the prams, the smell of the coffee and the radiators, the mothers who don't count and the fathers who don't count, the grandparents with the phones up, the phones that record, and the phones that record were the things that don't keep: they record. And the recording was the thing the hall did instead of the keeping: the hall recorded, and the recording, at two in the afternoon, was the thing the counter did — the hall was a counter, and the counter was a hall, and the hall recorded the shepherds, and the shepherds were on the stage, and the second shepherd was on the stage, and the second shepherd was mine, and I wasn't there. I was at the office, and the office was the thing I had instead: the office, the counter, the report — and the report was due at five, and the five was the thing that ate the two: the two, in the afternoon, was the thing the counter ate, and the counter, in March, didn't know about the play. The counter counted.

Edward called at six, from Yorkshire, and Edward's voice was the voice of a father who asks:

— Were you at the play? —

— I was at the office — I said.

— The play, next year — said Edward, and the saying was the saying of a father who doesn't say the thing he wants to say: the saying was the saying of the father who knows. The heavy things are not said: they are kept, and the keeping of the heavy things, at thirty-eight, was the thing Edward did, and the thing I did, and the thing the floor did. We all kept them. The keeping was the thing we had instead of the saying.

Polly, at bedtime, said the thing the children say: — You weren't there. —

And the saying, at seven, was the saying of the child who doesn't keep: the child says. The children say the things the adults keep — and the saying, at seven, was the thing I kept: I kept it the way you keep the things that hurt, and the hurting, at thirty-eight, was the thing the counter ate. The counter, in March, didn't know about the play: the counter counted. And the counting, I learned later, was the thing the machine came to stop: not the machine. The counting.

The evening came, and the counter was in the top right corner: 2,200, and the 2,200 were the thing I had instead of the play. The play was in the calendar, and the calendar kept it, and the counter ate it. The counter, in March, didn't know about the play: the counter counted.
