## Draft

> "O nature, o nature, / why do you not deliver / what you promise then? why do you deceive / your children so?"
>
> — Giacomo Leopardi, *A Silvia*

The play was in March, and March, at Meridian, is the month of the quarterly report: the firm asks for the report, and the asking is the asking of the firm. The play was at two in the afternoon, on a Tuesday, in the school hall, and Polly was the second shepherd — the second shepherd, and the second shepherd, at seven, is the thing you keep. I had put the play in the calendar in September, with the care of someone who knows the calendar is a sieve, and the calendar kept the play, and the counter ate it.

The report was due on the Tuesday, at five, and the report was the thing the firm asked for: the quarterly report, 2,200 hours, the objective. The firm's promise was the promise of the firm: the report, the objective, the numbers. And the promise of the firm, at thirty-eight, was the promise that doesn't deliver: O nature, why do you not deliver what you promise then? — the books say it (Leopardi), and the books say it about nature. The firm was my nature, and the firm's nature didn't deliver: the firm promised the numbers and gave me the hours, and the hours were the thing I didn't have on the Tuesday, at two.

The hall was at the school, and the school hall, at two in the afternoon, was the hall of the mothers who don't count: the mothers were there, and the mothers didn't count. The mothers didn't have a counter; the mothers had the hall. I wasn't there. I was at the office, with the report, and the report was due at five, and the hours were the hours of the firm — and the firm's hours, at thirty-eight, were the hours that eat the play. The counter, in March, didn't know about the play: the counter counted. And the counting, at thirty-eight, was the thing I had instead of the hall: the counter, the report, the numbers.

Polly was the second shepherd, and the second shepherd doesn't have lines: the second shepherd has the crook and the bathrobe. And the crook and the bathrobe, at seven, are the things you keep: you keep them the way you keep the things that don't count. I didn't keep them. I was at the office, and the office was the thing I had instead: the office, the counter, the hours.

Edward called at six, from Yorkshire, and Edward's voice was the voice of a father who asks:

— Were you at the play? —

— I was at the office — I said.

— The play, next year — said Edward, and the saying was the saying of a father who doesn't say the thing he wants to say: the saying was the saying of the father who knows. Le cose pesanti — the heavy things — are not said: they are kept, and the keeping of the heavy things, at thirty-eight, was the thing Edward did, and the thing I did, and the thing the floor did. We all kept them. The keeping was the thing we had instead of the saying.

The evening came, and the counter was in the top right corner: 2,200, and the 2,200 were the thing I had instead of the play. The play was in the calendar, and the calendar kept it, and the counter ate it. The counter, in March, didn't know about the play: the counter counted. And the counting, I learned later, was the thing the machine came to stop: not the machine. The counting.
