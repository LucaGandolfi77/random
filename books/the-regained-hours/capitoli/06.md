## Draft

April came, and the train to Yorkshire was the East Coast line: King's Cross to York, two hours and twenty, and the train, in the morning, was the train of the daughter who counts. The train was on time, and on time, in England, is the thing you don't choose: you inherit it. The counter was on the phone, in the top right corner, and the counter, on the train, was the thing I looked at: the hours, and the hours' hours. The garden was at the end of the line, and the garden was the thing Edward kept.

Edward was at the station, with the car, and Edward's face was the face of a father who keeps: the keeping of Edward, at seventy-two, was the thing he had instead of the saying. The garden was behind the house, and the garden was the garden of the things that are planted: the roses, the hedge, the tomatoes. The hedge was Edward's hedge, and the hedge was the thing Edward kept: fifty years, and the hedge didn't count. Nobody counted the hedge. The hedge was the thing you keep: you keep it, and the keeping, at seventy-two, is the whole of the garden.

The only thing that doesn't frighten is the work of the hands — the books say it (Tolstoy), and the books say it about a man in a field. The garden was Edward's field, and the work of the hands, in the garden, was the work that doesn't frighten: the roses, the weeds, the tomatoes. Edward worked the garden with his hands, and his hands, at seventy-two, were the hands of the garden: they kept. The keeping of the hands, at seventy-two, was the thing the counter didn't know: the counter counted, and the hands kept. The keeping was the thing the machine couldn't do — and the couldn't, I learned later, was the whole of the gift: the machine gives hours; the hands keep them.

The hedge was the hedge of the books: a boy, once, climbed a hill and looked beyond the hedge — the infinite spaces, the silences (Leopardi, *L'infinito*). The hedge didn't frighten him: it measured. The hedge was the measure that made the infinite possible — and the infinite, at thirty-eight, was the thing I had: six hundred hours a year, returned, and the six hundred were the infinite. And the infinite, without a hedge, frightened me. The hours were my hedge: the counter was the hedge that made the hours possible, and the hours, without the counter, were the infinite. And the infinite, at thirty-eight, was the thing I filled with work. The hedge was the thing I was missing: not the infinite. The hedge.

The Sunday calls were Edward's, and the Sunday calls, in April, were the calls of the father who keeps. Edward talked about the garden — the roses, the hedge, the tomatoes — and the garden, on the phone, was the thing that didn't count: nobody counted the garden. The machine was in the garden too — Edward had a phone, and the phone had a screen, and the screen had a counter: the step counter, the sleep counter, the hours. Edward's counter counted the steps, and the steps, at seventy-two, were the steps of the garden: they kept. The counting of the steps was the thing Edward didn't do: the counting did him. The machine counts, and the counting doesn't ask: the machine doesn't ask you what the steps are for. The steps are yours. And the yours, at seventy-two, was the thing Edward had: the garden, and the garden didn't count.

The evening came, and the train back was at seven, and the train back was the train of the daughter who counts. The garden was the thing that didn't count. And the things that don't count, at thirty-eight, were the things I was missing.
