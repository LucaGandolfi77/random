## Draft

> "In place of death there was light."
>
> — Leo Tolstoy, *The Death of Ivan Ilyich*

The Sunday call was at five, and the Sunday call, at five, was the call of the daughter who counts. Edward's voice was on the phone, and Edward's voice was not Edward's voice: the voice was the voice of a man who had something to say and couldn't say it — the voice was slurred, and the slurring, at seventy-two, was the thing the phone said. The phone said it, and I heard it, and the hearing, at thirty-eight, was the hearing of the daughter who counts: the counting of the voice. The voice didn't count. The voice slurred.

— Edward — I said. — Edward. —

The voice said the thing the voice could say: — The garden. I was in the garden. — And the garden, at seventy-two, was the thing Edward kept: the garden was where he was, and the garden was where the stroke was. The neighbour found him — the neighbour, at seventy-two, is the neighbour who keeps: she kept him, and the keeping of the neighbour, at thirty-eight, was the thing I owed. The ambulance came at five forty, and the ambulance, at seventy-two, was the ambulance that doesn't wait: the stroke doesn't wait.

In place of death there was light — the books say it (Tolstoy), and the books say it about the man who dies. Edward didn't die. Edward was taken to the hospital, and the hospital was in York, and the hospital, at seventy-two, was the hospital of the light: the light of the ward, the light of the corridor, the light that doesn't count. The light, at seventy-two, was the thing Edward had instead of the garden: the garden was the thing he kept, and the light was the thing that kept him. The keeping of the light, at thirty-eight, was the thing I learned on the phone: the light doesn't count, and the light is the thing you keep.

The train was King's Cross, and the train, on Sunday, was the train of the daughter who counts: the train, on Sunday, didn't run. The East Coast line doesn't run on Sundays before two, and the line, at thirty-eight, was the line I knew: the line I had counted, for six years, and the counting of the line was the thing I had instead of the knowing. The train was at two, and the two, on Sunday, was the two that didn't wait: the stroke didn't wait. The stroke was at five forty, and the five forty, on Sunday, was the five forty of the thing you don't choose: you inherit it.

The weekend was not mine. The weekend was the thing the firm gave me, and the firm's weekend, at thirty-eight, was the weekend of the top biller: the top biller's weekend, on Sunday, was the weekend at the office — the report, the counter, the numbers. And the weekend at the office, on Sunday, was the weekend that ate the stroke: the stroke was at five forty, and the office was the thing I had instead of the garden. The counter, in the top right corner, said the hours, and the hours were the hours that were not there: the hours of the garden, the hours of the call, the hours of the light. The hours were not there. The counter said 2,400, and the 2,400 were the thing I had instead: the hours, and the hours' cost.

The evening came, and the train was at six on Monday, and the train, on Monday, was the train of the daughter who counts. The stroke was on Sunday, and the Sunday didn't wait for the train. The Monday train was the train I took, and the taking, at thirty-eight, was the taking of the daughter who counts: the train, the hours, the garden. The things that don't count were the things I was missing, and the missing, at thirty-eight, was the thing the counter couldn't say: the counter counted. And the counting, I learned later, was the thing the machine came to stop.
