The train arrived at two, and the two, on Sunday, was the two of the train that didn't wait: the stroke didn't wait. The hospital was in York, and the hospital, at two, was the hospital of the light: the light of the ward, the light of the corridor, the light that doesn't count. The hand didn't count. The hand kept.

The days of the ward were the days of June, and the days of June, at thirty-eight, were the days of the daughter who counts: the days were crossed, and the crossing was the thing I did instead of the living. The keeping of the ward, at seventy-two, was the thing the counter didn't know: the counter counted, and the ward kept.

But the question didn't count. The question was the thing Priya had instead of the chart: the chart counted, and the question kept. And the keeping of the question, at thirty-eight, was the thing the machine couldn't do: the machine gives hours; the question keeps them.

— How is he today? — said Priya, every morning, at seven.

— He kept the cup — I said, one morning — the cup, with the left hand. —
