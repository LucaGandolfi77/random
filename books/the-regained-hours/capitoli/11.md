## Draft

The Monday after was the Monday of the conversation, and the conversation was with Dana, at ten, in the meeting room with the long table. The meeting room was the room of the firm, and the firm's room, at thirty-eight, was the room of the asking: the objective, the numbers, the counter. Dana said the thing the firm says:

— The numbers are extraordinary. We need more. —

And the more, at thirty-eight, was the more of the firm: the more was the thing that ate the play, the garden, the Sundays. I said the thing I hadn't said in a year — the truth, and the truth, at thirty-eight, was the thing the books say:

— The machine gave me six hundred hours — I said. — And I gave them back to you. And you ate them. And the play was in March, and the play was my daughter's, and I wasn't there. And my father had a stroke on a Sunday, and I wasn't there either. —

The truth, at thirty-eight, was the thing I had instead of the lie: the lie — *I'm fine* — was the thing I had said every morning for a year, and the saying of the lie was the thing the floor had given me. The truth was the thing I gave myself, and the giving of the truth, at thirty-eight, was the thing the books say: above all, don't lie to yourself — and the not lying, at thirty-eight, was the thing I did in the meeting room, at ten, with the counter on the phone. The truth didn't count. The truth kept.

Dana didn't say anything, and the nothing, at thirty-eight, was the nothing of the firm: the firm didn't say the thing the firm says, and the not saying was the thing I learned. The firm's nothing, at thirty-eight, was the nothing of the question: the question — what are the hours for? — was the thing the firm didn't ask, and the not asking was the thing the firm gave me. The firm gave me the nothing, and the nothing was mine.

— The hours are mine — I said. — The machine gave them back. The claims are yours; the hours are mine. —

And the saying, at thirty-eight, was the saying of the daughter who keeps: the keeping of the saying, at thirty-eight, was the thing I did instead of the lie. The hours were mine, and the mine was the thing the counter couldn't say: the counter counted. The counter said 2,400, and the 2,400 were the thing the firm loved, and the firm's love was the thing that ate the hours twice. The hours, at thirty-eight, were the thing I took back: the taking, at thirty-eight, was the taking of the daughter who keeps.

— The deal — said Dana, and the deal was the thing the firm had: the machine gives, the firm takes. — The deal is the deal. —

— The deal was the deal — I said. — The machine changed the deal. It gave the hours back. You can have the claims. The hours are for the school run, and the Sundays, and the garden. —

And the saying, at thirty-eight, was the saying of the woman who keeps: the keeping of the saying, at thirty-eight, was the thing the machine gave — the machine gave the hours, and the hours were the things I said: the school run, the Sundays, the garden. The things that don't count were the things I said to Dana, and the saying of the things that don't count, at thirty-eight, was the thing that didn't count: it kept.

Ruth, at the desk next to mine, said the thing the floor was saying:

— You said no. —

— I said the truth — I said.

And the saying, at thirty-eight, was the saying of the fear answered: Ruth's fear was the fear of the floor — they say it takes — and the saying of the fear, at thirty-eight, was the saying that wasn't true. The machine doesn't take: the machine gives, and the giving is the thing the floor should have said. Ruth said the thing the floor should have said, and the saying was the saying of the fear answered:

— It gives — said Ruth. — I see it now. —

It was the thing the floor learned in a year, and the learning, at thirty-eight, was the learning of the daughter who keeps. The machine gives, and the hours are yours, and the yours is the thing you keep: the keeping was the thing the floor hadn't done, and the doing of the keeping, at thirty-eight, was the thing I did instead of the lie.

Sam, two desks down, asked the question — the question of the coffee, the question of the young ones:

— What did you do with them? —

— I kept them — I said. — You don't count them. You keep them. —

And the teaching, at thirty-eight, was the teaching I owed Sam: the teaching of the keeping, said at the coffee, with the cup in the hand — the cup was the thing Sam had, and the cup, at twenty-four, was the thing that doesn't count: it keeps. The coffee was the coffee that nobody chooses, and the coffee that nobody chooses, at twenty-four, was the coffee that keeps: the keeping of the coffee, at twenty-four, was the thing Sam learned. The teaching was the thing I did instead of the counting: and the counting, at thirty-eight, was the thing I stopped.

The first kept hours were the hours of the school run. The school run was at seven fifty, and the school run, at thirty-eight, was the thing you keep: you don't count it. And the not counting, at thirty-eight, was the thing I learned: the counter didn't say the school run. The school run was mine.
