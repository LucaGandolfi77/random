## Draft

> "How could I not have seen that lofty sky before?"
>
> — Leo Tolstoy, *War and Peace*

The play was in October, and the play was the play of the second shepherd: Polly was the second shepherd again, and the second shepherd, at eight, is the thing you keep. The hall was at the school, at two in the afternoon, on a Tuesday, and the hall, at two in the afternoon, was the hall of the mothers who don't count: I was there. The counter was on the phone, in the top right corner, and the counter, in October, was the counter of the kept hours: the counter said 1,800, and the 1,800 were the thing I had instead of 2,400 — the six hundred hours, returned, and the six hundred were the thing I kept. The keeping, at thirty-nine, was the thing I did instead of the counting: the counter went down, and the going down was the going down of the woman who keeps.

Mark was there, and Mark was the man who took the weekends, and the weekends, at thirty-nine, were the weekends he gave back: Mark sat in the third row, and the third row was the row of the fathers who don't count, and the not counting, at thirty-nine, was the thing we shared — the sharing, at thirty-nine, was the thing the divorce hadn't taken. The divorce had taken the weekends; the play gave them back. The play was the thing we both attended, and the attending, at thirty-nine, was the thing you don't count: you keep it.

Polly was the second shepherd, and the second shepherd has the crook and the bathrobe: and the crook and the bathrobe, at eight, were the things I kept. The play was the thing I attended, and the attending, at thirty-nine, was the attending of the mother who doesn't count: the hall was full of mothers who don't count, and the mothers, at two in the afternoon, were the mothers of the kept hours. The kept hours were the thing I had instead of the counter, and the having, at thirty-nine, was the having of the life: the life was the thing the machine had given me, and the giving, at thirty-nine, was the thing I kept.

The train to Yorkshire was the East Coast line, and the train, in November, was the train of the daughter who keeps: the train was on time, and the on time, in England, is the thing you inherit. Edward was at home now, and Edward's hand kept — the hand was the hand of the garden, and the keeping of the hand, at seventy-two, was the thing the doctors counted. The hand kept, and the keeping, at seventy-two, was the thing the garden knew: the garden was the thing Edward kept, and the keeping of the garden, at seventy-two, was the thing the stroke had touched. The touch didn't take the keeping: the touch took the hand, and the hand kept.

The garden was behind the house, and the garden, in November, was the garden of the things that are planted: the roses, the hedge, the tomatoes — the tomatoes were the thing the neighbour kept, and the keeping of the neighbour, at seventy-two, was the thing Edward owed. The garden was the thing you keep, and the keeping was the thing you give: the neighbour gave the tomatoes, and the giving kept. And the keeping of the giving, at thirty-nine, was the thing I learned in the garden: the things that are planted, you don't count. You plant them.

Hull was the thing I did the week after: the train, the city, the street. Joan Whitfield's house was the house of the flood, and the house, at sixty-eight, was the house of the sewing machine: the machine that made, and the machine that was lost. Joan had a new machine now — the insurance had paid, and the paying, at sixty-eight, was the thing the number did: the number was the thing I wrote in September. But the new machine was on the kitchen table, and the new machine, at sixty-eight, was the machine that didn't sew: it waited. Joan hadn't threaded it. The threading was the thing Joan didn't do: — It's not his mother's — said Joan. — The new one. It's a machine. The old one was his mother's. —

And the difference, at sixty-eight, was the difference of the things that are kept: the machines don't keep. The mothers keep. And the keeping of the mothers, at thirty-nine, was the thing my mother did: the bread, on Saturdays, the kitchen of the flour — and the kitchen, at thirty-nine, was the kitchen I kept: I baked the bread. The bread, on the Saturday, was the thing I baked, and the baking was the thing I learned in the kitchen of Margaret: the bread is planted. You don't count it. You plant it.

How could I not have seen that lofty sky before? — the books say it (Tolstoy), and the books say it about the man on the field. The sky, in the garden, was the sky of November: the sky was high, and the sky was the sky I hadn't seen — the sky was the thing the counter couldn't say: the counter counted, and the sky didn't count. The sky was the thing I saw for the first time, and the seeing, at thirty-nine, was the seeing of the woman who keeps: the keeping of the seeing, at thirty-nine, was the thing I did instead of the counting. The machine gave the hours, and the hours were the sky: the hours were the thing I hadn't had yet — and the hadn't, at thirty-nine, was the thing the machine gave.

And the kept hours, in the stream, were the hours that kept — the play, the garden, the kitchen, the Sundays — and the hours that kept were the hours that flowed: the water flows in the pipes of the building, down, always down, into the cellars of the firm — and the cellars were the cellars where the bread was, and the bread was the thing I baked, and the baking was the thing I planted, and the planting was the thing the machine gave — the machine gave the hours, and the hours gave the kitchen, and the kitchen gave the bread, and the bread gave the smell, and the smell gave the mother — and the mother, in the stream, was the mother of the bread, and the mother was there: she was there, in the kitchen, in the smell, in the hour that kept — and the hour that kept, at thirty-nine, was the hour of the life: the life was the thing the machine gave, and the giving was the thing I kept, and the keeping was the whole of it.

The moral was forming, the way the things are planted: the machine gave me back my life — not the life I had lost: the life I hadn't had yet. The life I hadn't had yet was the life of the kept hours: the play, the garden, the Sundays, the bread. The life was the thing the machine gave, and the giving, at thirty-nine, was the thing the industry didn't say: the industry said the machine takes. The machine gives — and the giving, at thirty-nine, was the thing I kept.

The evening came, and the train back was at seven, and the train back was the train of the daughter who keeps. The things you keep, you don't count: you keep them. And the keeping, at thirty-nine, was the whole of it.
